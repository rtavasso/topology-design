## 1. Parameterization → dynamics → implicit bias (Phase 0)

These answer most of the v0 Study 1 in closed form. The v0 review did not cite any of them.

- **Arora, Cohen & Hazan (ICML 2018), *On the Optimization of Deep Networks: Implicit Acceleration by Overparameterization*.** Balanced deep linear chains follow a closed-form preconditioned flow on the end-to-end matrix. **Use:** Prop. 3 and Prop. 4.
- **Du, Hu & Lee (NeurIPS 2018), *Algorithmic Regularization in Learning Deep Homogeneous Models*.** Layer balancedness is conserved under gradient flow.
- **Arora, Cohen, Hu & Luo (NeurIPS 2019), *Implicit Regularization in Deep Matrix Factorization*.** Depth strengthens low-rank bias. **Use:** expected implicit-bias direction for chains.
- **Hardt & Ma (ICLR 2017), *Identity Matters in Deep Learning*; Bartlett, Helmbold & Long (ICML 2018), identity-initialized deep linear residual networks.** **Use:** the identity-initialized regime of our residual family; explains why Saxe-style plateaus are absent at identity init.
- **Kawaguchi (NeurIPS 2016); Laurent & von Brecht (ICML 2018).** Deep linear networks have no bad local minima. **Use:** Prop. 6 endpoint equivalence.
- **Soudry, Hoffer, Nacson, Gunasekar & Srebro (JMLR 2018); Gunasekar, Lee, Soudry & Srebro (NeurIPS 2018, linear convolutional networks); Ji & Telgarsky (ICLR 2019).** Directional implicit bias under separable cross-entropy, and how architecture changes it. **Use:** Prop. 6 separable case (open).
- **Woodworth et al. (COLT 2020), *Kernel and Rich Regimes in Overparametrized Models*.** Parameterization plus init scale interpolate between kernel and rich implicit bias. **Use:** the richness axis.
- [**Kunin et al. (NeurIPS 2024), *Get Rich Quick*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06158)† Unbalanced initializations promote rapid feature learning; exact solutions. **Use:** Phase 0 imbalanced-init item.
- [**Dominé et al. (ICLR 2025), *From Lazy to Rich: Exact Learning Dynamics in Deep Linear Networks*.**](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2025/domine2025iclr-lazy)† Lazy-to-rich transitions controlled by λ-balancedness.
- [**Marcotte, Gribonval & Peyré (NeurIPS 2023), *Abide by the Law and Follow the Flow*.**](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/c7bee9b76be21146fd592fc2b46614d5-Abstract.html)† An algorithm for finding all conservation laws of a parameterization. **Use:** Prop. 7, mixed graphs.
- **Kunin et al. (ICLR 2021), *Neural Mechanics*; Zhao et al. (ICLR 2023), *Symmetries, Flat Minima, and the Conserved Quantities of Gradient Flow*.** Symmetry → conserved quantity → dynamics.
- [**Lippl, Abbott & Chung (2022), *The Implicit Bias of Gradient Descent on Generalized Gated Linear Networks*.**](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/358423122_The_Implicit_Bias_of_Gradient_Descent_on_Generalized_Gated_Linear_Networks)† Architecture-dependent implicit bias with fixed gates.
- **Saxe, McClelland & Ganguli (ICLR 2014)** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.6120); **Xu & Ziyin (ICLR 2025)**; **Shi, Shea-Brown & Buice (NeurIPS 2022)**. Retained from v0. Note that Saxe 2014's staged mode learning depends on *small* initialization.

## Novelty boundary (after the literature review)

**Known (do not claim; cite as prior work or corroboration):**
- deep linear flows and implicit bias for chains, diagonal, convolutional and gated-linear parameterizations;
- topology cannot change local conservation laws (Marcotte 2025);
- parallel-branch collapse (Ziyin 2024; multi-branch convexity);
- identity-init orientation lock (Bartlett 2018);
- weight-decay bias of residual chains (Boix-Adsera 2025);
- lazy-regime kernel effects of connectivity;
- expressive-efficiency gaps from added connections (Cohen, Tamari & Shashua);
- pathway races in gated networks;
- shortcut/core competition;
- gradient coherence;
- the sample-complexity separation between 2- and 3-layer networks.

**Candidate contributions (open as far as the review searched):**
1. gradient-flow implicit bias for **general linear residual DAGs**, including which path-liftings commute (Phase 0);
2. **RLCT of residual and parallel parameterizations** of the same function class (Phase 0);
3. **richness-gated topology effects**, shown as a phase boundary with a dose–response over one-edge mutants;
4. **constituent→composite lag dynamics** across routing graphs (Phase 2);
5. **matched-parameter sequential vs parallel transformer blocks** compared on internal structure and induction-head timing (Phase 4);
6. **topology-dependent erasure cost**, separating the identity path from shortcuts (Phase 4).

## Routed, layer-shared, adaptively halting transformers (added 6 Nov 2026; the architectural family the thesis belongs to)

The researcher asked whether the proposal is "a type of MoE with dynamic halting, with experts reused per layer that are not just MLPs." At the block-diagram level it is, and this family was absent from the novelty boundary until now. Read in full before any architectural priority is claimed:

- [**Dehghani et al. (ICLR 2019), *Universal Transformers*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03819) One layer applied recurrently with per-position adaptive computation time (Graves 2016, ACT, [arXiv 1603.08983](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.08983)); [PonderNet (Banino et al. 2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.05407) is the probabilistic halting form.
- [**Tan, Shen, Chen, Courville & Gan (EMNLP 2023), *Sparse Universal Transformer*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07096) A UT whose shared layer is a sparse mixture of experts for **both attention and feed-forward**, with stick-breaking ACT halting; reports compositional-generalization gains (CFQ) and efficiency. **This is the closest existing architecture to the thesis's carrier at scale.**
- [**Csordás, Irie, Schmidhuber, Potts & Manning (NeurIPS 2024), *MoEUT: Mixture-of-Experts Universal Transformers*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16039) Layer-shared σ-MoE feed-forward and SwitchHead attention experts with layer grouping; competitive with dense transformers on language modelling at matched parameters and compute — not better in resolution, which is the fact the thesis has to explain (below).
- [**Bae et al. (2025), *Mixture-of-Recursions*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10524) Recursive (weight-shared) transformers with lightweight routers assigning a per-token recursion depth; the per-token depth-elasticity half of the thesis, with end-to-end-trained routers.
- [**Raposo et al. (2024), *Mixture-of-Depths*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02258) Per-token routing around blocks under a capacity budget: elasticity without a shared library.
- [**Rosenbaum, Klinger & Riemer (ICLR 2018), *Routing Networks***](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.01239) and [**Kirsch, Kunze & Barber (NeurIPS 2018), *Modular Networks*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.05249) Per-example composition of function blocks by a learned router, trained by RL (Rosenbaum; collapse problems) or by generalized EM over module assignments (Kirsch; EM chosen to avoid collapse). **Kirsch et al. is a direct precedent for rung 2's finding that posterior / hard-EM targets train a router where reward-only signals collapse (F35, F40); our results refine it (exact vs sampled posteriors, search, commitment), they do not originate it.**
- Neural Module Networks (Andreas et al. 2016) and the Neural Programmer-Interpreter (Reed & de Freitas 2016) for composition of named modules by a controller.

**Where the boundary sits.** Not in the architecture: a shared library of attention-and-MLP experts, routed per token per step with adaptive halting, exists (SUT, MoEUT, MoR). The thesis's claims are about (i) what the router *is* — amortized posterior inference over programs under a data prior (a PFN), routing on an explicit **computational state** with a writable register and setup steps rather than on the residual stream for load; (ii) how it is **trained** — by posterior or search targets (rungs 2–3: exactness against self-training, F45), not by end-to-end soft gating with load-balancing losses, which in our vocabulary is the own-policy family; (iii) what such a router **does** — programs as closures of per-state decisions (F30), the signal choosing among tied programs (F37), plasticity after commitment (F45–F49), generalization across worlds (rung 4), chains against bags (rung 5), library growth by abstraction (risk 9). None of these is posed or measured in the papers above, which report perplexity or benchmark accuracy. **The fact to explain:** MoEUT routes depth with shared experts and is parameter-matched-competitive, not better, than dense; the ledger's line "MoE routes width and has not produced resolution beyond parameter count" (1 Nov) does not cover it. The thesis's answer has to be that gating-trained, residual-routed routers learn wide bags rather than serial chains (Entry 74), which makes rung 5 posable *on a MoEUT-style carrier*: the same experts, a posterior-or-search-trained router with state against a gating-trained one, on a task with a depth variable. If the training signal and the state do not buy depth extrapolation there, the thesis is wrong in the way that matters.

## The thesis against prior work: a pushback review (added 10 Nov 2026, after rung 4; history Entry 82)

The researcher asked for the thesis and the ideas of the 10 Nov discussion to be grounded in prior work and pushed against. Entries marked ‡ had their abstract or full page read on 10 Nov 2026; unmarked entries are cited from memory of well-known results and should be checked before being quoted. Each entry says what it does to the thesis.

### A. The depth claim ("a token needing k > L steps is unreachable for a dense model")

- [**Merrill & Sabharwal (ICLR 2024), *The Expressive Power of Transformers with Chain of Thought*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07923v4)‡ With a polynomial number of intermediate decoding steps a transformer recognizes exactly the polynomial-time problems; linear steps add all regular languages; logarithmic steps add little. **Against:** the depth bound holds for one forward pass only. A dense model with a scratchpad already has unbounded serial depth and pays in tokens. The thesis cannot claim reach; it has to claim cost (compute per solved problem) or learnability.
- [**Liu, Ash, Goel, Krishnamurthy & Zhang (ICLR 2023), *Transformers Learn Shortcuts to Automata*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10749v1)‡ A T-step automaton can be simulated in O(log T) layers always and O(1) often, and standard training finds such shortcuts; the paper also studies their brittleness. **Against:** "required depth = number of sequential steps" is false for dense transformers in distribution. **Matches** F53 (the no-search router past lookahead-3). **For:** the brittleness of shortcuts is where an iterated primitive could win — off-support, which is where rung 4 found the prior failing.
- [**Ruoss et al. (NeurIPS 2024), *Amortized Planning with Large-Scale Transformers: A Case Study on Chess* (preprint title: *Grandmaster-Level Chess Without Search*).**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04494)‡ A 270M-parameter transformer trained by supervision on Stockfish annotations of 10M games reaches Lichess blitz Elo 2895 with no search; "perfect distillation is still beyond reach". **Against / matches:** search amortizes into a dense model very far given support (F53 at scale). Lehnert et al. (2024), *Beyond A\** (Searchformer), is the planning counterpart.
- [**Dziri et al. (NeurIPS 2023), *Faith and Fate: Limits of Transformers on Compositionality*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18654v3) Transformers solve multi-step compositional tasks by reducing them to linearized subgraph matching; accuracy decays with compositional depth. **For:** the motivation — the dense model's in-support competence is pattern matching, not composition.
- [**Wang, Yue, Su & Sun (NeurIPS 2024), *Grokked Transformers are Implicit Reasoners*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15071)‡ Implicit multi-hop reasoning is learned only through grokking; out of distribution transformers "fail to systematically generalize for composition but succeed for comparison"; the mechanistic account ties the failure to the configuration of the generalizing circuit, and the authors suggest "encouraging cross-layer knowledge sharing". **For:** the closest published statement of the thesis's reuse claim — composition fails when the second hop's knowledge lives in different layers from the first's.
- [**Zhou et al. (ICLR 2024), *What Algorithms can Transformers Learn? A Study in Length Generalization*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16028v1)‡ The RASP-Generalization conjecture: a transformer tends to length-generalize on a task if a short RASP program solves it at all lengths. **Use:** a principled predictor for rung 4's Q2 (rules against tables) that we did not use; it says *which* tasks a dense model will extrapolate on, so a size-extrapolation test should be chosen on both sides of it.

### B. Reuse across depth (the "library" half)

- [**Jolicoeur-Martineau (2025), *Less is More: Recursive Reasoning with Tiny Networks* (TRM)**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.04871)‡, after the Hierarchical Reasoning Model (Wang et al. 2025). One 2-layer network (5–19M parameters) recursed on a latent state with deep supervision and a learned halt: ARC-AGI-1 44.6 %, ARC-AGI-2 7.8 %, Sudoku-Extreme 87.4 %, Maze-Hard 85.3 % as reported, above HRM (27M) and most LLMs listed. **For:** the strongest current evidence that reuse in depth buys reasoning at tiny parameter counts. **Against:** it does so with **one block and no router** (m = 1), trained with a signal at every recursion. What a library of m > 1 primitives and a router add over a loop is exactly what the thesis has not shown; the ledger's F32 (a loop never learned the second circuit) is our only evidence and it is one toy.
- [**Sun, Pickett, Nain & Jones (2024), *Transformer Layers as Painters*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.09298v4)‡ In trained LLMs the middle layers share a representation space and tolerate skipping, reordering and parallel execution with graceful loss — but replacing them by copies of one layer's weights is the most damaging intervention, worse than skipping; looping a parallelized group helps; math and reasoning tasks are the most order-sensitive. **Against:** a trained dense model's layers are not repetitions of one primitive. **Open (risk 1):** whether they are a small library of m, as a curve in m — nobody in this search has measured it; ALBERT (Lan et al. 2020) is the trained-from-scratch version of m = 1 and loses some accuracy.
- MoEUT‡ ([abstract re-read](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16039v2)): "slightly outperforms standard Transformers on language modeling tasks such as BLiMP and PIQA, while using significantly less compute and memory". The fact to explain stands: shared experts with gating are competitive, not qualitatively better.

### C. Learning the router (the "routing" half)

- [**Rosenbaum, Cases, Riemer & Klinger (2019), *Routing Networks and the Challenges of Modular and Compositional Computation*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.12774)‡ Names the problems of learning modules and their composition together: the chicken-and-egg instability at the start, module collapse, overfitting through exponentially many paths, the transfer–interference trade-off coupled to exploration, non-stationarity (the "actions" change as modules train); routing "often needs external structure" such as task labels or curricula. **Against / matches:** our F22–F23, F32 and F40 are instances of a documented family, not discoveries. The idea of labelling routes by search under the current primitives and alternating (10 Nov discussion) is their setting; Kirsch et al.'s EM is the best-behaved member.
- [**Mittal, Bengio & Lajoie (NeurIPS 2022), *Is a Modular Architecture Enough?***](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02713v1)‡ On data with known modular structure, end-to-end learned modular systems are "sub-optimal … as opposed to their claimed potential"; the gap is in optimization (specialization), not capacity. **Against:** having the right architecture does not deliver the right routing.
- [**Roller, Sukhbaatar, Szlam & Weston (NeurIPS 2021), *Hash Layers For Large Sparse Models*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04426v2)‡ Routing by a fixed hash of the token "either outperforms or is competitive with learning-to-route mixture-of-expert methods" (Switch, BASE). **Against:** at scale, gating-trained routers have learned little that a random fixed assignment does not supply. The thesis's reply has to be that the *training signal* is the difference (posterior or search targets on an explicit state), which rungs 2–3 support with handed-over primitives only.

### D. Support, and the router as a PFN

- [**Müller et al. (ICLR 2022), *Transformers Can Do Bayesian Inference*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10510v6) A PFN approximates the posterior predictive under the prior it is trained on. Nothing is promised off the prior's support.
- [**Raventós, Paul, Chen & Ganguli (NeurIPS 2023), *Pretraining task diversity and the emergence of non-Bayesian in-context learning for regression*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15063)‡ Below a task-diversity threshold (between 2^14 and 2^15 tasks in their base setting) the transformer "cannot solve unseen regression tasks" and "behaves like a Bayesian estimator with the non-diverse pretraining task distribution as the prior"; above it, it departs from that estimator and matches the solution for the full task distribution. **This is rung 4's P2 result, predicted:** eight training worlds is far below any such threshold, and a router that acts like a posterior over the eight worlds it saw is what the literature says to expect. The researcher's 10 Nov critique (insufficient support confounds the reading) is this paper's finding. **Consequence:** "does the router recombine" is not a yes/no question; it is a threshold in the number and diversity of worlds, and that threshold is the quantity to measure.
- [**Schug et al. (ICLR 2024), *Discovering modular solutions that generalize compositionally*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15001v2)‡ In a teacher–student setting with a modular teacher and hypernetwork students, "identification up to linear transformation purely from demonstrations is possible" without seeing "an exponential number of module combinations", under conditions on the training distribution (as I recall the body: the support must cover each module and connect them; not re-read). **For:** recombination from sub-exponential coverage is possible in principle, with multiplicative (hypernetwork-like) conditioning on the task code. **Against rung 4's design:** the presence tokens entered additively as input tokens with eight patterns.

### E. Designed primitives, designed state, and labels from traces

- **RASP** (Weiss, Goldberg & Yahav, ICML 2021) and [**Tracr**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05062) (Lindner et al., NeurIPS 2023): transformer weights compiled from programs — designing rather than learning primitives is established, on a designed (superposition-free) state code.
- **Neural Programmer-Interpreters** (Reed & de Freitas, ICLR 2016) and [**Cai, Shin & Song (ICLR 2017), *Making Neural Programming Architectures Generalize via Recursion*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06611) A learned controller over designed operations, trained on **execution traces**; with recursion, generalization can be verified exhaustively. **Matches** the 10 Nov conclusion: designed primitives move the whole problem to the controller, and intermediate labels exist only where a reference algorithm's trace exists.
- [**Ellis et al. (PLDI 2021), *DreamCoder*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.08381) A designed base language, a library grown by abstraction over found programs, and a neural recognition model trained on solved tasks and on programs sampled from the prior ("dreams") to guide search. **Against novelty:** "an amortized posterior over programs under a prior, with a library that grows by abstraction" is DreamCoder, in symbolic program synthesis, in 2021. What the thesis adds can only be that the programs run inside a network's latent computation.
- [**Mentzer et al. (ICLR 2024), *Finite Scalar Quantization*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15505v2) The bounded, per-dimension-rounded latent whose implicit codebook is a grid of hypercubes — the "hypercube latent" of the 10 Nov discussion, as a quantizer.
- [**AlphaProof (Nature 2025), *Olympiad-level formal mathematical reasoning with reinforcement learning*.**](https://research.google/pubs/olympiad-level-formal-mathematical-reasoning-with-reinforcement-learning/) and the line behind it (GPT-f, HTPS, expert iteration, LeanDojo's premise retrieval): a learned policy and value over Lean's tactics with search, trained by reinforcement on found proofs. **Against:** under the "tactics are the designed primitives, the kernel is the executor" reading, the thesis's outer loop is this line of work, at a scale we cannot approach. Rung 4 was a toy of it.

### What survives, and what it changes

1. **Three cheaper alternatives stand between the thesis and its payoff.** Depth: a scratchpad (A). Reuse: one looped block (B). Routing: a fixed or random assignment (C). The thesis is the claim that a *library with a posterior- or search-trained router on an explicit state* beats all three — dense with chain of thought at matched compute, a single recursed block at matched parameters, and the same library with gating-trained or hashed routing. No paper found here runs that comparison; neither have we.
2. **The regime is off-support.** In support, dense models shortcut and amortize (Liu, Ruoss, F53). Any claimed advantage has to be shown where shortcuts break — deeper compositions than trained, new combinations — with coverage as a measured variable (Raventós), not a fixed split.
3. **The published hint in the thesis's favour** is specific: composition fails out of distribution when knowledge is not shared across layers (Wang et al.), and a recursed tiny block outperforms far larger models on puzzle reasoning (TRM). Both support reuse; neither supports a router.
4. **Not new:** the architecture (SUT, MoEUT, MoR); EM or search labels for routing (Kirsch); the pathologies of joint training (Rosenbaum); the support threshold (Raventós); amortized posteriors over programs with a growing library (DreamCoder); designed primitives with trace supervision (NPI); policy-plus-search over Lean (AlphaProof).
5. **Still open as far as this search went:** the fidelity of a trained deep model as a router over m primitives, as a curve in m (risk 1); a router trained by posterior or search targets over *learned* reused primitives; the three-way comparison of item 1 stratified by required computation off-support.

## Looped transformers, experts inside loops, and latent reasoning (added 11 Nov 2026; history Entry 83)

Read on 11 Nov 2026 during the discussion that moved the program from discrete primitives toward looped encoders. ‡ = abstract or page read that day (several pages were truncated by the reader; noted where it matters). One line each on what the work shows and what it does to our direction.

### What a looped block learns
- [**Yang, Lee, Nowak & Papailiopoulos (ICLR 2024), *Looped Transformers are Better at Learning Learning Algorithms*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.12424v3)‡ (read to §5.3) One GPT-2 layer looped ~20 times matches an 8–12-layer model on in-context regression with 1/12 of the parameters; the prompt is **re-injected every iteration** (Y_{t+1} = M(Y_t + P)) — without it the loop diverges past trained iterations, with it the loop settles to a fixed point; loss over a window of the last T iterations. Underperforms the dense model under input rescaling.
- [**Gatmiry, Saunshi, Reddi, Jegelka & Kumar (ICML 2024), *Can Looped Transformers Learn to Implement Multi-step Gradient Descent for In-context Learning?***](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2410.08292)‡ For linear looped attention on in-context regression the global minimizer does one step of preconditioned gradient descent per loop, with the preconditioner ≈ the inverse population covariance: context = the problem, state = the iterate, weights = the prior. The literal "gradient field" case.
- [**Fan, Du, Ramchandran & Lee (ICLR 2025), *Looped Transformers for Length Generalization*.**](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2409.15647)‡ (results tables not read) n-RASP-L: a fixed RASP-L step repeated T(n) times. Parity, copy, addition: each loop is an algorithmic step, not descent; step count supplied per example or chosen by confidence; **no intermediate supervision** — varying lengths supervise the same block at many depths; input injection; full-output prediction.
- [**Giannou et al. (ICML 2023), *Looped Transformers as Programmable Computers*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13196v1)‡ A fixed 13-layer looped block as an interpreter; the input is the punchcard (program and memory).
- [**Saunshi et al. (ICLR 2025), *Reasoning with Latent Thoughts*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17416v1)‡ (abstract) k layers looped L times ≈ kL layers on reasoning tasks; T loops simulate T chain-of-thought steps; a reasoning-against-memorization dichotomy.
- **Use:** a looped block is one rule for moving the state, driven by a re-injected input; a gradient field is the special case (estimation, refinement), a state-transition the other (algorithms, where stopping matters).

### Loops at scale: storage against manipulation
- [**Zhu et al. (2025), *Scaling Latent Reasoning via Looped Language Models* (Ouro).**](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25741v2)‡ (truncated) 1.4B and 2.6B looped LMs, 4 loop steps, 7.7T tokens; authors report matching 4B and 8B dense models on most benchmarks. Controlled experiments: looping does **not** raise storage (≈ 2 bits per parameter either way) and **does** raise knowledge manipulation (composition, multi-hop). Exit gates collapse without an entropy regularizer; stage II freezes the LM and trains the gate alone — "primitives first, control after", as our F22–F23 suggested.
- [**Chen, Vegesna, Dahal & Wilson (2026), *How Model Growth, Recursion, and Boundary Operators Influence Scaling Exponents*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.19107)‡ (truncated) Prelude–core–coda. On fresh data, weight sharing and a higher fixed loop count are not compute-optimal alone; growth schedules and a boundary operator (normalize, re-inject the prelude output) change the scaling exponent; looping regularizes when data is repeated.
- [**Aleksandrov et al. (2025), *AbbIE*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08567v1)‡ Head–body–tail, only the body looped; trained at 2 iterations, still improving at 8.
- [**Cai, Zhu, Dong, He & Arora (2026), *T²MLR*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15178)‡ (abstract) and [**Huang et al. (2026), *Latent Recurrent Transformer*.**](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.26797.md)‡ Recurrence across decoding steps (a mid-layer state carried to the next token) in autoregressive decoders; a localized middle block beats full-layer recurrence. The alternative to an encoder loop for where latent computation lives.
- [**Takashiro et al. (2026), *Fast-Slow Latent Recurrence for Train-Short, Test-Long Generalization*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.01577)‡ (truncated) Encoder → a set of latent tokens → a shared core applied T times per observation, the latent carried across the stream; weight-shared loop beats an unshared stack of twice the parameters out of distribution; probes find stack depth in the latent trajectory on Dyck. **Closest to the "looped encoder with registers" idea, and already a latent-path characterization against known structure.**

### Experts inside shared layers
- SUT and MoEUT (above). **Correction recorded:** SUT reports compositional-generalization gains (CFQ); on 11 Nov I told the researcher that none of the experts-in-loop papers tested reasoning, which is true of the three below and false of SUT.
- [**Nouriborji, Rohanian & Rohanian (2025), *Improving Recursive Transformers with Mixture of LoRAs* (ModernALBERT).**](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.12880v2)‡ A recursive **encoder** (shared layer groups) with token-routed low-rank experts on the shared feed-forward; attention untouched; 1 → 8 experts: 76.08 → 77.24 in ablation; 120M ≈ a 149M standard encoder on GLUE.
- [**Chen et al. (2026), *Mixture of Universal Experts*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04971v1)‡ (truncated) A pool of experts reachable from several depths, per-layer routers; letting every depth reach every shared expert was the worst ablation.
- [**Jaggi (2026), *Tying the Loop: Tied Expert Layers in Mixture-of-Experts Language Models*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16825v1)‡ (truncated) Expert feed-forward weights tied across groups of 4 layers, attention and routers per layer, first and last two layers untied; at 7B matches the untied model with half the parameters; untying routers changes which experts fire and barely changes the loss; attention is what keeps tied layers distinct.
- **Pattern:** only the feed-forward part is made dynamic; full sharing loses and partial sharing wins; the router matters little; all of it is routing for capacity, evaluated at a fixed number of passes on language benchmarks.

### Theorem proving
- [**Wu, Jiang, Ba & Grosse (ICLR 2021), *INT*.**](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2007.02924)‡ (truncated in the search section) A synthetic inequality prover built to test generalization over axiom combinations (K) and proof length (L). With 25 training axiom combinations a transformer proves 29.3 % with unseen combinations (a GNN 47.5 %); with ≥ 100 the transformer is ahead; success falls ~12 points per extra proof step; test-time MCTS helps (25 % → 40 % on longer proofs). **Rung 4's questions, asked properly in 2021; we trained on eight combinations.**
- AlphaProof's network ([supplement](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-025-09833-y/MediaObjects/41586_2025_9833_MOESM1_ESM.pdf)‡, hyperparameter tables): a 3B encoder–decoder, 18 encoder and 24 decoder blocks, a binned value head, 6 sampled tactics per node. The encoder–decoder shape for Lean is established; no loop.

### Probing latent trajectories of looped and recurrent models (deeper check, 11 Nov 2026; history Entry 84)

‡ = page or abstract read (most pages truncated by the reader at ~100k characters). The question: can the set of states that decode to a correct answer, and the way a loop reaches it, be defined by measurements? A toolkit exists; none of it has been applied to proof states as far as two targeted searches went.

**The endpoint: which states decode correctly**
- [**Anil et al. (NeurIPS 2022), *Path Independent Equilibrium Models Can Better Exploit Test-Time Computation*.**](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.09961)‡ Path independence = the loop reaches the same limit from any starting state; measured by the Asymptotic Alignment score (cosine similarity between fixed points reached from the training initialization and from other inputs' fixed points). It "strongly correlates" with accuracy on harder instances needing more iterations (mazes: AA 1.00 with 100 % against AA 0.32 with 87 %); promoted by mixed initialization and randomized depth; weight tying and input injection are necessary; penalizing it lowers accuracy; per-instance, it flags correct predictions without labels.
- [**Ren & Liu (Jan 2026), *Are Your Reasoning Models Reasoning or Guessing? A Mechanistic Analysis of HRM*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10679v1)‡ Fixed points are not reliably stable (≈ 75 % on one-cell-masked Sudoku); several fixed points exist and spurious ones are attractors; the model tends to settle in the first it reaches, often the nearest to its initialization; loss across segments is flat and then drops in a few steps ("guessing" more than "reasoning"). Fixes that amount to restarts and voting take Sudoku-Extreme from 54.5 % to 96.9 %.
- [**Efstathiou & Balwani (ICLR 2026), *Recursive Reasoning as Attractor Landscape Search: Mechanistic Dynamics of the Tiny Recursive Model*.**](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/10016652)‡ (abstract) Sparse-autoencoder features and latent PCA across recursion: hypotheses form early, later steps stabilize more than refine; trajectories diverge within the first steps into initialization-dependent minima; failures are stable high-loss attractors; noise on the answer initialization and more attempts escape them.
- [**Roye-Azar et al. (2026), *Tiny Recursive Models on ARC-AGI-1*.**](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2512.11847)‡ Decoding after each recursion: pass@1 38.25 % at step 1 against 40.50 % at step 4 — the first application does most of the work. The ARC Prize analysis of HRM ([blog](https://arcprize.org/blog/hrm-analysis)‡) found the outer refinement loop, not the hierarchy, was the driver.
- [**Movahedi et al. (2026), *Fixed-Point Reasoners*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.18206)‡ and [**Fein-Ashley & Rashidinejad (2026), *Solve the Loop: Attractor Models*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12466v1)‡ Stability by design (pre-norm with tied residual scaling, damping; implicit differentiation); the relative residual of the iteration is both the convergence measure and the halting signal; compute scales with difficulty.

**The path: how the loop gets there**
- [**Taufeeque, Tucker, Gleave & Garriga-Alonso (2025), *Interpreting learned search … an RNN that plays Sokoban*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10138v1)‡ The standard to aim at: plans live in direction-specific "path channels", are extended by identifiable kernels, pruned by value-like magnitudes and committed by winner-take-all; readouts predict future moves with AUC > 95 %, interventions change the action in 99.7 % of cases, ablating the path channels costs 57.6 points of solve rate. Extra thinking steps had been shown to help (+4.7 %).
- [**Zhu et al. (2025), *Reasoning by Superposition*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12514v3)‡ Graph reachability: a 2-layer transformer with D continuous-thought steps suffices (D = diameter) where discrete chain of thought needs O(n²) steps; a continuous thought is a superposition of search frontiers (parallel breadth-first search), and this emerges in training on optimal paths alone.
- [**Kaissis et al. (Feb 2026), *Step-resolved data attribution for looped transformers*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.10097)‡ Influence decomposed per loop iteration: a period-4 cycle on parity; harder Sudoku puzzles put their influence in late iterations.
- [**Chang et al. (2026), *Unlocking the Black Box of Latent Reasoning*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01243)‡ Coconut and CODI latents align with explicit reasoning steps (CKA, mappers, a lexical probe) but causal effects are small (dropping a latent flips ~10 % of answers; transplants 2.5 %): decodable is not the same as used.
- Also: the tuned lens (Belrose et al. 2023) and *Lines of Thought in LLMs* (Sarfati et al., ICLR 2025) for dense models; the fast-slow paper's stack-depth probes on Dyck (above).

**What this does to our direction**
1. *The measurements exist:* decoded answer and loss per iteration; the iteration's residual; path independence under changed starting states; basin mapping and spurious attractors; probes with causal interventions; per-step attribution.
2. *Two regimes are reported.* On refinement tasks trained with supervision at every step (Sudoku, ARC) the loop mostly "falls into a basin": answer early, then stabilize; failures are wrong attractors; restarts help. On sequential tasks (Sokoban plans, reachability, parity) the loop does stepwise or parallel-frontier work. Which regime a proof task with a given training signal produces is not known.
3. *Not found* (two targeted searches, not a full review): any of these measurements on a prover's latent states; reuse of latent moves across problems; the effect of experts on trajectories. Lean adds one thing the puzzle work lacks: the kernel verifies the decoding of *any* state, and a goal has many valid next steps, so the set of correct states is a union of regions that can be mapped exactly.

### Structure that emerges when a loop is given room (12 Nov 2026; history Entry 85)

‡ = page or abstract read on 12 Nov (several truncated). Read for the researcher's request: less hand engineering, what exists, what is left along the original thesis.

- [**Kohli, Parthasarathy, Sun & Yao (2026), *Loop, Think, & Generalize: Implicit Reasoning in Recurrent-Depth Transformers*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.07822)‡ Small looped transformers on synthetic multi-hop knowledge-graph tasks: "recurrent-depth transformers exhibit strong systematic generalization, while vanilla transformers fail to do so"; deeper chains with more inference iterations; training-time maximum recurrence sets the range and randomly sampled recurrence extends it; three grokking stages; *overthinking* (logit margins peak and decline with more loops); scaling the model did not clearly improve extrapolation. The follow-up to Wang et al. (2024) that our 10 Nov review said was missing.
- [**Liang, Jin & Pan (Sep 2026), *Beyond the Training Horizon: Mechanisms and Limits of Length Generalization in Looped Transformers*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33144v1)‡ (first 100k characters of a long page) Polynomial iteration, finite-state function composition, multi-hop traversal; **final answer supervised only**. Two learned styles: a fixed readout state updated each loop, or intermediate states propagated across positions as an advancing frontier. States carry *computational status* as well as content (live/consumed, fresh/aged): an aged state stays decodable (96.9 %) but no longer supports the next hop (0.650 → 0.038), and adding the direction restores it (9.4 % → 93.8 % in one case). Beyond the horizon the cues degrade and errors compound with little self-correction. Activation patching and state swaps throughout — a worked example of "decodable is not used".
- [**Shen, Su & Kyrillidis (2026), *One Model, Two Roles: Emergent Specialization in a Shared Recurrent Transformer*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.17811)‡ One shared model with two states whose only built-in difference is whether the input is injected: one state becomes a committed proposal, the other keeps local uncertainty. The model "needs to be able to tell the two update types apart"; an attendable tag recovers most of the gain, an added vector does not.
- [**Lee, Lampinen, Singh & Saxe (2025), *Distinct Computations Emerge From Compositional Curricula in In-Context Learning*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.13253)‡ Whether intermediate sub-task results are represented (linear probes) depends on the curriculum: when sub-tasks co-occur in context and the composed block is short, the model computes compositionally and generalizes zero-shot to unseen compositions; otherwise it mixes in a direct strategy. "Not a binary strategy choice but rather a spectrum."
- [**Todd et al. (ICLR 2024), *Function Vectors in Large Language Models*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.15213v2)‡ (truncated before the vector-algebra section) A task demonstrated in context is carried by a compact vector that triggers the procedure in new contexts (zero-shot 9.5 % → 57.5 %) and can be summed to compose tasks "to some extent". Soft, reusable, partly composable primitives already exist in dense models. *Circuit Component Reuse Across Tasks* (Merullo et al. 2024) is the circuit-level counterpart (title only).
- **Pattern.** Structure appeared in each case from the training conditions — what is supervised and when, what co-occurs in context, a minimal asymmetry that lets the model tell two situations apart — not from designed modules.
- **What these leave open, as far as this search went.** The programs in these tasks are *supplied* (the relations or operators sit in the input, in order) and each step is the same kind of operation. Not found: a loop that must *choose* the next operation among several kinds (the router's job in the original thesis), and whether the choice is held in the state as a distribution over next steps that sharpens across iterations — the "amortized posterior over programs", measured inside the loop. Rung 4's carrier has the exact posterior over shortest-proof first steps and a verifier, which is the ground truth such a measurement needs.

### Does "decode several paths, verify, feed back into a recurrent encoder" exist? (novelty check, 12 Nov 2026; history Entry 86)

The researcher's scheme: a looped encoder holds probability mass over next paths; the autoregressive decoder is where lock-in happens; decode several paths in parallel, let the kernel judge, feed the outcomes back into the encoder, iterate — exploration internalized by recursion. Six searches; pages read in full unless noted. One page could not be read (rate-limited twice): *Latent Recurrent Thoughts: Recurrent Refinement of Proposed Latents for Reasoning with Frozen LLMs* (arXiv 2609.01117).

### Prior work the design review found for the belief experiment (read 13 Nov 2026; history Entry 92)

Design review 1 named three papers I had not read. All three read through (offsets to the end of each page).

- [**Decision-Pretrained Transformer** (Lee, Xie, Pacchiano, Chandak, Finn, Nachum & Brunskill, NeurIPS 2023)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.14892) (complete, 27 pages with appendices). Supervised pretraining: sample a task, an in-context dataset of interactions from it, a query state, and train a transformer to predict the *optimal action* at the query state. **Theorem 1:** the trained model's behaviour equals posterior sampling, under three conditions — the model fits the pretraining distribution exactly (their Assumption 1), the in-context data are **compliant** (each recorded action is conditionally independent of the task given the state and the data so far), and there is enough support for the conditionals to be defined. Data from random or adaptive policies (PPO, Thompson sampling) are compliant; **expert-biased data are not** ("privileged knowledge of τ is being used"), and the model trained on them behaves differently. Test time: greedy on a fixed dataset, sampling online. Environments: bandits, linear bandits, Dark Room (100 goals, 80 for training), Miniworld (four tasks). The number of pretraining tasks is **not varied**; generalisation beyond the pretraining distribution is called "preliminary".
  *For this program:* the argument in the belief design that the cross-entropy minimiser is the Bayes predictor, and its requirement that proposals not depend on the hidden world, are this theorem and its compliance condition, specialised. They are not new, and the design now says so. What the paper does not have: a finite pool of tasks with both the memorising and the generalising posterior computed exactly, a coverage sweep, or any score for cheaper mechanisms than the posterior.
- [**The Bayesian Geometry of Transformer Attention** — "Bayesian wind tunnels" (Agarwal, Dalal & Misra, Dec 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.22471v3) (main text complete; the HTML page carries no appendices). A wind tunnel is a task where "the analytic posterior is known exactly at each step" and "the hypothesis space is so large that memorization is computationally infeasible"; a fresh hypothesis is drawn for every training sequence. Tasks: bijection elimination (V = 20; the posterior is uniform over the outputs not yet seen), HMM filtering (five states, a fresh HMM per sequence), Bayesian regression, associative recall. Small transformers match the exact posterior (entropy error 0.007 bits on bijections, 0.049 on HMMs; KL ~10⁻⁴–10⁻³ nats; smooth degradation to 2.5× the training length); capacity-matched MLPs fail (1.85 and 0.40 bits); an LSTM fails on HMMs and recall. Mechanism: near-orthogonal keys in the first layer, elimination sharpening across depth, value vectors on a curve indexed by posterior entropy.
  *For this program:* the closest methodological relative — exact posterior as the yardstick — and it already reports that small transformers reach it in distribution. Three differences. Memorisation is excluded by construction (fresh hypotheses), so the coverage question is not asked. No cheaper mechanism is scored against the posterior; in the bijection task the exact posterior *is* a deletion (remove the outputs already seen), the case history Entry 87 showed carries no updating beyond a mask. There is no acting model and no checker: evidence arrives, it is not chosen.
- [**Meta-trained agents implement Bayes-optimal agents** (Mikulik, Delétang, McGrath, Genewein, Martic, Legg & Ortega, NeurIPS 2020)](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2010.11223) (complete with appendices). Memory-based meta-learners (LSTMs) trained on tasks drawn fresh from a prior match Bayes-optimal agents in behaviour (KL between predictive distributions; reward difference for bandits, against Gittins-index policies) and in structure (a learned map between internal states and sufficient statistics; the Bayes-optimal agent simulates the network well, the reverse less well). Ten prediction and four bandit tasks with conjugate priors; trained at 20 steps, near-optimal at 30. Caveat stated by the authors: the result holds when the optimal policy is in the search space and training converges to it. Untrained networks also score well on state similarity for bandits ("reservoir effect"), so state similarity alone can mislead. The theoretical step (log-loss over the task distribution is minimised by the Bayes mixture) is cited to earlier work (their reference 10; not read by me) and not proved in the paper. Finite task sets and off-distribution inputs other than longer episodes: not covered.
  *For this program:* in-distribution Bayes-optimality of a recurrent learner is a 2020 result. The belief experiment's in-distribution reading is therefore a gate (did training reach the known optimum), not a finding.

**What these three leave to the belief experiment** (as far as five searches by the reviewer and these readings go): a finite pool of hidden worlds with the memorising and generalising posteriors both exact (Raventós et al. have this for in-context regression, without actions); evidence that the model itself elicits from a checker; evidence patterns that no training world fits; and the cheapest non-Bayesian mechanisms scored beside the posterior.
