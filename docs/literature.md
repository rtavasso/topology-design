## 1. Parameterization → dynamics → implicit bias (Phase 0)

These answer most of the v0 Study 1 in closed form. The v0 review did not cite any of them.

- **Arora, Cohen & Hazan (ICML 2018), *On the Optimization of Deep Networks: Implicit Acceleration by Overparameterization*.** Balanced deep linear chains follow a closed-form preconditioned flow on the end-to-end matrix. **Use:** Prop. 3 and Prop. 4.
- **Du, Hu & Lee (NeurIPS 2018), *Algorithmic Regularization in Learning Deep Homogeneous Models*.** Layer balancedness is conserved under gradient flow.
- **Arora, Cohen, Hu & Luo (NeurIPS 2019), *Implicit Regularization in Deep Matrix Factorization*.** Depth strengthens low-rank bias. **Use:** expected implicit-bias direction for chains.
- **Hardt & Ma (ICLR 2017), *Identity Matters in Deep Learning*; Bartlett, Helmbold & Long (ICML 2018), identity-initialized deep linear residual networks.** **Use:** the identity-initialized regime of our residual family; explains why Saxe-style plateaus are absent at identity init.
- **Kawaguchi (NeurIPS 2016); Laurent & von Brecht (ICML 2018).** Deep linear networks have no bad local minima. **Use:** Prop. 6 endpoint equivalence.
- **Soudry, Hoffer, Nacson, Gunasekar & Srebro (JMLR 2018); Gunasekar, Lee, Soudry & Srebro (NeurIPS 2018, linear convolutional networks); Ji & Telgarsky (ICLR 2019).** Directional implicit bias under separable cross-entropy, and how architecture changes it. **Use:** Prop. 6 separable case (open).
- **Woodworth et al. (COLT 2020), *Kernel and Rich Regimes in Overparametrized Models*.** Parameterization plus init scale interpolate between kernel and rich implicit bias. **Use:** the richness axis.
- [**Kunin et al. (NeurIPS 2024), *Get Rich Quick*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06158)† Unbalanced initializations promote rapid feature learning; exact solutions. **Use:** Phase 0 imbalanced-init item.
- [**Dominé et al. (ICLR 2025), *From Lazy to Rich: Exact Learning Dynamics in Deep Linear Networks*.**](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2025/domine2025iclr-lazy)† Lazy-to-rich transitions controlled by λ-balancedness.
- [**Marcotte, Gribonval & Peyré (NeurIPS 2023), *Abide by the Law and Follow the Flow*.**](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/c7bee9b76be21146fd592fc2b46614d5-Abstract.html)† An algorithm for finding all conservation laws of a parameterization. **Use:** Prop. 7, mixed graphs.
- **Kunin et al. (ICLR 2021), *Neural Mechanics*; Zhao et al. (ICLR 2023), *Symmetries, Flat Minima, and the Conserved Quantities of Gradient Flow*.** Symmetry → conserved quantity → dynamics.
- [**Lippl, Abbott & Chung (2022), *The Implicit Bias of Gradient Descent on Generalized Gated Linear Networks*.**](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/358423122_The_Implicit_Bias_of_Gradient_Descent_on_Generalized_Gated_Linear_Networks)† Architecture-dependent implicit bias with fixed gates.
- **Saxe, McClelland & Ganguli (ICLR 2014)** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.6120); **Xu & Ziyin (ICLR 2025)**; **Shi, Shea-Brown & Buice (NeurIPS 2022)**. Retained from v0. Note that Saxe 2014's staged mode learning depends on *small* initialization.

## Novelty boundary (after the literature review)

**Known (do not claim; cite as prior work or corroboration):**
- deep linear flows and implicit bias for chains, diagonal, convolutional and gated-linear parameterizations;
- topology cannot change local conservation laws (Marcotte 2025);
- parallel-branch collapse (Ziyin 2024; multi-branch convexity);
- identity-init orientation lock (Bartlett 2018);
- weight-decay bias of residual chains (Boix-Adsera 2025);
- lazy-regime kernel effects of connectivity;
- expressive-efficiency gaps from added connections (Cohen, Tamari & Shashua);
- pathway races in gated networks;
- shortcut/core competition;
- gradient coherence;
- the sample-complexity separation between 2- and 3-layer networks.

**Candidate contributions (open as far as the review searched):**
1. gradient-flow implicit bias for **general linear residual DAGs**, including which path-liftings commute (Phase 0);
2. **RLCT of residual and parallel parameterizations** of the same function class (Phase 0);
3. **richness-gated topology effects**, shown as a phase boundary with a dose–response over one-edge mutants;
4. **constituent→composite lag dynamics** across routing graphs (Phase 2);
5. **matched-parameter sequential vs parallel transformer blocks** compared on internal structure and induction-head timing (Phase 4);
6. **topology-dependent erasure cost**, separating the identity path from shortcuts (Phase 4).

## Routed, layer-shared, adaptively halting transformers (added 6 Nov 2026; the architectural family the thesis belongs to)

The researcher asked whether the proposal is "a type of MoE with dynamic halting, with experts reused per layer that are not just MLPs." At the block-diagram level it is, and this family was absent from the novelty boundary until now. Read in full before any architectural priority is claimed:

- [**Dehghani et al. (ICLR 2019), *Universal Transformers*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03819) One layer applied recurrently with per-position adaptive computation time (Graves 2016, ACT, [arXiv 1603.08983](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.08983)); [PonderNet (Banino et al. 2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.05407) is the probabilistic halting form.
- [**Tan, Shen, Chen, Courville & Gan (EMNLP 2023), *Sparse Universal Transformer*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07096) A UT whose shared layer is a sparse mixture of experts for **both attention and feed-forward**, with stick-breaking ACT halting; reports compositional-generalization gains (CFQ) and efficiency. **This is the closest existing architecture to the thesis's carrier at scale.**
- [**Csordás, Irie, Schmidhuber, Potts & Manning (NeurIPS 2024), *MoEUT: Mixture-of-Experts Universal Transformers*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16039) Layer-shared σ-MoE feed-forward and SwitchHead attention experts with layer grouping; competitive with dense transformers on language modelling at matched parameters and compute — not better in resolution, which is the fact the thesis has to explain (below).
- [**Bae et al. (2025), *Mixture-of-Recursions*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10524) Recursive (weight-shared) transformers with lightweight routers assigning a per-token recursion depth; the per-token depth-elasticity half of the thesis, with end-to-end-trained routers.
- [**Raposo et al. (2024), *Mixture-of-Depths*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02258) Per-token routing around blocks under a capacity budget: elasticity without a shared library.
- [**Rosenbaum, Klinger & Riemer (ICLR 2018), *Routing Networks***](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.01239) and [**Kirsch, Kunze & Barber (NeurIPS 2018), *Modular Networks*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.05249) Per-example composition of function blocks by a learned router, trained by RL (Rosenbaum; collapse problems) or by generalized EM over module assignments (Kirsch; EM chosen to avoid collapse). **Kirsch et al. is a direct precedent for rung 2's finding that posterior / hard-EM targets train a router where reward-only signals collapse (F35, F40); our results refine it (exact vs sampled posteriors, search, commitment), they do not originate it.**
- Neural Module Networks (Andreas et al. 2016) and the Neural Programmer-Interpreter (Reed & de Freitas 2016) for composition of named modules by a controller.

**Where the boundary sits.** Not in the architecture: a shared library of attention-and-MLP experts, routed per token per step with adaptive halting, exists (SUT, MoEUT, MoR). The thesis's claims are about (i) what the router *is* — amortized posterior inference over programs under a data prior (a PFN), routing on an explicit **computational state** with a writable register and setup steps rather than on the residual stream for load; (ii) how it is **trained** — by posterior or search targets (rungs 2–3: exactness against self-training, F45), not by end-to-end soft gating with load-balancing losses, which in our vocabulary is the own-policy family; (iii) what such a router **does** — programs as closures of per-state decisions (F30), the signal choosing among tied programs (F37), plasticity after commitment (F45–F49), generalization across worlds (rung 4), chains against bags (rung 5), library growth by abstraction (risk 9). None of these is posed or measured in the papers above, which report perplexity or benchmark accuracy. **The fact to explain:** MoEUT routes depth with shared experts and is parameter-matched-competitive, not better, than dense; the ledger's line "MoE routes width and has not produced resolution beyond parameter count" (1 Nov) does not cover it. The thesis's answer has to be that gating-trained, residual-routed routers learn wide bags rather than serial chains (Entry 74), which makes rung 5 posable *on a MoEUT-style carrier*: the same experts, a posterior-or-search-trained router with state against a gating-trained one, on a task with a depth variable. If the training signal and the state do not buy depth extrapolation there, the thesis is wrong in the way that matters.

## The thesis against prior work: a pushback review (added 10 Nov 2026, after rung 4; history Entry 82)

The researcher asked for the thesis and the ideas of the 10 Nov discussion to be grounded in prior work and pushed against. Entries marked ‡ had their abstract or full page read on 10 Nov 2026; unmarked entries are cited from memory of well-known results and should be checked before being quoted. Each entry says what it does to the thesis.

### A. The depth claim ("a token needing k > L steps is unreachable for a dense model")

- [**Merrill & Sabharwal (ICLR 2024), *The Expressive Power of Transformers with Chain of Thought*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07923v4)‡ With a polynomial number of intermediate decoding steps a transformer recognizes exactly the polynomial-time problems; linear steps add all regular languages; logarithmic steps add little. **Against:** the depth bound holds for one forward pass only. A dense model with a scratchpad already has unbounded serial depth and pays in tokens. The thesis cannot claim reach; it has to claim cost (compute per solved problem) or learnability.
- [**Liu, Ash, Goel, Krishnamurthy & Zhang (ICLR 2023), *Transformers Learn Shortcuts to Automata*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10749v1)‡ A T-step automaton can be simulated in O(log T) layers always and O(1) often, and standard training finds such shortcuts; the paper also studies their brittleness. **Against:** "required depth = number of sequential steps" is false for dense transformers in distribution. **Matches** F53 (the no-search router past lookahead-3). **For:** the brittleness of shortcuts is where an iterated primitive could win — off-support, which is where rung 4 found the prior failing.
- [**Ruoss et al. (NeurIPS 2024), *Amortized Planning with Large-Scale Transformers: A Case Study on Chess* (preprint title: *Grandmaster-Level Chess Without Search*).**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04494)‡ A 270M-parameter transformer trained by supervision on Stockfish annotations of 10M games reaches Lichess blitz Elo 2895 with no search; "perfect distillation is still beyond reach". **Against / matches:** search amortizes into a dense model very far given support (F53 at scale). Lehnert et al. (2024), *Beyond A\** (Searchformer), is the planning counterpart.
- [**Dziri et al. (NeurIPS 2023), *Faith and Fate: Limits of Transformers on Compositionality*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18654v3) Transformers solve multi-step compositional tasks by reducing them to linearized subgraph matching; accuracy decays with compositional depth. **For:** the motivation — the dense model's in-support competence is pattern matching, not composition.
- [**Wang, Yue, Su & Sun (NeurIPS 2024), *Grokked Transformers are Implicit Reasoners*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15071)‡ Implicit multi-hop reasoning is learned only through grokking; out of distribution transformers "fail to systematically generalize for composition but succeed for comparison"; the mechanistic account ties the failure to the configuration of the generalizing circuit, and the authors suggest "encouraging cross-layer knowledge sharing". **For:** the closest published statement of the thesis's reuse claim — composition fails when the second hop's knowledge lives in different layers from the first's.
- [**Zhou et al. (ICLR 2024), *What Algorithms can Transformers Learn? A Study in Length Generalization*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16028v1)‡ The RASP-Generalization conjecture: a transformer tends to length-generalize on a task if a short RASP program solves it at all lengths. **Use:** a principled predictor for rung 4's Q2 (rules against tables) that we did not use; it says *which* tasks a dense model will extrapolate on, so a size-extrapolation test should be chosen on both sides of it.

### B. Reuse across depth (the "library" half)

- [**Jolicoeur-Martineau (2025), *Less is More: Recursive Reasoning with Tiny Networks* (TRM)**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.04871)‡, after the Hierarchical Reasoning Model (Wang et al. 2025). One 2-layer network (5–19M parameters) recursed on a latent state with deep supervision and a learned halt: ARC-AGI-1 44.6 %, ARC-AGI-2 7.8 %, Sudoku-Extreme 87.4 %, Maze-Hard 85.3 % as reported, above HRM (27M) and most LLMs listed. **For:** the strongest current evidence that reuse in depth buys reasoning at tiny parameter counts. **Against:** it does so with **one block and no router** (m = 1), trained with a signal at every recursion. What a library of m > 1 primitives and a router add over a loop is exactly what the thesis has not shown; the ledger's F32 (a loop never learned the second circuit) is our only evidence and it is one toy.
- [**Sun, Pickett, Nain & Jones (2024), *Transformer Layers as Painters*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.09298v4)‡ In trained LLMs the middle layers share a representation space and tolerate skipping, reordering and parallel execution with graceful loss — but replacing them by copies of one layer's weights is the most damaging intervention, worse than skipping; looping a parallelized group helps; math and reasoning tasks are the most order-sensitive. **Against:** a trained dense model's layers are not repetitions of one primitive. **Open (risk 1):** whether they are a small library of m, as a curve in m — nobody in this search has measured it; ALBERT (Lan et al. 2020) is the trained-from-scratch version of m = 1 and loses some accuracy.
- MoEUT‡ ([abstract re-read](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16039v2)): "slightly outperforms standard Transformers on language modeling tasks such as BLiMP and PIQA, while using significantly less compute and memory". The fact to explain stands: shared experts with gating are competitive, not qualitatively better.

### C. Learning the router (the "routing" half)

- [**Rosenbaum, Cases, Riemer & Klinger (2019), *Routing Networks and the Challenges of Modular and Compositional Computation*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.12774)‡ Names the problems of learning modules and their composition together: the chicken-and-egg instability at the start, module collapse, overfitting through exponentially many paths, the transfer–interference trade-off coupled to exploration, non-stationarity (the "actions" change as modules train); routing "often needs external structure" such as task labels or curricula. **Against / matches:** our F22–F23, F32 and F40 are instances of a documented family, not discoveries. The idea of labelling routes by search under the current primitives and alternating (10 Nov discussion) is their setting; Kirsch et al.'s EM is the best-behaved member.
- [**Mittal, Bengio & Lajoie (NeurIPS 2022), *Is a Modular Architecture Enough?***](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02713v1)‡ On data with known modular structure, end-to-end learned modular systems are "sub-optimal … as opposed to their claimed potential"; the gap is in optimization (specialization), not capacity. **Against:** having the right architecture does not deliver the right routing.
- [**Roller, Sukhbaatar, Szlam & Weston (NeurIPS 2021), *Hash Layers For Large Sparse Models*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04426v2)‡ Routing by a fixed hash of the token "either outperforms or is competitive with learning-to-route mixture-of-expert methods" (Switch, BASE). **Against:** at scale, gating-trained routers have learned little that a random fixed assignment does not supply. The thesis's reply has to be that the *training signal* is the difference (posterior or search targets on an explicit state), which rungs 2–3 support with handed-over primitives only.

### D. Support, and the router as a PFN

- [**Müller et al. (ICLR 2022), *Transformers Can Do Bayesian Inference*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10510v6) A PFN approximates the posterior predictive under the prior it is trained on. Nothing is promised off the prior's support.
- [**Raventós, Paul, Chen & Ganguli (NeurIPS 2023), *Pretraining task diversity and the emergence of non-Bayesian in-context learning for regression*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15063)‡ Below a task-diversity threshold (between 2^14 and 2^15 tasks in their base setting) the transformer "cannot solve unseen regression tasks" and "behaves like a Bayesian estimator with the non-diverse pretraining task distribution as the prior"; above it, it departs from that estimator and matches the solution for the full task distribution. **This is rung 4's P2 result, predicted:** eight training worlds is far below any such threshold, and a router that acts like a posterior over the eight worlds it saw is what the literature says to expect. The researcher's 10 Nov critique (insufficient support confounds the reading) is this paper's finding. **Consequence:** "does the router recombine" is not a yes/no question; it is a threshold in the number and diversity of worlds, and that threshold is the quantity to measure.
- [**Schug et al. (ICLR 2024), *Discovering modular solutions that generalize compositionally*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15001v2)‡ In a teacher–student setting with a modular teacher and hypernetwork students, "identification up to linear transformation purely from demonstrations is possible" without seeing "an exponential number of module combinations", under conditions on the training distribution (as I recall the body: the support must cover each module and connect them; not re-read). **For:** recombination from sub-exponential coverage is possible in principle, with multiplicative (hypernetwork-like) conditioning on the task code. **Against rung 4's design:** the presence tokens entered additively as input tokens with eight patterns.

### E. Designed primitives, designed state, and labels from traces

- **RASP** (Weiss, Goldberg & Yahav, ICML 2021) and [**Tracr**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05062) (Lindner et al., NeurIPS 2023): transformer weights compiled from programs — designing rather than learning primitives is established, on a designed (superposition-free) state code.
- **Neural Programmer-Interpreters** (Reed & de Freitas, ICLR 2016) and [**Cai, Shin & Song (ICLR 2017), *Making Neural Programming Architectures Generalize via Recursion*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06611) A learned controller over designed operations, trained on **execution traces**; with recursion, generalization can be verified exhaustively. **Matches** the 10 Nov conclusion: designed primitives move the whole problem to the controller, and intermediate labels exist only where a reference algorithm's trace exists.
- [**Ellis et al. (PLDI 2021), *DreamCoder*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.08381) A designed base language, a library grown by abstraction over found programs, and a neural recognition model trained on solved tasks and on programs sampled from the prior ("dreams") to guide search. **Against novelty:** "an amortized posterior over programs under a prior, with a library that grows by abstraction" is DreamCoder, in symbolic program synthesis, in 2021. What the thesis adds can only be that the programs run inside a network's latent computation.
- [**Mentzer et al. (ICLR 2024), *Finite Scalar Quantization*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15505v2) The bounded, per-dimension-rounded latent whose implicit codebook is a grid of hypercubes — the "hypercube latent" of the 10 Nov discussion, as a quantizer.
- [**AlphaProof (Nature 2025), *Olympiad-level formal mathematical reasoning with reinforcement learning*.**](https://research.google/pubs/olympiad-level-formal-mathematical-reasoning-with-reinforcement-learning/) and the line behind it (GPT-f, HTPS, expert iteration, LeanDojo's premise retrieval): a learned policy and value over Lean's tactics with search, trained by reinforcement on found proofs. **Against:** under the "tactics are the designed primitives, the kernel is the executor" reading, the thesis's outer loop is this line of work, at a scale we cannot approach. Rung 4 was a toy of it.

### What survives, and what it changes

1. **Three cheaper alternatives stand between the thesis and its payoff.** Depth: a scratchpad (A). Reuse: one looped block (B). Routing: a fixed or random assignment (C). The thesis is the claim that a *library with a posterior- or search-trained router on an explicit state* beats all three — dense with chain of thought at matched compute, a single recursed block at matched parameters, and the same library with gating-trained or hashed routing. No paper found here runs that comparison; neither have we.
2. **The regime is off-support.** In support, dense models shortcut and amortize (Liu, Ruoss, F53). Any claimed advantage has to be shown where shortcuts break — deeper compositions than trained, new combinations — with coverage as a measured variable (Raventós), not a fixed split.
3. **The published hint in the thesis's favour** is specific: composition fails out of distribution when knowledge is not shared across layers (Wang et al.), and a recursed tiny block outperforms far larger models on puzzle reasoning (TRM). Both support reuse; neither supports a router.
4. **Not new:** the architecture (SUT, MoEUT, MoR); EM or search labels for routing (Kirsch); the pathologies of joint training (Rosenbaum); the support threshold (Raventós); amortized posteriors over programs with a growing library (DreamCoder); designed primitives with trace supervision (NPI); policy-plus-search over Lean (AlphaProof).
5. **Still open as far as this search went:** the fidelity of a trained deep model as a router over m primitives, as a curve in m (risk 1); a router trained by posterior or search targets over *learned* reused primitives; the three-way comparison of item 1 stratified by required computation off-support.

## Looped transformers, experts inside loops, and latent reasoning (added 11 Nov 2026; history Entry 83)

Read on 11 Nov 2026 during the discussion that moved the program from discrete primitives toward looped encoders. ‡ = abstract or page read that day (several pages were truncated by the reader; noted where it matters). One line each on what the work shows and what it does to our direction.

### What a looped block learns
- [**Yang, Lee, Nowak & Papailiopoulos (ICLR 2024), *Looped Transformers are Better at Learning Learning Algorithms*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.12424v3)‡ (read to §5.3) One GPT-2 layer looped ~20 times matches an 8–12-layer model on in-context regression with 1/12 of the parameters; the prompt is **re-injected every iteration** (Y_{t+1} = M(Y_t + P)) — without it the loop diverges past trained iterations, with it the loop settles to a fixed point; loss over a window of the last T iterations. Underperforms the dense model under input rescaling.
- [**Gatmiry, Saunshi, Reddi, Jegelka & Kumar (ICML 2024), *Can Looped Transformers Learn to Implement Multi-step Gradient Descent for In-context Learning?***](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2410.08292)‡ For linear looped attention on in-context regression the global minimizer does one step of preconditioned gradient descent per loop, with the preconditioner ≈ the inverse population covariance: context = the problem, state = the iterate, weights = the prior. The literal "gradient field" case.
- [**Fan, Du, Ramchandran & Lee (ICLR 2025), *Looped Transformers for Length Generalization*.**](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2409.15647)‡ (results tables not read) n-RASP-L: a fixed RASP-L step repeated T(n) times. Parity, copy, addition: each loop is an algorithmic step, not descent; step count supplied per example or chosen by confidence; **no intermediate supervision** — varying lengths supervise the same block at many depths; input injection; full-output prediction.
- [**Giannou et al. (ICML 2023), *Looped Transformers as Programmable Computers*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13196v1)‡ A fixed 13-layer looped block as an interpreter; the input is the punchcard (program and memory).
- [**Saunshi et al. (ICLR 2025), *Reasoning with Latent Thoughts*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17416v1)‡ (abstract) k layers looped L times ≈ kL layers on reasoning tasks; T loops simulate T chain-of-thought steps; a reasoning-against-memorization dichotomy.
- **Use:** a looped block is one rule for moving the state, driven by a re-injected input; a gradient field is the special case (estimation, refinement), a state-transition the other (algorithms, where stopping matters).

### Loops at scale: storage against manipulation
- [**Zhu et al. (2025), *Scaling Latent Reasoning via Looped Language Models* (Ouro).**](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25741v2)‡ (truncated) 1.4B and 2.6B looped LMs, 4 loop steps, 7.7T tokens; authors report matching 4B and 8B dense models on most benchmarks. Controlled experiments: looping does **not** raise storage (≈ 2 bits per parameter either way) and **does** raise knowledge manipulation (composition, multi-hop). Exit gates collapse without an entropy regularizer; stage II freezes the LM and trains the gate alone — "primitives first, control after", as our F22–F23 suggested.
- [**Chen, Vegesna, Dahal & Wilson (2026), *How Model Growth, Recursion, and Boundary Operators Influence Scaling Exponents*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.19107)‡ (truncated) Prelude–core–coda. On fresh data, weight sharing and a higher fixed loop count are not compute-optimal alone; growth schedules and a boundary operator (normalize, re-inject the prelude output) change the scaling exponent; looping regularizes when data is repeated.
- [**Aleksandrov et al. (2025), *AbbIE*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08567v1)‡ Head–body–tail, only the body looped; trained at 2 iterations, still improving at 8.
- [**Cai, Zhu, Dong, He & Arora (2026), *T²MLR*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15178)‡ (abstract) and [**Huang et al. (2026), *Latent Recurrent Transformer*.**](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.26797.md)‡ Recurrence across decoding steps (a mid-layer state carried to the next token) in autoregressive decoders; a localized middle block beats full-layer recurrence. The alternative to an encoder loop for where latent computation lives.
- [**Takashiro et al. (2026), *Fast-Slow Latent Recurrence for Train-Short, Test-Long Generalization*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.01577)‡ (truncated) Encoder → a set of latent tokens → a shared core applied T times per observation, the latent carried across the stream; weight-shared loop beats an unshared stack of twice the parameters out of distribution; probes find stack depth in the latent trajectory on Dyck. **Closest to the "looped encoder with registers" idea, and already a latent-path characterization against known structure.**

### Experts inside shared layers
- SUT and MoEUT (above). **Correction recorded:** SUT reports compositional-generalization gains (CFQ); on 11 Nov I told the researcher that none of the experts-in-loop papers tested reasoning, which is true of the three below and false of SUT.
- [**Nouriborji, Rohanian & Rohanian (2025), *Improving Recursive Transformers with Mixture of LoRAs* (ModernALBERT).**](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.12880v2)‡ A recursive **encoder** (shared layer groups) with token-routed low-rank experts on the shared feed-forward; attention untouched; 1 → 8 experts: 76.08 → 77.24 in ablation; 120M ≈ a 149M standard encoder on GLUE.
- [**Chen et al. (2026), *Mixture of Universal Experts*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04971v1)‡ (truncated) A pool of experts reachable from several depths, per-layer routers; letting every depth reach every shared expert was the worst ablation.
- [**Jaggi (2026), *Tying the Loop: Tied Expert Layers in Mixture-of-Experts Language Models*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16825v1)‡ (truncated) Expert feed-forward weights tied across groups of 4 layers, attention and routers per layer, first and last two layers untied; at 7B matches the untied model with half the parameters; untying routers changes which experts fire and barely changes the loss; attention is what keeps tied layers distinct.
- **Pattern:** only the feed-forward part is made dynamic; full sharing loses and partial sharing wins; the router matters little; all of it is routing for capacity, evaluated at a fixed number of passes on language benchmarks.

### Theorem proving
- [**Wu, Jiang, Ba & Grosse (ICLR 2021), *INT*.**](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2007.02924)‡ (truncated in the search section) A synthetic inequality prover built to test generalization over axiom combinations (K) and proof length (L). With 25 training axiom combinations a transformer proves 29.3 % with unseen combinations (a GNN 47.5 %); with ≥ 100 the transformer is ahead; success falls ~12 points per extra proof step; test-time MCTS helps (25 % → 40 % on longer proofs). **Rung 4's questions, asked properly in 2021; we trained on eight combinations.**
- AlphaProof's network ([supplement](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-025-09833-y/MediaObjects/41586_2025_9833_MOESM1_ESM.pdf)‡, hyperparameter tables): a 3B encoder–decoder, 18 encoder and 24 decoder blocks, a binned value head, 6 sampled tactics per node. The encoder–decoder shape for Lean is established; no loop.

### Probing latent trajectories of looped and recurrent models (deeper check, 11 Nov 2026; history Entry 84)

‡ = page or abstract read (most pages truncated by the reader at ~100k characters). The question: can the set of states that decode to a correct answer, and the way a loop reaches it, be defined by measurements? A toolkit exists; none of it has been applied to proof states as far as two targeted searches went.

**The endpoint: which states decode correctly**
- [**Anil et al. (NeurIPS 2022), *Path Independent Equilibrium Models Can Better Exploit Test-Time Computation*.**](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.09961)‡ Path independence = the loop reaches the same limit from any starting state; measured by the Asymptotic Alignment score (cosine similarity between fixed points reached from the training initialization and from other inputs' fixed points). It "strongly correlates" with accuracy on harder instances needing more iterations (mazes: AA 1.00 with 100 % against AA 0.32 with 87 %); promoted by mixed initialization and randomized depth; weight tying and input injection are necessary; penalizing it lowers accuracy; per-instance, it flags correct predictions without labels.
- [**Ren & Liu (Jan 2026), *Are Your Reasoning Models Reasoning or Guessing? A Mechanistic Analysis of HRM*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10679v1)‡ Fixed points are not reliably stable (≈ 75 % on one-cell-masked Sudoku); several fixed points exist and spurious ones are attractors; the model tends to settle in the first it reaches, often the nearest to its initialization; loss across segments is flat and then drops in a few steps ("guessing" more than "reasoning"). Fixes that amount to restarts and voting take Sudoku-Extreme from 54.5 % to 96.9 %.
- [**Efstathiou & Balwani (ICLR 2026), *Recursive Reasoning as Attractor Landscape Search: Mechanistic Dynamics of the Tiny Recursive Model*.**](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/10016652)‡ (abstract) Sparse-autoencoder features and latent PCA across recursion: hypotheses form early, later steps stabilize more than refine; trajectories diverge within the first steps into initialization-dependent minima; failures are stable high-loss attractors; noise on the answer initialization and more attempts escape them.
- [**Roye-Azar et al. (2026), *Tiny Recursive Models on ARC-AGI-1*.**](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2512.11847)‡ Decoding after each recursion: pass@1 38.25 % at step 1 against 40.50 % at step 4 — the first application does most of the work. The ARC Prize analysis of HRM ([blog](https://arcprize.org/blog/hrm-analysis)‡) found the outer refinement loop, not the hierarchy, was the driver.
- [**Movahedi et al. (2026), *Fixed-Point Reasoners*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.18206)‡ and [**Fein-Ashley & Rashidinejad (2026), *Solve the Loop: Attractor Models*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12466v1)‡ Stability by design (pre-norm with tied residual scaling, damping; implicit differentiation); the relative residual of the iteration is both the convergence measure and the halting signal; compute scales with difficulty.

**The path: how the loop gets there**
- [**Taufeeque, Tucker, Gleave & Garriga-Alonso (2025), *Interpreting learned search … an RNN that plays Sokoban*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10138v1)‡ The standard to aim at: plans live in direction-specific "path channels", are extended by identifiable kernels, pruned by value-like magnitudes and committed by winner-take-all; readouts predict future moves with AUC > 95 %, interventions change the action in 99.7 % of cases, ablating the path channels costs 57.6 points of solve rate. Extra thinking steps had been shown to help (+4.7 %).
- [**Zhu et al. (2025), *Reasoning by Superposition*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12514v3)‡ Graph reachability: a 2-layer transformer with D continuous-thought steps suffices (D = diameter) where discrete chain of thought needs O(n²) steps; a continuous thought is a superposition of search frontiers (parallel breadth-first search), and this emerges in training on optimal paths alone.
- [**Kaissis et al. (Feb 2026), *Step-resolved data attribution for looped transformers*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.10097)‡ Influence decomposed per loop iteration: a period-4 cycle on parity; harder Sudoku puzzles put their influence in late iterations.
- [**Chang et al. (2026), *Unlocking the Black Box of Latent Reasoning*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01243)‡ Coconut and CODI latents align with explicit reasoning steps (CKA, mappers, a lexical probe) but causal effects are small (dropping a latent flips ~10 % of answers; transplants 2.5 %): decodable is not the same as used.
- Also: the tuned lens (Belrose et al. 2023) and *Lines of Thought in LLMs* (Sarfati et al., ICLR 2025) for dense models; the fast-slow paper's stack-depth probes on Dyck (above).

**What this does to our direction**
1. *The measurements exist:* decoded answer and loss per iteration; the iteration's residual; path independence under changed starting states; basin mapping and spurious attractors; probes with causal interventions; per-step attribution.
2. *Two regimes are reported.* On refinement tasks trained with supervision at every step (Sudoku, ARC) the loop mostly "falls into a basin": answer early, then stabilize; failures are wrong attractors; restarts help. On sequential tasks (Sokoban plans, reachability, parity) the loop does stepwise or parallel-frontier work. Which regime a proof task with a given training signal produces is not known.
3. *Not found* (two targeted searches, not a full review): any of these measurements on a prover's latent states; reuse of latent moves across problems; the effect of experts on trajectories. Lean adds one thing the puzzle work lacks: the kernel verifies the decoding of *any* state, and a goal has many valid next steps, so the set of correct states is a union of regions that can be mapped exactly.

### Structure that emerges when a loop is given room (12 Nov 2026; history Entry 85)

‡ = page or abstract read on 12 Nov (several truncated). Read for the researcher's request: less hand engineering, what exists, what is left along the original thesis.

- [**Kohli, Parthasarathy, Sun & Yao (2026), *Loop, Think, & Generalize: Implicit Reasoning in Recurrent-Depth Transformers*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.07822)‡ Small looped transformers on synthetic multi-hop knowledge-graph tasks: "recurrent-depth transformers exhibit strong systematic generalization, while vanilla transformers fail to do so"; deeper chains with more inference iterations; training-time maximum recurrence sets the range and randomly sampled recurrence extends it; three grokking stages; *overthinking* (logit margins peak and decline with more loops); scaling the model did not clearly improve extrapolation. The follow-up to Wang et al. (2024) that our 10 Nov review said was missing.
- [**Liang, Jin & Pan (Sep 2026), *Beyond the Training Horizon: Mechanisms and Limits of Length Generalization in Looped Transformers*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33144v1)‡ (first 100k characters of a long page) Polynomial iteration, finite-state function composition, multi-hop traversal; **final answer supervised only**. Two learned styles: a fixed readout state updated each loop, or intermediate states propagated across positions as an advancing frontier. States carry *computational status* as well as content (live/consumed, fresh/aged): an aged state stays decodable (96.9 %) but no longer supports the next hop (0.650 → 0.038), and adding the direction restores it (9.4 % → 93.8 % in one case). Beyond the horizon the cues degrade and errors compound with little self-correction. Activation patching and state swaps throughout — a worked example of "decodable is not used".
- [**Shen, Su & Kyrillidis (2026), *One Model, Two Roles: Emergent Specialization in a Shared Recurrent Transformer*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.17811)‡ One shared model with two states whose only built-in difference is whether the input is injected: one state becomes a committed proposal, the other keeps local uncertainty. The model "needs to be able to tell the two update types apart"; an attendable tag recovers most of the gain, an added vector does not.
- [**Lee, Lampinen, Singh & Saxe (2025), *Distinct Computations Emerge From Compositional Curricula in In-Context Learning*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.13253)‡ Whether intermediate sub-task results are represented (linear probes) depends on the curriculum: when sub-tasks co-occur in context and the composed block is short, the model computes compositionally and generalizes zero-shot to unseen compositions; otherwise it mixes in a direct strategy. "Not a binary strategy choice but rather a spectrum."
- [**Todd et al. (ICLR 2024), *Function Vectors in Large Language Models*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.15213v2)‡ (truncated before the vector-algebra section) A task demonstrated in context is carried by a compact vector that triggers the procedure in new contexts (zero-shot 9.5 % → 57.5 %) and can be summed to compose tasks "to some extent". Soft, reusable, partly composable primitives already exist in dense models. *Circuit Component Reuse Across Tasks* (Merullo et al. 2024) is the circuit-level counterpart (title only).
- **Pattern.** Structure appeared in each case from the training conditions — what is supervised and when, what co-occurs in context, a minimal asymmetry that lets the model tell two situations apart — not from designed modules.
- **What these leave open, as far as this search went.** The programs in these tasks are *supplied* (the relations or operators sit in the input, in order) and each step is the same kind of operation. Not found: a loop that must *choose* the next operation among several kinds (the router's job in the original thesis), and whether the choice is held in the state as a distribution over next steps that sharpens across iterations — the "amortized posterior over programs", measured inside the loop. Rung 4's carrier has the exact posterior over shortest-proof first steps and a verifier, which is the ground truth such a measurement needs.

One page could not be read (rate-limited twice): *Latent Recurrent Thoughts: Recurrent Refinement of Proposed Latents for Reasoning with Frozen LLMs* (arXiv 2609.01117).

### Prior work the design review found for the belief experiment (read 13 Nov 2026; history Entry 92)

Design review 1 named three papers I had not read. All three read through (offsets to the end of each page).

- [**Decision-Pretrained Transformer** (Lee, Xie, Pacchiano, Chandak, Finn, Nachum & Brunskill, NeurIPS 2023)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.14892) (complete, 27 pages with appendices). Supervised pretraining: sample a task, an in-context dataset of interactions from it, a query state, and train a transformer to predict the *optimal action* at the query state. **Theorem 1:** the trained model's behaviour equals posterior sampling, under three conditions — the model fits the pretraining distribution exactly (their Assumption 1), the in-context data are **compliant** (each recorded action is conditionally independent of the task given the state and the data so far), and there is enough support for the conditionals to be defined. Data from random or adaptive policies (PPO, Thompson sampling) are compliant; **expert-biased data are not** ("privileged knowledge of τ is being used"), and the model trained on them behaves differently. Test time: greedy on a fixed dataset, sampling online. Environments: bandits, linear bandits, Dark Room (100 goals, 80 for training), Miniworld (four tasks). The number of pretraining tasks is **not varied**; generalisation beyond the pretraining distribution is called "preliminary".
  *For this program:* the argument in the belief design that the cross-entropy minimiser is the Bayes predictor, and its requirement that proposals not depend on the hidden world, are this theorem and its compliance condition, specialised. They are not new, and the design now says so. What the paper does not have: a finite pool of tasks with both the memorising and the generalising posterior computed exactly, a coverage sweep, or any score for cheaper mechanisms than the posterior.
- [**The Bayesian Geometry of Transformer Attention** — "Bayesian wind tunnels" (Agarwal, Dalal & Misra, Dec 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.22471v3) (main text complete; the HTML page carries no appendices). A wind tunnel is a task where "the analytic posterior is known exactly at each step" and "the hypothesis space is so large that memorization is computationally infeasible"; a fresh hypothesis is drawn for every training sequence. Tasks: bijection elimination (V = 20; the posterior is uniform over the outputs not yet seen), HMM filtering (five states, a fresh HMM per sequence), Bayesian regression, associative recall. Small transformers match the exact posterior (entropy error 0.007 bits on bijections, 0.049 on HMMs; KL ~10⁻⁴–10⁻³ nats; smooth degradation to 2.5× the training length); capacity-matched MLPs fail (1.85 and 0.40 bits); an LSTM fails on HMMs and recall. Mechanism: near-orthogonal keys in the first layer, elimination sharpening across depth, value vectors on a curve indexed by posterior entropy.
  *For this program:* the closest methodological relative — exact posterior as the yardstick — and it already reports that small transformers reach it in distribution. Three differences. Memorisation is excluded by construction (fresh hypotheses), so the coverage question is not asked. No cheaper mechanism is scored against the posterior; in the bijection task the exact posterior *is* a deletion (remove the outputs already seen), the case history Entry 87 showed carries no updating beyond a mask. There is no acting model and no checker: evidence arrives, it is not chosen.
- [**Meta-trained agents implement Bayes-optimal agents** (Mikulik, Delétang, McGrath, Genewein, Martic, Legg & Ortega, NeurIPS 2020)](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2010.11223) (complete with appendices). Memory-based meta-learners (LSTMs) trained on tasks drawn fresh from a prior match Bayes-optimal agents in behaviour (KL between predictive distributions; reward difference for bandits, against Gittins-index policies) and in structure (a learned map between internal states and sufficient statistics; the Bayes-optimal agent simulates the network well, the reverse less well). Ten prediction and four bandit tasks with conjugate priors; trained at 20 steps, near-optimal at 30. Caveat stated by the authors: the result holds when the optimal policy is in the search space and training converges to it. Untrained networks also score well on state similarity for bandits ("reservoir effect"), so state similarity alone can mislead. The theoretical step (log-loss over the task distribution is minimised by the Bayes mixture) is cited to earlier work (their reference 10; not read by me) and not proved in the paper. Finite task sets and off-distribution inputs other than longer episodes: not covered.
  *For this program:* in-distribution Bayes-optimality of a recurrent learner is a 2020 result. The belief experiment's in-distribution reading is therefore a gate (did training reach the known optimum), not a finding.

**What these three leave to the belief experiment** (as far as five searches by the reviewer and these readings go): a finite pool of hidden worlds with the memorising and generalising posteriors both exact (Raventós et al. have this for in-context regression, without actions); evidence that the model itself elicits from a checker; evidence patterns that no training world fits; and the cheapest non-Bayesian mechanisms scored beside the posterior.

### Prior work design review 2 found for the belief experiment (read 13 Nov 2026; history Entry 93)

Review 2 named four more papers. Three read through to the end of each page; the fourth not read.

- [**Competition Dynamics Shape Algorithmic Phases of In-Context Learning** (Park, Lubana, Pres & Tanaka, arXiv 2412.01003)](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01003v4) (complete, four chunks to Appendix L). Sequences from a *finite* set of N Markov chains (N from 2² to 2¹¹, ten states, length 512), a two-layer transformer trained online. Four exactly computed algorithms: two "retrieval" predictors with a discrete prior over the training chains (on unigram or bigram statistics) and two "inference" predictors that use only the context. **The model's next-token distribution is fitted as a combination of the algorithms' outputs** — "linear interpolation of algorithms": weights non-negative and summing to one, squared error in probability space, fitted per checkpoint and per N on 300 in-distribution chains; residual about 10⁻³ (0.005 at phase boundaries); irrelevant algorithms added to the set get weight near zero. Findings: retrieval at low N, inference at high N, a threshold near N = 2⁶; **the generalising solution is transient** — at N = 2⁶ the model moves from unigram to bigram inference and then, after about 10³ steps, toward bigram retrieval, which is better in distribution; a model four times wider needs sixteen times the diversity for the generalising solution to appear (the effect disappears at equal FLOPs). The fit predicts out-of-distribution loss slightly too high, so the decomposition is not complete.
  *For this program:* this is the belief design's "regression on the mechanisms" (with a simplex constraint) and its question 2, on passive sequences, published. The design must credit it, and question 2's upper end ("not memorising at 32 worlds") is a statement about training length and width as much as about coverage.
- [**In-Context Learning Strategies Emerge Rationally** (Wurgaft, Lubana et al., arXiv 2506.17859)](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17859v1) (complete with appendices). Three settings (balls and urns, linear regression, classification), each trained on D tasks, D from 2² to 2¹². A memorising predictor (discrete prior on the seen tasks) and a generalising predictor (the true prior), both in closed form. The model's output is a sigmoid-weighted mix of the two; the log-odds are a loss term that favours the memoriser and grows with training, minus a complexity term that favours the generaliser — three fitted parameters across eleven runs. Time to the loss of generalisation grows faster than linearly in D; wider MLPs memorise more. Only in-distribution behaviour is fitted; only two predictors are modelled.
  *For this program:* question 2's two references and its dependence on coverage and training time, already measured in three settings.
- [**Understanding the Training and Generalization of Pretrained Transformer for Sequential Decision Making** (Wang, Pan, Sun, Liu, Talluri, Chen & Li, arXiv 2405.14219)](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.14219v2) (complete with appendices). Supervised pretraining on optimal actions in bandits, pricing and newsvendor problems; histories from a fixed decision function. The Bayes-optimal decision given the history minimises the pretraining loss (their Proposition 4.1) and can have linear regret because it does not explore (4.3). The shift between pretraining histories and the model's own is repaired by mixing in model-generated histories after an initial phase (a third from the fixed function). Finite pools of 4, 8, 16 and 100 environments: the transformer's actions nearly match the Bayes-optimal decision, less well as the pool grows. Behaviour on environments outside the pool is not tested.
  *For this program:* the deferred "own-sample histories" of the belief design is this paper's algorithm; finite pools with an exact Bayes reference exist for decision making, in distribution.
- Goddard et al., *When can in-context learning generalize out of task distribution?* (arXiv 2506.05574): named by the reviewer from its abstract; **not read by me**.

**What is left to the belief experiment after these** (the reviewer's two searches and these readings): a carrier with *hard* evidence, on which "the evidence fits no training task" is a defined event (with soft likelihoods nothing is strictly out of support); counterfactual single-verdict probes at a fixed state; a rival that differs from the posterior only in how much it hedges, scored beside it. Question 2 as posed is a replication of a known effect on a new carrier.

### Streams of learned functions inside a loop, with learned recombination (13 Nov 2026; history Entry 94)

Read for the researcher's question: a middle way between designed primitives and one monolithic looped block — primitives as separate learned streams inside the loop, recombined by something like an expert router. ‡ = partial read.

- [**Rahaman, Gondal, Joshi, Gehler, Bengio, Locatello & Schölkopf (NeurIPS 2021), *Dynamic Inference with Neural Interpreters*.**](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.06399) (complete, three chunks with appendices). **The closest existing form of the idea.** A self-attention network factorised into *functions*: each function is a learned pair (signature, code); a shared "interpreter" (attention and MLP layers whose linear maps are modulated by the code) processes the tokens once per function, **in parallel streams**; a learned type vector per token is matched to the signatures through a truncated kernel, giving a soft, sparse routing weight; the streams' outputs are **summed, weighted by that compatibility**; the whole thing is **applied recurrently** ("function iterations" add depth without parameters). Trained end to end. Results: on par with a ViT in distribution with a third of the parameters (0.6M against 1.8M); on Raven-style matrices (PGM) best test score on four of six generalisation splits, by small margins (extrapolation 19.4 against 16.4; triples 20.0 against 15.9; neutral 77.0 against 72.7); a model trained with eight iterations keeps 70–80 % of its accuracy at half of them; on a toy task, retuning only the routing recovers R² 0.986 against 0.995 for retuning everything (their evidence that the functions are recomposable); functions can be added after training. Fragilities: signatures are best kept fixed at initialisation; RAdam failed and a second-order optimiser was used; single-function runs mostly fail; learned against fixed routing is compared only by t-SNE. Stated limits: no top-down processing, no higher-order functions.
- [**Lamb, He, Goyal, Ke, Liao, Ravanelli & Bengio (2021), *Transformers with Competitive Ensembles of Independent Mechanisms* (TIM).**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00336v1)‡ (abstract) Each layer's hidden state *and parameters* are split into separate mechanisms that exchange information only through attention, with a competition step; evaluated on BERT, an image transformer and speech enhancement; "semantically meaningful specialization" claimed. Not looped. Its recurrent ancestor is Recurrent Independent Mechanisms (Goyal et al. 2019; **from memory, not re-read**).
- [***Looped Language Models: A Survey and Meta-Analysis* (2026)**](https://www.preprints.org/frontend/manuscript/07176648ad0d1a09ab839b7c90c888b1/download_pub)‡ (first 100k of 419k characters). Experts inside a shared block now exist at scale: a 20B looped expert model (Loopie), a 9B one (LoopMoE), per-loop routers with shared experts and the reverse, token-level routers over recursion depth, routing each token through shared modules in any order, a looped sublayer in parallel with a once-applied one. Its design axes: what is looped, how state is carried, how much is shared, how many loops. Open, by its account: which components must stay untied; routers over layer programs recover little of what oracle searches show is available. It does not discuss specialisation of experts within a loop.
- [***Towards Looped Models Done Right* (2026, arXiv 2610.23036)**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23036) (complete). Untied prelude, tied core, untied coda, with the prelude output re-injected. In the expert variant, expert selection differs across loop iterations and freezing the routes to the first iteration's lowers accuracy on six tasks; **a two-state high/low hierarchy through a shared module "gave no consistent benefit"**.
- **Where this leaves the idea.** The architecture — learned functions as parallel streams, soft learned recombination, recurrence — is Neural Interpreters, and experts routed inside loops are in production-scale models. What these do not have is a ground truth for what a stream *should* be: evidence that streams are reusable primitives is a toy retuning experiment and embedding plots. Not found: streams compared against known primitive operations of the task, or recombination tested on combinations of operations held out of training with a matched monolithic loop as control.

- [***Looping Beyond Twice: A Scalable Recipe for Looped Mixture-of-Experts* (2026, arXiv 2610.01153)**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.01153) (complete; some table cells garbled). Attention and experts shared across loops, **a separate router per layer for each loop**, still routing per token. With one shared router the expert assignments were nearly identical on every loop ("expert selection collapse"); per-loop routers made them differ, and removing them cost about a point of perplexity (18.62 → 19.83, one ablation at 350M, one checkpoint). Read for the researcher's follow-up question (routing per loop, not per token; history Entry 95): routing that *depends on the loop index* exists at scale; a router that chooses **one** learned block per loop for the whole state was not found in looped transformers (one search and the survey's first quarter), and is the setting of Routing Networks and Modular Networks (above, 6 Nov section) and of this program's rungs 1–3.

### How published work trains experts inside loops stably (read 15 Nov 2026; history Entry 104)

Read for the architecture search on streams gated inside one looped block (`docs/moe_in_loops.md`). All pages read to the end unless marked ‡.

- [**Csordás, Irie & Schmidhuber (EMNLP Findings 2023), *Approximating Two-Layer Feedforward Networks for Efficient Transformers* (σ-MoE).**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.10837) (complete; one passage of §3.3 garbled). Selection scores are **sigmoids**, top-K of them are taken **before** the experts are computed, and the selected scores are used **without renormalising**. The argument: "softmax introduces competition between experts", and renormalising after top-K (equivalently, top-K before the softmax) "can also encourage expert collapsing" — when one score rises the others fall automatically. Two regularisers: an entropy bonus on the *batch-mean* softmax of the selection logits (γ = 1e-3; pushes usage to be balanced on average, not per token) and **expert dropout** (a Bernoulli mask on the scores, rate 0.05–0.2, no rescaling) so that "the less popular ones take over". Selector rows are initialised with equal norms. Ablations (perplexity, 238M model): σ-MoE 10.37; softmax renormalised 11.27; softmax not renormalised 10.54; no regularisation 10.41; standard init 10.59; Switch (top-1) 11.24. Collapse is reported for Switch and for renormalised softmax; "removing all regularisation does not entail catastrophic collapse". More active experts help with diminishing returns (K=1: 12.26, K=2: 11.90, K=4: 11.59, K=8: 11.58 on the small model). *Nothing in the paper says how the selector learns about an expert it did not pick* — the exploration is whatever expert dropout provides.
- [**Csordás, Irie, Schmidhuber, Potts & Manning (NeurIPS 2024), *MoEUT: Mixture-of-Experts Universal Transformers*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16039v1) (complete). A shared-layer language model that beats a parameter-matched dense transformer slightly (18.30 against 18.97 perplexity at 44M). Three ingredients. (1) σ-MoE feedforward and expert-routed value/output projections in attention; entropy regulariser computed **per sequence**; **no expert dropout**. (2) **Layer grouping**: a group of G = 2–4 *different* layers is the unit that repeats (ABAB, not AABB — "almost as bad as not doing grouping at all"); a single shared layer is worse. (3) **Peri-layernorm**: no layernorm on the main path at all; layernorm only "before linear layers followed directly by a sigmoid or softmax" — attention queries/keys, the expert selectors, the classifier. The reason given is specific to tied weights: with pre-norm the residual grows with depth and a tied layer cannot scale its output per depth; post-norm renormalises the whole residual each layer and limits gradient flow. The router is tied across repeats and is **not told the repeat index**; selection nevertheless changes with depth (some experts used only in the last layer, most over a contiguous range), driven by the token's state. Gradient clipping 0.1–0.25.
- [**Tan, Shen, Chen, Courville & Gan (EMNLP 2023), *Sparse Universal Transformer*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.07096v1) (complete). Shared block with sparse experts for attention and feedforward, selected before they are computed, plus halting. Collapse is fought with a **mutual-information loss**: maximise the entropy of the batch-marginal expert distribution and minimise the per-input conditional entropy (balanced on average, decisive per input). Halting is **stick-breaking** (α_l = α̂_l ∏_{l'<l}(1 − α̂_{l'})) with the output a mixture of the states at each halt, chosen because the Universal Transformer's convex gate "can lead to vanishingly small gradients"; halted positions are routed to a **no-op expert**. The gate reads the hidden state; no repeat index. Removing the mutual-information loss costs 0.3 BLEU (29.2 → 28.9). In MoEUT's comparison this model is "significantly worse" than both MoEUT and the dense baseline (different codebase, per MoEUT's own caveat).
- [**Zoph, Bello, Kumar, Du, Huang, Dean, Shazeer & Fedus (2022), *ST-MoE: Designing Stable and Transferable Sparse Expert Models*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.08906) (complete, 38 pages). Sparse models diverge more often than dense ones; the cause they identify is round-off in the router's exponentials. **Router z-loss** (mean over tokens of (log Σ_j e^{x_j})², coefficient 1e-3) keeps selection logits small: 3/3 stable runs *and* better quality, against 4/6 for the baseline. Every other stabiliser bought stability with quality (dropout, input jitter, removing multiplicative interactions; update clipping was "catastrophic"). Load-balancing loss α·N·Σ_i f_i P_i with α = 1e-2 (f_i the dispatched fraction, not differentiable; P_i the mean router probability). Recommends top-2. **Negative results:** pre-training noise — "dropout, dropping whole experts, an entropy loss on the router" — hurt or did not help; telling the router expert positions did not help.
- [**Panda et al. (NeurIPS 2025), *Dense Backpropagation Improves Training for Sparse Mixture-of-Experts* (Default MoE).**](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12463v3) (complete; appendix findings are figures only). States the problem this program met in Entry 103: "the router does not receive a gradient update from experts that it does not activate." Fix: for each unselected expert substitute a **default vector** — a running mean of that expert's past outputs — in the forward sum, so the router's gradient has a term for every expert without running any more of them. Reaches a target perplexity with 9 % fewer tokens, tolerates a higher learning rate than top-K, overhead under 2 % throughput. "Takes longer to warm up for sparser models"; zero-initialised defaults give no signal early. The default is input-independent: it is a good stand-in only when an expert's output varies little across inputs.
- [**Nie et al. (2021), *EvoMoE: An Evolutional Mixture-of-Experts Training Framework via Dense-To-Sparse Gate*.**](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.14397) (complete, two chunks; found after round B2 of the search, when a dense start turned out to be what worked). **Prior art for "start dense, then go sparse".** Diagnosis of sparse training from scratch: with gate and experts both random, "most tokens keep concentrating" on one expert at first and the selection is unstable. Method: (1) train one shared expert, then diversify copies by masking; (2) a dense-to-sparse gate — softmax with temperature annealed from 2.0 to 0.3, experts kept when their score exceeds 0.001, scores not renormalised, then top-1. The dense part is 7.5–10 % of training (10k of 100k steps on GLUE, 15k of 200k for language modelling). Beats Switch, BASE, Hash and StableMoE (perplexity 12.24 against 13.12 for Switch); the ablation credits the dense-to-sparse gate (removing it costs about 1 BLEU), not the shared-expert stage (removing it changes nothing). Reasons given: dense routing "allows us to get training feedback from all diverse experts"; no formal account, no ablation of the dense phase's length, schedule fixed in advance.
- Read earlier and used here: [*Looping Beyond Twice*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.01153) (a router shared across loops repeats the same selection on every loop; one router per loop fixes it; sigmoid routers; some always-on shared experts beside the routed ones; re-injection and residual scales that shrink with loop count) and [*Towards Looped Models Done Right*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23036) (untied prelude and coda around a tied core; routes differ across iterations and freezing them hurts).

- **Two older relatives that the independent review named and I had not placed** (read 15 Nov, after the rounds). [**Hu, Andreas, Darrell & Saenko (ECCV 2018), *Explainable Neural Computation via Stack Neural Module Networks***](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1807.08556) (main text complete; the last fifth of the page is references). **The closest existing form of the gate in `moe_in_loops.md`.** A controller attends over the question's words at every step and emits (i) a "textual parameter" — the attention-weighted word states — and (ii) **softmax weights over a set of modules**; **all modules run at every step and their results are averaged by those weights**; trained end to end with no supervision of the layout (93.0 % on CLEVR against 96.6 % with expert layouts; earlier module networks reach 69 % without). The progress signal is a clock: one linear transform of the question *per time step*, "not shared across different time steps", plus the previous textual parameter. The soft weights become nearly one-hot on their own (entropy 0.082, about 98 % on one module), and replacing them by the arg-max at test time costs under four points. Differences from the candidate: modules are hand-designed (find, transform, and, or, filter, …) and act on attention maps through a stack; every module is always computed in training; the controller is not tied across steps; no test of more steps than trained. [**Hudson & Manning (ICLR 2018), *Compositional Attention Networks for Machine Reasoning* (MAC)**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.03067v2.pdf) (complete with appendices). One recurrent cell with a control state and a memory state; the control attends over the question's words at each step, again through a per-step transform q_i = W_i q + b_i; **no selection among alternative operations** — "one universal cell" instead of an inventory of modules. Tying the cell across its 12 steps helps (98.9 against 97.8 on CLEVR; 84.5 against 67.5 on a tenth of the data); using the whole question as the control instead of attention over it costs 18 points.

**What the literature agrees on, and where it is silent.**

| Question | What is done | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Score function | Sigmoid, not softmax; no renormalisation after top-K | σ-MoE ablations; adopted by MoEUT and Looping Beyond Twice |
| Sparsity | Top-K *before* computing experts, K ≥ 2 | σ-MoE (K=1 worst), ST-MoE (top-2) |
| Collapse | Balance the batch-average usage (entropy / mutual information / load loss); never per input | σ-MoE, SUT, ST-MoE |
| Exploration | Expert dropout (σ-MoE: helps) — but ST-MoE: dropping experts hurt | **the two disagree** |
| Router gradient for unrun experts | Stand-in outputs (Default MoE); or run everything for the first tenth of training, then top-1 (EvoMoE) | one paper each |
| Numerical stability | Keep selection logits small (z-loss); normalise the *selector's input*, not the main path (peri-layernorm) | ST-MoE, MoEUT |
| Which pass am I in | Either nothing (MoEUT, SUT: the state says) or one router per loop (Looping Beyond Twice: a shared router collapses) | **the two disagree**; neither tests a task whose instruction changes per pass |
| Depth growth | Residual and re-injection scales shrink with loop count; grouping of two or more distinct layers | Looping Beyond Twice, MoEUT |

Not found in the expert papers above: routing *per loop* rather than per token; a task with a known ground truth for which expert should run on which pass; an account of how a tied router comes to select differently on different passes when it starts out selecting identically. Routing one module per step for the whole state *is* found outside them — Routing Networks and Modular Networks (above, Entry 95) and Stack-NMN — so the per-loop gate is not new; what those lack is the sparse execution, a tied controller, and learned rather than designed modules in one place.

### Has context-dependent weight lookup been done? (read 18 Nov 2026; history Entry 118)

The researcher's question after stating the line's object (Entry 117): weights assembled per context by attention over a bank of learned pieces — "compositionally built mixtures of experts with effectively infinite experts (through unique composition) and program lookup … working directly on weights. Has this work been done by anyone already?" Read depth is given per entry: (complete), (abstract), or the share of the page read. A search of one session, not a systematic review.

**A. A bank of learned units retrieved by key; composition by choosing a subset.**
- [**He (2024), *Mixture of A Million Experts* (PEER).**](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04153v1) (complete, two chunks). **The unit-grain lookup of `weight_lookup.md` §2–4 exists.** An expert is one hidden neuron, e_i(x) = σ(u_iᵀx) v_i; over a million of them; a query network on the layer input, learned product keys, top-k, h heads; output Σ g_i e_i(x) with router scores g. At equal compute on C4, perplexity 16.45 against 18.31 dense, 17.12 for a 128-expert mixture, 17.36 for product-key memory. Stated aim: "decoupling computational cost from parameter count". Query is the current token's state only; keys are stored; not looped; no test of held-out combinations; lifelong learning is motivation, not experiment.
- [**Lample et al. (NeurIPS 2019), *Large Memory Layers with Product Keys*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.05242) (complete). The same retrieval with fixed value vectors in place of neurons: "keys and values … are free embedding vectors"; replaces the feed-forward block in some layers; a 12-layer model with one memory beats a 24-layer one (13.7 against 14.0).
- [**Wang et al. (ICLR 2025), *TokenFormer*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.23168v2) (complete). Every linear projection replaced by attention from the input token to learned key and value "parameter tokens", output = Θ(X K_Pᵀ) V_P; its own appendix frames this as a reformulated two-layer network; motivation is growing a model by appending tokens. Query is the token's own state.
- [**Zhao et al. (2025), *Each Rank Could be an Expert* (SMoRA).**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15103v1) (abstract). Each rank-one component of a LoRA is an expert, activated dynamically; finer partitions do better at equal budgets.
- [**Geva et al. (EMNLP 2021), *Transformer Feed-Forward Layers Are Key-Value Memories*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.14913v2) (abstract). The reading of a static feed-forward layer used in Entry 117: keys detect input patterns, values write, "the output of a feed-forward layer is a composition of its memories".

**B. Weights composed from learned modules, conditioned on context.**
- [**Schug et al. (ICLR 2025), *Attention as a Hypernetwork*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.05816v4) (complete, two chunks). **Answers "does query–key compatibility work on weights": standard multi-head attention already does it.** For a query–key pair the scores across heads are a latent code, and the value map applied is W_{q,k} = Σ_h a_{h,q,k} W^out_h W^value_h — a linear hypernetwork over learned head matrices. Making the generated value network nonlinear and normalising across heads (HYLA) raises accuracy on held-out combinations (fuzzy logic 81 against 63 R²; symbolic Raven 69 against 57 %); the code clusters by the operation being performed and decodes it on held-out tasks. A mixture-of-experts feed-forward gave gains "similar to other increases of model capacity". Nothing generalises to unseen *terms*.
- [**Schug et al. (ICLR 2024), *Discovering modular solutions that generalize compositionally*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.15001v2) (main text; the appendix not read; also noted under Entry 82). W = Σ_m z^(m) Θ^(m) with the code z inferred from a task's demonstrations. **Theory for exactly the weight-space average that Entry 115 calls inexact:** when the teacher has this form, a student that fits the training tasks recovers the modules up to a linear map and generalises to unseen combinations — if every module appears in training, the training combinations are connected, and the student is **not overparameterised**. With spare modules the student "could use one module per task"; modular models "often collapse to the monolithic solution".
- [**Ponti et al. (2022), *Combining Modular Skills in Multitask Learning* (Polytropon).**](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.13914) (complete). A task is a learned binary row over a skill inventory; its weights are the base plus the *average* of the active skills' low-rank or sparse deltas. Conditioned on task identity, not on input.
- [**Muqeeth et al. (2023), *Soft Merging of Experts with Adaptive Routing* (SMEAR).**](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03745v2) (first 94 %). Router-weighted **average of expert parameters**, one merged expert applied; one routing decision per example. Matches output ensembling (81.6 against 81.7 on T5-GLUE) at about one expert's cost and beats discrete routing trained with gradient estimators. Experts are adapters on a frozen backbone, randomly initialised and not aligned; permutation-aware merging is left as future work. Per-token routing would cost as much as ensembling.
- [**Ha, Dai & Le (2016), *HyperNetworks*.**](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1609.09106) (complete). Weights of a recurrent network regenerated at every step from input and hidden state. Generating full matrices was impractical, so the hypernetwork outputs a vector that **scales the rows** of a static matrix — a multiplicative gate per unit.
- [**Abdalla et al. (2025), *Zhyper*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19733v2) (complete) and [**Jaber & Jaber (2026), *Ouroboros*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02051v1) (complete). ΔW = B diag(z) A: context sets coefficients over a shared bank of rank-one terms. Zhyper: z from a task or culture description, per prompt. **Ouroboros does this inside a looped block**, z from the mean hidden state and the step index, bank frozen (SVD of removed layers). Against a static per-step table it wins on training data (5.08 against 6.52 at one loop, 5.08 against 5.12 at eight), **loses to the unlooped baseline on held-out text** (5.96 against 5.69), and gains nothing from more loops.

**C. Weights written from the context (fast weights).**
- [**Schlag, Irie & Schmidhuber (ICML 2021), *Linear Transformers Are Secretly Fast Weight Programmers*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2102.11174v3) (first 78 %). W^(i) = W^(i−1) + v^(i) ⊗ φ(k^(i)), read as W φ(q): attention is a weight matrix built from the context as a sum of outer products. "A traditional slow net with slow weights continually changes or reprograms the fast weights of a fast net" (Schmidhuber 1991–93). Capacity is limited by key dimension; a delta-rule write fixes overwriting. Slow weights are never selected or composed by context.
- [**Irie et al. (ICML 2022), *A Modern Self-Referential Weight Matrix*.**](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2202.05780) (first 55 %). One matrix emits its own key, query and learning rate and updates itself by a rank-one delta rule; "the fast WM is completely context-dependent".

**D. Learned functions bound to items of the context by attention.**
- [**Goyal, Didolkar et al. (NeurIPS 2021), *Neural Production Systems*.**](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.01937) (first 40 %; the main text). N rules, each "a learned rule embedding vector" with its own MLP; each entity slot queries the rule embeddings, a hard choice picks a rule and a second slot, and the rule's MLP updates the slot. **The per-item binding that recombined in Entry 108, with stored keys.** Extrapolates from 4 to 6–8 objects; no held-out-combination test. With Neural Interpreters (Entry 94) this is prior art for binding functions to context items.
- [**Bakermans et al. (2025), *Compositional Meta-Learning Through Probabilistic Task Inference*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01858) (complete). Modules selected per step by *inference*: a particle filter scores module choices by the likelihood of the observed outputs, no parameter updates. The relative of the evidence-keyed lookup of Entry 113. Names the bootstrap problem: "simultaneous learning of modules and gating risks training instability and local minima due to a 'chicken-and-egg' problem".

**E. Experts inside loops, 2026.**
- [**Chen et al. (2026), *LoopMoE*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04438) (complete, two chunks) — a shared block of expert layers run four times between untied layers; routing recomputed per pass from the token's state; conditioning on the pass through the normalisation. Beats an unlooped mixture at equal compute (44.0 against 41.2 average at the larger scale). One seed. Middle passes use nearly the same experts; the last pass switches.
- [**Jaggi (2026), *Tying the Loop*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.16825) (complete). Ties only the expert weights across consecutive layers, keeping attention and routers per layer: half the parameters for 0.05–0.07 loss; tying everything costs about 0.2.
- [**Zeitoun, Torroba-Hennigen & Kim (2026), *Hyperloop Transformers*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21254v2) (complete, two chunks). Not a hypernetwork: a looped block whose residual streams are mixed at each loop boundary by a per-token, per-loop diagonal. Beats per-loop LoRA on the block (14.40 against 14.77–14.85).

**F. Where keys come from, and why averaging weights is fragile.**
- [**Ahrac, Hochwald & Geva (2026), *Routers Learn the Geometry of Their Experts*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12476) (complete). A router's vector and its expert's input weights "receive gradients along the same input direction, differing only in scalar coefficients", so stored keys come to align with the experts' read weights; a load-balancing loss blurs this. A router built from running centroids of routed inputs comes within 2.6 % of a learned one. Does not derive keys from the expert's weights.
- [**Mohamud, Wagner & Ravanelli (2026), *Self-Routing*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00421v1) (complete). Router removed: N coordinates of the hidden state are the logits. Competitive with a learned router.
- [**Ainsworth, Hayase & Srinivasa (ICLR 2023), *Git Re-Basin*.**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.04836v6) (abstract) and [**Pfeiffer et al. (2023), *Modular Deep Learning*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.11529v1) (first third). Independently trained networks must have their hidden units permuted into alignment before weights can be merged. The survey's statement of Entry 115's rule: averaging parameters and averaging outputs "are equivalent if the functions are linear", not otherwise; and its list of what goes wrong with learned routing — under-constrained decompositions, module collapse, early instability.

**What exists, and what was not found.**

- [**Wiedemer, Mayilvahanan, Bethge & Brendel (NeurIPS 2023), *Compositional Generalization from First Principles*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.05596v1) (first 36 %: framework, definitions, Theorem 1, the motivating experiment). Data f(z) = C(φ₁(z₁), …, φ_K(z_K)). If the model uses the **same, known composition function C**, every component value is seen (compositional support) and the support pins down C's derivatives (sufficient support), then matching the training distribution implies matching on unseen combinations. Nothing is proved when C is learned; a monolithic model fits and does not generalise.
- [**Lachapelle, Mahajan, Mitliagkas & Lacoste-Julien (NeurIPS 2023), *Additive Decoders for Latent Variables Identification and Cartesian-Product Extrapolation*.**](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2307.02598) (first 10 %: statements only). - [**Zhao et al. (2024), *Sparse Mixture-of-Experts for Compositional Generalization*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.13964) (complete). Does not analyse what the gate leaves undetermined, pooled against per-item gating, or interaction terms.

### A looped bank standing in for a deep network of distinct layers: what exists (read 23 Nov 2026; history Entry 126)

Read before designing a stage for the idea as the researcher corrected it (Entries 123–125): one bank of units; a block composed from it per pass, by context and state; the looped model standing in for a deep network whose layers are all different; fewer stored units than those layers add up to. Twenty-one papers; read depth per entry. Papers already in these notes (MoEUT, Sparse Universal Transformer, Tying the Loop, Mixture of Universal Experts, Looping Beyond Twice, LoopMoE, PEER, Transformer Layers as Painters) are cited, not repeated.

**1. Can a tied block stand in for distinct layers at all? (theory)**
- [**Bai, Kolter & Koltun (NeurIPS 2019), *Deep Equilibrium Models*.**](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1909.01377) (main text first third; Appendices B and C). **Theorem 3:** any L-layer network z⁽ⁱ⁺¹⁾ = σ⁽ⁱ⁾(W⁽ⁱ⁾z⁽ⁱ⁾ + b⁽ⁱ⁾) equals a weight-tied, input-injected network of the same depth, z̃⁽ⁱ⁺¹⁾ = σ(W_z z̃⁽ⁱ⁾ + W_x x + b̃): the state has one slot per layer, W_z carries the layers' matrices on its block subdiagonal, each application shifts activations one slot down; width is the sum of the layers' widths. Also: stacking equilibrium models adds no representational power. "Infinite depth" there means the fixed point of a tied network, not variety of layers.
  *For this program:* the tied block that stands in for L distinct layers always exists at L times the width, with no selection mechanism — the state's slot is the program counter. A bank lookup is that wide block with only the current slot's units evaluated. So what a bank can add is (a) compute, and (b) fewer than L·w units when layers share units. This is the exact reference for any stage: the no-sharing bank has L·w units.
- [**Xu & Sato (ICML 2025), *On Expressive Power of Looped Transformers*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01405v5) (first 78 %). Approximation rate of a looped transformer in the number of loops carries two extra continuity terms that untied transformers do not have, "specific to the looped architecture", because "weight-tied feed-forward networks cannot map accurately": one fixed block cannot hold what distinct steps must memorise. A per-loop scaling vector on the feed-forward output, η(t) ⊙ FF(X), restores exact memorisation (an existence construction). In practice the scalings come from a timestep embedding through a small network. In-context learning: untied 8.6e-3, looped 1.4e-2, looped with timestep encoding 1.7e-3; WikiText-103 perplexity 19.6 untied, 28.2 looped, 22.4 with the encoding.
  *For this program:* something must change from pass to pass, and the minimal published form of it is a diagonal modulation indexed by the pass.

**2. Layers as selections or combinations from one shared bank — static, per layer.**
- [**Savarese & Maire (ICLR 2019), *Learning Implicitly Recurrent CNNs Through Parameter Sharing*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.09701) (complete). W⁽ⁱ⁾ = Σⱼ αⱼ⁽ⁱ⁾ T⁽ʲ⁾: every layer a learned linear combination of a global bank of templates, fewer templates than layers. "Having k as a free parameter decouples the number of parameters in network from its depth." One third of the parameters at near-baseline error (CIFAR-10 4.01 against 4.0); at equal parameters slightly better. **Layers' coefficient vectors come out nearly equal in groups, and those layers can be folded into loops** (six layers into a two-layer loop for 0.02 % error). Whole filter banks, coefficients static.
  *For this program:* the vision with static coefficients and whole-layer templates, in 2019. Averaging whole weight tensors works here because the templates are trained under the averaging.
- [**Wang et al. (ICLR 2025), *Basis Sharing*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03765v1) (complete). Post hoc on trained language models: the matrices of n layers written over one shared basis with per-layer coefficients. Works for W_Q, W_K, W_V, W_up, W_gate; **not** for W_O or W_down ("should not be applied"). Adjacent pairs by default; four or five layers per basis at low compression; perplexity 7.74 against 7.94 for per-layer factorisation at 20 % compression.
  *For this program:* in trained models the matrices that read the residual stream share across neighbouring layers and the two that write to it do not. With Entry 115's rule (the write side is where mixing is exact) this points to one shape: shared read rows, write coefficients chosen per layer or per context. My inference, not the paper's.
- [**Kopiczko, Blankevoort & Asano (ICLR 2024), *VeRA*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.11454) (complete). ΔW = Λ_b B Λ_d A with one frozen random pair (A, B) shared by all layers and trained per-layer diagonal vectors: per-layer reweighting of one shared set of rank-one terms. GLUE average 87.8 against LoRA's 87.8 at a thirteenth of the parameters (RoBERTa-large); weaker on some tasks.
- [**Bae et al. (ICLR 2025), *Relaxed Recursive Transformers*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.20672v2) (first 54 %). A looped block plus per-depth low-rank modules; rank is a dial from tied to untied. Tying costs because "each model layer ends up having to serve multiple roles associated with different depths". Gemma: tied at half the parameters 51.7 (a reduced-size model with the same count: 38.2); rank 512 reaches 58.4 against 58.6 for the full model, at 1.60B against 1.99B parameters.
  *For this program:* a measured sharing curve for real layers. Most of what layers do is shared; the last points cost most of the parameters back.
- [**López García-Arias et al. (2021), *Hidden-Fold Networks*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.12330) (complete). Folded ResNet stages with frozen random weights and one learned mask shared by every iteration; only the normalisation parameters differ per iteration. CIFAR-100 77.21 against 78.01 at a fifth of the parameters. Not a different sub-network per iteration.

**3. A tied block made to act differently per pass — dynamic.**
- [**Lee, Biloki, Hu & May (NeurIPS 2026), *Sparse Layers are Critical to Scaling Looped Language Models*.**](https://huggingface.co/papers/2605.09165.md) (the paper page's full summary; not the PDF). Four arms at equal active parameters and compute: untied dense, looped dense, untied mixture of experts, looped mixture of experts. **Dense looped models scale worse than untied; looped mixtures do not** (scaling exponent 0.077 against 0.076 for untied dense; downstream 39.6 looped mixture, 38.7 untied dense, 37.4 looped dense, with 216M against 246M stored parameters). The stated reason is routing divergence: on the second pass 25–53 % of tokens get a completely disjoint set of experts and 4–14 % the same set. Routing statistics, no causal test. Eight experts, two active; up to 305M active parameters.
  *For this program:* the nearest published support for the idea — a tied block becomes distinct layers through which experts fire — at expert grain, routed by the token's state, at two loops. It contradicts Looping Beyond Twice, where a router shared across loops repeated one selection.
- [**Hegazy, Alanwar & Elhoushi (2026), *RecurrentGPT / Gated Recurrent Transformers*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15062v2) (first 29 %: method and main table). A shared core between untied first and last blocks; per pass an elementwise gate computed from the state and the first block's output (no pass index) blends old state and block output. "A single weight tensor [behaves] as multiple specialized layers." 36M matches a 124M dense model at small scale (3.14 against 3.15), trails at medium (2.89 against 2.84). Gate bias +4 at the start, noise on state and gate.
- [**Ciccone, Taati & Jeddi (2026), *LoopFormer*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11451v1) (complete). Per-loop conditioning of normalisation scales and branch gates on time and step size, zero-initialised. At 24 layers of compute: untied 9.49 perplexity with eight times the parameters; looped 10.91; looped with loop-index conditioning 10.38; LoopFormer 10.28.
- [**Ekbote et al. (Meta, 2026), *How to Loop Your Transformer*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40325) (first 39 %). Looping the full stack is best per parameter; "gains eventually saturate, reverse, or become unstable". Per-loop low-rank specialisation helps from four loops up, and an untied model of doubled depth still beats it at matched compute. Looping attention and feed-forward independently "degrades sharply". Mixtures of experts not evaluated.
- [**Berges et al. (ICML 2025), *Memory Layers at Scale*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.09764v2) (complete). Key–value lookup of learned vectors by the token's state, top-k, product keys; **one pool of memory shared by the three layers that use it**. More layers reading the shared pool help up to three, then hurt. Beats dense models of twice the compute on factual tasks; needed gating and query–key normalisation to train large memories stably.
  *For this program:* a bank shared across depth at the grain of single value vectors exists at scale; its query is the token's state.
- Also relevant, in these notes already: PEER (single-neuron experts by key; one bank per layer); Tying the Loop (attention is what keeps tied expert layers distinct); Mixture of Universal Experts (every depth reaching every expert was the worst ablation); MoEUT.

**4. Do a few units per context suffice, and do layers share features? (evidence from trained models)**
- [**Liu et al. (ICML 2023), *Deja Vu: Contextual Sparsity*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.17157) (first 67 %). In trained language models, "small, input-dependent sets of attention heads and MLP parameters" reproduce the dense output: up to 95 % of feed-forward units and 80 % of heads can be skipped for a given token; a small classifier on the previous layer's state predicts the set; no accuracy loss to 75 % sparsity. Models not retrained. Works because the residual stream changes slowly from layer to layer (cosine about 0.99).
- [**Belcak & Wattenhofer (2023), *Exponentially Faster Language Modelling*.**](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.10770) (complete). Trained from scratch with conditional execution of single units along a tree: 12 of 4,095 units per layer at inference, GLUE average on par with BERT-base except CoLA (35.1 against 51.3). Selection by the token's own state.
- [**Lindsey et al. (Anthropic, 2024), *Sparse Crosscoders*.**](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2024/crosscoders/index.html) (complete; a research note). One sparse dictionary reading and writing all layers "substantially outperform[s]" per-layer dictionaries at a fixed total number of features; "many features [have] substantial norm across most or even all layers", though most peak at one layer and directions drift.
  *For this program:* evidence that the layers of a trained network lie near the span of fewer shared features than the layers taken separately — the condition under which one bank can stand in for many layers (Entry 124).

**5. Does a looped model do different things per pass, and what tells it which? Unseen orders.**
- [**Wu, Hu & Zhang (2026), *Shared Weights, Selected Computations*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.39892) (first 93 %). "Sharing weights does not guarantee the same algorithm": loops advance by different amounts; a linear map on the state at the loop boundary, block frozen, switches which transition the block performs, and attention patterns carry the switch (patching them moves answers from 0.1 % to 65.9 %). The state routes a shared executor. Beyond trained loops: 97.4 % at loops 9–16, 35.1 % at 17–32.
- [**Freiberger et al. (2024), *LayerShuffle*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04513v2) (complete). Vision transformers trained with the layer order permuted every batch run in arbitrary orders at 63 % (a normally trained model: 0.13 %), for 5–7 points in the original order. A layer can tell its position from its input (99.99 %), and telling it explicitly barely helps; layers contribute when their input is in distribution and otherwise pass it on.
  *For this program:* blocks trained under varied sequences work in sequences never seen, and need no index. The price is paid in the best case.
- [**Chen (2026), *Thinking Deeper, Not Longer*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.21676v1) (first 92 %). One block, a learned depth embedding per step, an identity-biased gate, LayerScale at 1e-4, random depth in training, loss on the last step only. Holds to 1.6–1.75 times the trained composition depth. No ablation of the stabilisers; no new combinations held out.
- [**Kapl et al. (2026), *From Growing to Looping*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16490v1) (first half). At equal unique parameters looped models beat untied ones on reasoning primitives (31.3 against 21.6 at 360M); at equal depth they have worse likelihood; "looping more than about two times tends to hurt"; untied first and last layers restore robustness.
- [**Sesodia, Torr & Schroeder de Witt (NeurIPS 2026), *Inside the Loop*.**](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/150577) (abstract). A four-layer block looped eight times solves permutation traversal on every seed where an untied model of the same depth never does without a lower rate or intermediate supervision.
- [**Yang et al. (2026), *LoopCoder-v2*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18023) (first 45 %). At 7B: two loops beat no loop broadly; three or more regress.

**What exists, and what was not found.**

| Piece of the idea | Status |
|---|---|
| A tied block can represent any network of distinct layers | Theorem, at the sum of the layers' widths, with the state as program counter (Bai et al.) |
| A tied block is limited unless something changes per pass | Theorem and fix by per-pass diagonal scaling (Xu & Sato) |
| Layers as combinations of one bank, static per layer | Done: templates (Savarese & Maire), shared bases (Basis Sharing), shared rank-one bank with per-layer scalings (VeRA), tied block plus per-depth low rank (Relaxed Recursive) |
| A tied block acting as distinct layers through which experts fire, chosen from the state | Reported at scale with routing statistics (Sparse Layers are Critical); also state-conditioned gates (RecurrentGPT) |
| A bank shared across depth at the grain of single stored vectors | Done at scale with value vectors (Memory Layers); single-unit experts exist per layer (PEER) |
| A few units per context suffice; layers share features | Measured post hoc in trained models (Deja Vu; crosscoders); trainable from scratch at unit grain (UltraFastBERT) |
| Sequences of blocks never seen in training work | Shown for layer order when trained with shuffling (LayerShuffle) |
| **Units (read row with write column) from one bank shared across all passes, chosen by context items and state, without a pass index** | **Not found** as one system |
| **Unit configurations never used together in training, tested as such** | **Not found**; routing papers report statistics on seen data |
| **How small the bank can be against the untied network as a function of how much the layers share, with exact references** | **Not found**; the measured curves are for real models at one sharing level (Relaxed Recursive, Tying the Loop) |

**What the stage has to take from this.**
1. The reference is exact: a tied block of the summed width solves any teacher of distinct layers (Theorem 3). A bank arm must be compared with a static tied block of the *same total width*, which isolates what selection adds (compute), as well as with an untied network.
2. The cheap baselines are strong and must be arms: a tied block with a per-pass diagonal scaling or its own normalisation per pass; a tied block with per-pass low-rank terms; a state-conditioned gate.
3. Read and write sides should be separate dials of sharing in the teacher (Basis Sharing), and a bank with shared read rows and selected write coefficients is the shape both that result and Entry 115 point to.
4. Pass index against state as the selector is a factor to vary, not assume: the published evidence conflicts (Looping Beyond Twice against Sparse Layers are Critical; LayerShuffle says the state is enough).
5. Stability practice that recurs: untied first and last blocks; re-injecting the input; identity-biased gates or near-zero residual scales at the start; random depth; sigmoid scores.
6. What published work does not measure, and a small carrier can: configurations held out as such.

*Universal Transformers, dropout and Mixture-of-Recursions were read on the same day in the deeper search (Entry 128, next section), which also narrows this section's "not found" lines.*

Not read, and relevant: Dehghani et al. (2018) Universal Transformers; ALBERT; DictFormer; Srivastava et al. (2014) on dropout as training over unit subsets; Mixture-of-Recursions beyond its abstract; Giannou et al. beyond the abstract; the remaining parts of the papers marked partial above.

### The deeper and wider search: every part of the idea against sixty-nine more papers (read 23 Nov 2026; history Entry 128)

After Entry 127 the researcher asked for a search as thorough and many-sided as possible. Eight waves, sixty-nine papers, chosen to leave the mixture-of-experts literature: module networks and controllers (2016–2021), hypernetworks and fast weights, weight-space mixing (convolution kernels, LoRA libraries, task vectors), looped mixtures of experts including five papers from 2025–2026, tests and theory of unseen combinations, binding, supernets and dropout, continual learning, and multitask recurrent networks in neuroscience. Read depth is on each entry. "In these notes" marks a paper an earlier section already holds and that was re-read here for a different question. Numbers are the papers' own. **Independently reviewed the same day** ([review](../experiments/theory/lookup/review/review_literature_128.md): "sound with corrections", twelve of them; 34 searches of its own). The corrections are made in place below and listed in history Entry 128; section H and the lines marked *reviewer* come from that review.

**A. A layer's weights built from a learned bank by the context: older and larger than Entry 118 found.**
- [**Gidaris & Komodakis (CVPR 2018), *Dynamic Few-Shot Visual Learning without Forgetting*.**](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1804.09458) (first 43 %). A new class's weight vector is w′ = φ_avg ⊙ w_avg + φ_att ⊙ w_att, where w_att is **an attention over a memory of the base classes' learned weight vectors**: query from the context example's feature, one free learned key per stored weight, cosine scores, softmax. Learned weights as values, context as query, in 2018; one layer, one row of weights at a time.
- [**Yang, Bender, Le & Ngiam (NeurIPS 2019), *CondConv*.**](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.04971) (complete). Kernel = Σ α_i(x) W_i with α a sigmoid of the pooled input, per example; "equivalent to a more expensive linear mixture of experts formulation" because convolution is linear in the kernel — the exact case of Entry 115's rule, stated there. Sigmoid beats softmax (62.0 against 60.5); **one routing shared by all layers is worst (56.5)**; routing becomes class-specific with depth; larger routers overfit.
- [**Zhong, Xia, Chen & Lewis (2024), *Lory*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.03133v2) (complete). The feed-forward block applied to a token is Σ e_i θ_i over stored blocks, with e computed from **the mean hidden state of the preceding segment**: weights set by context, at pre-training scale (up to 1.5B active and 29.5B stored parameters; the 0.3B model with 32 experts improves on the dense model's perplexity by 13.9 % on one domain). Trained with similar documents batched together (the 7B runs went without); about 900 of 1,536 experts end up used. Read and write matrices are averaged separately, which is not the average of outputs (W8); every expert starts as a copy of one block.
- [**Tian et al. (NeurIPS 2024), *HydraLoRA*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.19245v2) (first 82 %, read in wave 1). ΔW = Σ ω_i B_i A: **one shared read matrix, several write matrices mixed by a router** — the case where averaging weights is exactly averaging outputs (W7), arrived at from the observation that separately trained adapters share A and differ in B.
- [**Ostapenko et al. (ICML 2024), *Towards Modular LLMs by Building and Reusing a Library of LoRAs*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.11157v1) (first 46 %). **Arrow routing: each adapter's key is the top singular vector of its own weight product**, computed from the weights with no data and no training; per layer and per token the score is |h · key|, the absolute value because a singular vector's sign is arbitrary (the symmetry point of U6); top-k, softmax, then A and B mixed separately. Zero-shot on held-out tasks: 66.6 against 64.8 for the uniform average (Phi-2, 256 adapters); no gain on a clustered library with Mistral (63.9 against 64.6).
- [**Huang et al. (2023), *LoraHub*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.13269v3) (complete). For an unseen task, (Σ w_i A_i)(Σ w_i B_i) with coefficients found by gradient-free search on a few examples: 34.7 against 27.0 zero-shot and 37.7 for tuning a new adapter; fails far from the upstream tasks (1.0 against 22.2 on Dyck).
- [**Oldfield et al. (NeurIPS 2024), *Multilinear Mixture of Experts*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12550v3) (first 81 %). Experts are slices of one factorised weight tensor, y = W ×₁ a ×₂ z; with several gate levels the effective number of experts is the product ∏ N_e while parameters grow with the sum. Combinatorially many experts by composition, soft and differentiable; accuracy on par with dense at about 100M parameters.
- [**Veness et al. (AAAI 2021), *Gated Linear Networks*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.01526) (complete). **Every neuron holds a table of weight vectors and the input picks one** by fixed random half-space tests, so the network is a product of matrices that depend on the input; each neuron learns locally. Per-unit lookup of weights by context, with an address that is never trained.
- [**Cheung, Terekhov, Chen, Agrawal & Olshausen (NeurIPS 2019), *Superposition of many models into one*.**](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1902.05522) (first 43 %). One weight matrix stores many models; model k is read out by a context key, y = W(c_k ⊙ x); near-orthogonal keys make the others noise. Context is a task or time index; inferring it from the input is left open.
- [**Träuble et al. (ICML 2023), *Discrete Key-Value Bottleneck*.**](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.11240) (first 92 %). Frozen encoder, **frozen keys, learned values**; only retrieved values are updated; class-incremental learning without replay. Stability bought by taking the address out of training.
- [**Zhmoginov, Sandler & Vladymyrov (ICML 2022), *HyperTransformer*.**](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2201.04182) (first 37 %). A transformer generates a small network's weights from the support set **layer by layer, each layer's weights conditioned on the support samples' activations under the layers already generated**: weights set by context and by the state the context has reached. Helps small networks; above a size, generating the last layer is enough.
- [**Schlag, Munkhdalai & Schmidhuber (ICLR 2021), *Learning Associative Inference Using Fast Weight Memory*.**](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.07831) (complete). Weights written from context by outer products and read several times in a row, each read forming the next query; 96.75 % against 87.66 % for a larger Transformer-XL on a chained-inference task. Values are activations, not learned parameters.
- [**Jayakumar et al. (ICLR 2020), *Multiplicative Interactions and Where to Find Them*.**](https://gwern.net/docs/www/openreview.net/bedd2fc3533091dd885be551546da207c54f2700.pdf) (complete). f(x, z) = zᵀWx + zᵀU + Vx + b is exactly a hypernetwork W′ = zᵀW + V; gating and FiLM are restricted forms (attention is named and not studied; mixtures of experts are mentioned as an earlier example); ReLU networks that only add or concatenate the context are a strict subset; "summation is trivially easier". **Entry 117's map (static against context-dependent weights is additive against multiplicative conditioning) is this paper.**
- [**Dherin et al. (2025), *Learning without training: the implicit dynamics of in-context learning*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16003v3) (first 27 %: the theorems). Exact: attention followed by an MLP equals the MLP with its first weight matrix changed by a rank-one term set by the context, ΔW = (W δA) A(x)ᵀ / ‖A(x)‖². An ordinary transformer block is already a context-dependent-weight block, on the read side, at rank one.
- [**Tong et al. (2025), *Neural ODE Transformers*.**](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2503.01329) (first 41 %). All weights are a function of a continuous layer index, W(t) = Proj(MLP(sin(t))); "comparable to or better than" ordinary transformers. A network of infinitely many distinct layers from one generator, indexed by depth and not by context.
- [**Ortiz-Jimenez, Favero & Frossard (NeurIPS 2023), *Task Arithmetic in the Tangent Space*.**](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.12827) (first 38 %). Adding stored weight differences composes functions when each direction changes the function only on its own inputs ("weight disentanglement"); this is a product of pre-training, absent at random initialisation, and stronger for linearised models.
- [**Han et al. (TPAMI 2021), *Dynamic Neural Networks: A Survey*.**](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2102.04906) (first 61 %). The taxonomy that contains all of the above: dynamic parameters W(x, Θ) by attention over kernels or by weight prediction, and "applying attention on features is equivalent to performing convolution with dynamic weights". Nothing on unseen combinations in the part read.

**B. Choosing among stored functions by what they do to the input, not by a separate table of keys.**
- [**Goyal et al. (2020), *Object Files and Schemata* (SCOFF).**](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.16225) (first 63 %). Entities keep states and own no weights; a small set of **schemata — whole sets of recurrent weights** — is shared. At each step each entity runs every schema on its input, and **the key for schema j is a projection of that hypothetical update**; the entity's state is the query; one schema is chosen (hard). Per item, per step, choice among weight sets by keys computed from the weights applied to the item. 82 % against 56 % for a single recurrent network (74 % when that network is matched in parameters) when the grid grows; no held-out combinations.
- [**Mittal, Raparthy, Rish, Bengio & Lajoie (ICLR 2022), *Compositional Attention*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.09419v2) (complete). S searches and R value matrices shared among them; a second attention, per token, picks which value matrix each search uses, **with keys computed from what each value matrix retrieved**. Trained on some search-retrieval pairings and tested on the held-out ones: 0.17 in / 0.18 out against 0.24 / 0.67 for ordinary heads in the best configuration (at a quarter of the width its held-out error is 0.47–0.88). Failure case reported: nothing forces the value matrices to differ.
- [**Ostapenko, Rodríguez, Caccia & Charlin (NeurIPS 2021), *Continual Learning via Local Module Composition*.**](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2111.07736) (first 76 %). Every module carries its own scorer of how well the input fits it; a layer mixes modules by those scores; no global router. "It is the locality of the structural component that enables generalization to related but unseen tasks"; modules from separately trained networks combine without tuning. High variance; harder with many modules; hard selection does poorly.
- [**Aljundi, Chakravarty & Tuytelaars (CVPR 2017), *Expert Gate*.**](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1611.06194) (complete). One autoencoder per expert; the expert whose autoencoder reconstructs the input best is used; 97.6 % correct selection against 97.8 % for a trained gate that needs stored data.
- [**Wortsman et al. (NeurIPS 2020), *Supermasks in Superposition*.**](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.14769) (first 24 %). Thousands of sub-networks as masks over one fixed random network; the right one is found for an input by descending the output entropy over a mixture of masks, often in one step.
- Three different things, which the first draft lumped as "seven forms" (*reviewer*): **a key computed from the weights alone** (Arrow, in A); **a score from the stored function applied to the item** (SCOFF, Compositional Attention, Supermasks; MOSAIC 1998 and modular meta-learning 2018 from Entry 121); **a separate scorer attached to each module** (Local Module Composition, Expert Gate).

**C. One bank of functions applied step after step, chosen by the state.**
- [**Chang, Gupta, Levine & Griffiths (ICLR 2019), *Automatically Composing Representation Transformations as a Means for Generalization*.**](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1807.04640) (first 63 %). A controller reads the current state and picks the next module from one bank, or halts; the same bank is applied recursively. **Tested on compositions never trained:** longer arithmetic (trained to length 5, about 60 % at 100 terms where a recurrent network fails at 10) and new combinations of image transformations (baselines reach under a third of its score). Controller trained by reinforcement learning with a curriculum; "tricky to tune"; the modules did not become the intended primitives.
- **In these notes, re-read:** Routing Networks ([complete](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.01239): the router is called recursively on its own output; a single router collapses onto one or two blocks); Neural Interpreters ([first 37 %](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.06399): functions are codes that modulate one shared weight set, re-applied over iterations with routing recomputed from the state, "depth without increasing the number of parameters"; signatures are best frozen at initialisation or they collapse); Stack-NMN and MAC ([first 83 %](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1807.08556), [complete](https://arxiv.org/html/1803.03067): per step, the question sets soft weights over one shared module set, or steers one shared cell; the cell with shared weights beat its unshared version, 98.9 against 97.8).
- [**Reed & de Freitas (ICLR 2016), *Neural Programmer-Interpreters*.**](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1511.06279) (first 87 %, wave 1). One recurrent core; a memory of program keys and embeddings; the embedding is an input to the core, "a more succinct representation than neural programs encoded as the full set of weights". The static-weights form of a program lookup; needs execution traces.
- [**Meyerson & Miikkulainen (ICLR 2018), *Beyond Shared Hierarchies: Deep Multitask Learning through Soft Layer Ordering*.**](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1711.00108) (first 67 %, wave 1). Every depth of every task is a soft mixture over one bank of layers; the mixture is fixed per task and depth.
- [**Eigen, Ranzato & Sutskever (2013), *Learning Factored Representations in a Deep Mixture of Experts*.**](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1312.4314) (complete). Gates at each layer conditioned on the previous layer's output: "our model can express an exponentially large number of effective experts … yet maintains a modest model size". **"Effectively infinite experts by composition" in 2013**, with its problem: "SGD by itself results in a degenerate local minimum", fixed by forcing balanced assignments early; no better than one expert on test.
- [**Wang et al. (2025), *Chain-of-Experts*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18945v1) (first 74 %). Inside one layer, several iterations over the same expert pool, re-routed on the updated state: two iterations match three times the width. The ablation that **reuses the first iteration's selection on every iteration** stalls, worse than the ordinary mixture: re-selecting on the updated state is what helps. (First draft said this ablation was one gate network shared across iterations; it is not — gating weights "fixed across iterations". *Reviewer's correction, re-read and confirmed.*)
- **MoEUT, in these notes, re-read for its analysis** ([complete](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16039v1)). Experts of 128 units from a pool shared across passes, chosen by the token's state with no pass index, in groups of two to four untied layers. "MoEUT is capable of specializing in a specific layer if necessary and sharing weights between them when advantageous"; "experts usually change dynamically in a single column, performing different functionality in different layers". The *mechanism* of claim (3) at this grain is this paper. Not shown there (*reviewer*): a bank smaller than the layers it replaces — by the paper's table the 243M model stores more pooled expert units than the equal-depth dense baseline has feed-forward units, at 13.24 against 13.40 perplexity; and layers inside a group are untied, so position within the group is known by construction.
- [**Wang et al. (2026), *How to Loop MoE: Flatten the Experts, Untie the Attention*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.35751) (complete). Experts and routers shared across passes, attention untied per pass; "flattening" trades layers for passes down to one layer of 64 experts run sixteen times; the router sees only the state. Loss falls a little with flattening when attention is untied (2.511 to 2.499); with tied attention it "rises again after the first flattening step". One seed; no equal-compute unlooped control; no held-out combinations.
- **Looping Beyond Twice, in these notes, re-read** ([complete](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.01153)). Names "expert selection collapse": with a shared router the experts picked are nearly the same on every loop; fix is a router per loop, plus residual scaling, input re-injection and truncated backpropagation (nine loops untruncated ends in a loss spike it does not recover from).
- [**Qiu et al. (COLM 2026), *MoRE: Mixture of Reused Experts*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18176) (complete). Adjacent layers share an expert pool; each has its own router and **a learned depth embedding added to the router's input**, because shared experts "have no way to condition on which layer is calling them". Slightly better than per-layer experts (15.89 against 15.98 perplexity) and clearly better than MoEUT in their setting (16.94). Sharing across two layers or eighteen makes no measurable difference. Counts possible combinations; does not test unused ones.
- [**Qiu et al. (ICLR 2025), *Layerwise Recurrent Router for Mixture-of-Experts*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06793v2) (complete). The router at each layer reads a recurrent state carried up the layers, h_i = GRU(x_i, h_{i−1}): **each choice of experts is conditioned on the token's earlier choices**, because independent routers "may prevent routers from discovering more effective token-expert combinations". Small, consistent gains. The mechanism of idea I39, in an untied network, not used for correction.
- [**Zhou et al. (Nov 2026), *Understanding Mixture-of-Experts Language Models Beyond Expert Boundaries*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13975) (first 46 %). In trained models, domain specialisation is organised by neurons that cut across experts, "neurons, rather than whole experts, as the unit". Descriptive; no analysis of combinations.
- [**Wang, Chen, Yu, Cheung & LeCun (WACV 2023), *Recurrent Parameter Generators*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.07110v2) (complete). One ring of parameters; every layer's kernel is a fixed permutation and sign-flip of a slice of it, so a bank of any size yields a network of any depth. Accuracy is log-linear in bank size: 96 % of ResNet-18 at 18 % of its parameters. Static.
- [**Li, Li & Zhou (2025; ICML 2026), *Skip a Layer or Loop It?***](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.07996) and [*Learning Program-of-Layers*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.06574) (both complete). A pretrained model's layers skipped or repeated per input: for over 75 % of correct answers a shorter chain exists and for over 60 % of wrong ones some chain is right (search with labels); a small predictor recovers a few points of that from the input text alone. Layers trained in one order work in orders never trained.
- Also read: Universal Transformers ([first 91 %](https://arxiv.org/html/1807.03819v1): one block with a time-step embedding; universal "assuming sufficient memory"); Sparse Universal Transformer, in these notes ([first 81 %](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.07096): CFQ 58.4 against 21.4; no analysis of experts per pass); Mixture-of-Recursions ([first 28 %](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10524v3): routers set depth per token, one parameter set); Recurrent Independent Mechanisms ([first 36 %](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1909.10893): modules with own weights, top-k active; no test of module sets never active together); Neural Attentive Circuits ([first 46 %](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.08031): module codes can be generated from the input; without a prior all signatures collapse to one value); ModuleFormer ([first 73 %](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.04640): "uses more parameters to achieve performance comparable to dense models").

**D. Do combinations never trained work? Tests, and the theory of when.**
- [**Bahdanau et al. (ICLR 2019), *Systematic Generalization: What Is Required and Can It Be Learned?***](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1811.12889) (first 59 %). Object pairs never seen together. With the right module layout given, perfect; with **the layout learned, the outcome depends on the start and on the module type** (one type of module started at 10 % on the right layout stays there, 32 % error, and started at 50 % stays at 50 %; the other type reaches the right layout from 50 %); with the modules' inputs learned, **a blurred assignment fits training and fails held-out**. Systematic generalisation "may require explicit regularizers or priors". The empirical form of tests B1–B5: seen combinations do not fix the part that decides the unseen ones.
- [**Kobayashi, Schug, Akram et al. (2024), *When can transformers compositionally generalize in-context?***](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2407.12275) (complete). Tasks are combinations of six modules; demonstrations in context; held-out combinations. An ordinary transformer fits training and **fails held-out combinations although the combination is linearly decodable from its activations**; a transformer that must pass through a small code that generates the executing network's weights generalises; with disconnected training combinations that model fails too. **The nearest published experiment to stage B.**
- [**Saxe, Sodhani & Lewallen (ICML 2022), *The Neural Race Reduction*.**](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10160774/1/saxe22a.pdf) (in these notes; now read complete). With gates given, pathways shared by more routes learn faster and win; untrained input-output routes work once roughly 40 % of routes are trained (a simulation at M = 10; about 25 % with leaky-ReLU units on MNIST), and "only small initializations are capable of zero-shot generalization to untrained routes". How gates are found is outside the theory.
- [**Lippl & Stachenfeld (ICLR 2025)**](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16391v3) (first 17 %, wave 1) and [**Abbe, Bengio, Lotfi & Rizk (ICML 2023)**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13105v2) (abstract): kernel-regime models are "conjunction-wise additive" on unseen combinations, and trained networks take the lowest-degree fit there. Interactions between parts never seen together are not learned by default.
- [**D'Amario, Sasaki & Boix (NeurIPS 2021), *How Modular Should Neural Module Networks Be for Systematic Generalization?***](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.08170) (first 39 %). Layout given. Modules per *group* of sub-tasks beat both one shared module and one module per sub-task (on CLEVR-CoGenT, grouped encoders 77.3 against 73.2 shared), and ten times the data does not rescue the finest grain. Grain matters and not monotonically.
- [**Ruis & Lake (CogSci 2022), *Improving Systematic Generalization Through Modularity and Augmentation*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.10745) (complete). Supervised modules alone: 25.9 ± 32.1 against 19.0 ± 4.1 on a new adverb-verb combination, "no clear improvement from modularity alone"; with augmentation 76.8.
- [**Chen et al. (WACV 2021), *Meta Module Network*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.03230) (complete). One module instantiated by a key-value "recipe" of the function wanted; functions never trained run at 61–77 % against 74–86 % when trained.
- **In these notes, read further:** Mittal, Bengio & Lajoie ([first 45 %](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.02713): learned routing neither attends to the right information nor specialises, and worsens with more rules); Csordás et al. ([complete](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.02066): trained networks keep separate weights for two instances of the same function and combination-specific weights on SCAN, "fail to learn general, modular, compositional algorithms"); Kirsch et al. ([complete](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.05249): names module collapse; more modules raise training accuracy on images and "this does not lead to improved generalization"). New: [Jarvis et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14981v1) (abstract): "the modules specializing is not" guaranteed; [Lepori, Serre & Pavlick (NeurIPS 2023)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.10884) (first 59 %): ordinary networks often do hold sub-routines in separable sub-networks, and whether that gives new combinations is left open.
- **Subsets of one pool of units that were never trained as such.** [Dropout (JMLR 2014)](https://jmlr.csail.mit.edu/papers/v15/srivastava14a.html) (abstract): "samples from an exponential number of different 'thinned' networks", tested as one network never sampled. [Once-for-All (ICLR 2020)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1908.09791) (about half): about 2 × 10¹⁹ sub-networks of one weight set usable without retraining, one to four sampled per step; "different sub-networks are interfering with each other", cured by training the largest first (74.8 against 71.5). [E. Bengio et al. (2015)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.06297) (complete): a policy on each layer's input switches blocks of units per example. [Masse, Grant & Freedman (PNAS 2018)](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1802.01569) (first 69 %): a fixed random fifth of the units per task; "likely does not support transfer learning in its current form". So unit subsets never trained together do work when subsets are sampled at random on one data distribution; none of these selects them by context and tests held-out contexts.

**E. Binding.**
- [**Greff, van Steenkiste & Schmidhuber (2020), *On the Binding Problem in Artificial Neural Networks*.**](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2012.05208) (first 39 %). Several entities in one shared representation give "an indiscriminate bag of features because there is no association of features to objects" — the superposition catastrophe (Rosenblatt 1961; von der Malsburg 1981, 1986). Remedy: keep a separate slot per entity. The binding problem is named the "underlying cause" of failures to generalise systematically. **The pooled gate's failure (Entry 122) is this, at the address: items' addresses summed before they are normalised lose which item called which block; normalising each item first is the slot remedy.**

**F. The static alternative: one weight set, the context as an input.**
- [**Yang, Joglekar, Song, Newsome & Wang (Nature Neuroscience 2019)**](https://www.cns.nyu.edu/wanglab/publications/pdf/yang.nn2019.pdf) (complete). One recurrent network, twenty tasks, a rule input. Units cluster by process; rule inputs compose (the network performs one task when given the sum and difference of three others' rule vectors; fails for a second family); a new task is learned by training only the rule input's weights.
- [**Driscoll, Shenoy & Sussillo (Nature Neuroscience 2024)**](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.08.15.503870v1.abstract) (abstract only). "Dynamical motifs are implemented by clusters of units and are reused across different contexts."
- [**Riveland & Pouget (Nature Neuroscience 2024)**](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10211884/1/s41593-024-01607-5.pdf) (first 86 %). Fifty tasks, five held out, instructions embedded by a language model and given as input: **83 % on tasks never trained, against 39 % with unstructured rule vectors** and 88 % with hand-structured ones.
- With MAC above: **fixed weights reach unseen combinations when the context arrives as a structured input.** The comparison the research question asks for (Entry 117) therefore has a strong static side in the literature, and its strength depends on the structure of the context code, not on the weights being fixed.

**G. Why training these is unstable, and what is used against it.**
- [**Chi et al. (NeurIPS 2022), *On the Representation Collapse of Sparse Mixture of Experts*.**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.09179) (complete). Dot-product routing pulls hidden states toward the expert embeddings, into a subspace of as many dimensions as experts; remedy: low-dimensional, normalised (cosine) scores.
- [**Ortiz, Guttag & Dalca (ICLR 2024), *Magnitude Invariant Parametrizations Improve Hypernetwork Learning*.**](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.07645) (first 68 %). The scale of generated weights follows the scale of the generator's input, so gradient scale swings; remedy: constant-norm inputs and **weights = a learned default + a generated change**. "Training hypernetworks is challenging."
- Recurring across the sections above: collapse onto few modules (Eigen 2013; Rosenbaum 2018; Kirsch 2018; Neural Attentive Circuits; Lory's unused experts); nearly the same experts chosen on every pass (Looping Beyond Twice); state variance growing with passes. Recurring remedies: forced balance early; an address that is frozen or computed from the weights (Discrete Key-Value Bottleneck, Neural Interpreters, Arrow, Gated Linear Networks); a selector per pass; residual scaling and re-injection of the input; all members starting as copies of one block (Lory).

**H. Added after the independent review: sparse coding and unrolled inference, units selected by the prompt, pools shared by all layers, and a concept paper.** The review's main finding was a body of work the four passes had not touched. The first six entries I then read myself; the rest are the reviewer's reads, marked.
- [**Rolfe & LeCun (ICLR 2013), *Discriminative Recurrent Sparse Auto-Encoders*.**](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1301.3775) (first 68 %). z^{t+1} = max(0, E x + S z^t − b), eleven passes, 400 units, the same E, S, b on every pass: "a deep network, with parameters shared between the hidden layers", the input re-injected at each pass. **One bank of units, a sparse set of them active per pass, chosen by the state.** After training the units split into two kinds, and the second kind typically first becomes active on the second pass: different units do the work on different passes with no pass index. Descends from LISTA (Gregor & LeCun 2010; *reviewer's read, through a survey*), unrolled sparse coding with tied parameters. This makes "a single-unit bank shared across passes" false as I first worded it. Difference from the idea: every unit is computed on every pass; sparsity is in the activations, not in what is retrieved.
- [**Barin Pacela, Joshi, Camacho, Lacoste-Julien & Klindt (2026), *Stop Probing, Start Coding*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28744v1) (first 93 %). Sparse latents mixed linearly into fewer dimensions; training shows the pairs (z₁, z₂) and (z₂, z₃) active together and **the test shows (z₁, z₃), a set of units never co-active in training**. One-pass encoders (sparse autoencoders of three kinds, linear probes) recover the latents poorly (correlation 0.2–0.5); iterative per-sample inference with the *true* dictionary recovers them (≥ 0.95, held-out accuracy 0.97): "the OOD failure is not inherent to superposition". Iterative inference on the *learned* dictionary does not help: "the SAE-learned dictionary itself is the binding constraint". The nearest thing found to the remaining question, with an answer in outline: unseen unit sets are no obstacle given the right bank and iterative selection; learning the bank is what fails. Differences: the input selects (no separate context); the block reconstructs its input; the learned models are one pass. (The reviewer's summary said the theory "covers unseen supports"; on my read the conditions are stated by sparsity level, and the paper's claim is about dictionary learning.)
- [**Hou et al. (ICML 2025), *Instruction-Following Pruning for Large Language Models*.**](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.02086v3) (complete). A small predictor reads the prompt and **selects which individual feed-forward units run, per layer**, trained jointly with the model: 9B stored, 3B active, ahead of a dense 3B model by 3–8 points. Lower layers pick nearly the same units for every input, higher layers differ by domain. Context-selected units at language-model scale; no held-out test of unit sets; no loop.
- [**Jiao et al. (2026), *Improving Parameter Utilization by Sharing Neural Experts Across Layers* (CS-MoE).**](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.22199) (complete). One central pool of experts for every layer, plus one private expert per layer, a router per layer: 9.19 against 9.40 perplexity for per-layer experts at 12B. **One router shared by all layers: 10.29 against 10.08.** Use of the pool drifts smoothly with depth (68 % overlap of the most-used experts between adjacent layers, 42 % five layers apart).

**What a design would have to take from this** (for decision D141; no design document was changed).
1. Whether selection needs a pass index is unsettled in print (one clear failure without it, three recipes that work without it, two small gains from adding it). It stays a factor to vary, as Entry 126 said; the variety of blocks across passes has to be measured, not assumed.
2. Stage B has a near-neighbour (Kobayashi et al. 2024) whose known axis is bottleneck against none. The stage should say what it adds: retrieval from a bank by matching applied blocks, in a loop, with exact references.
3. The static arms should be expected to be strong when the context code is structured (MAC; Riveland & Pouget). A static arm losing is a result about the code as much as about fixed weights.
4. Predictions should be written against Saxe (share of routes trained, small initialisation), Schug (connected support, no spare capacity), Lippl (additive limit) and sparse recovery (H: with the right bank, an unseen set of units is recovered by iterative, residual-driven selection, and a one-pass selector fails; learning the bank is the hard part). The first draft said theory does not reach unit grain; it does (*reviewer*), and it gives the loop a job. The information is in learned selection by a separate context, and in whether the bank is learned well enough.
5. Grain is a factor with a non-monotone effect (D'Amario), so unit against block grain belongs in the arms.
6. Remedies with precedent, to try before inventing: frozen or weight-derived addresses; per-pass selector; residual scaling and re-injection; default-plus-change parametrisation; members initialised as copies.
