# Topology steers learning through composition, not invariants

Prior work places this program in well-mapped territory with an unmapped center. The theory agrees that skip connections act mainly by moving the origin of the parameterization to the identity and by reshaping the distribution of path lengths. A 2025 result shows that **residual blocks have exactly the same conservation laws as the same blocks without the skip** ([Marcotte et al., ICML 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06194)). So any effect of parameter-free topology on emergent structure has to come from how blocks are composed into a global parameter-to-function map, not from new local invariants. That matches the program's own Phase 0 result that every parent-selection graph is first-order identical at zero. Several neighboring questions are already settled. Connectivity changes the lazy-regime kernel and convergence rate ([Huang et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06262); [Chen et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05662)). A single added connection can change expressive efficiency ([Cohen, Tamari & Shashua](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06846)). The weight-decay inductive bias of residual chains has a closed form ([Boix-Adsera 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19149)). Identical parallel branches are predicted to collapse under weight decay or noise ([Ziyin 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16932)). What remains open is what gradient flow, without regularization, selects in general residual DAGs. Also open: the singular-learning complexity of non-chain parameterizations, saddle-to-saddle dynamics and μP transfer for parallel and mixed graphs, and, on the empirical side, any matched-parameter study that changes only routing and measures internal structure or the end-to-end learning of compositional targets. The strongest empirical evidence comes from single-edge ablations read out through representations. Many headline routing gains are confounded by normalization placement, initialization, bundled parameters, and unequal tuning. A valid program therefore needs function-space primary endpoints, causal tests against a shared reference algorithm, nulls that respect the fixed topology, per-arm tuning, richness swept as a phase diagram, and paired-seed statistics powered at roughly 29 runs per arm for headline claims. For the topology-design plan, the flagship composition study and the linear-DAG implicit-bias problem (Prop. 7) are the best novelty bets. The parallel-collapse result (Prop. 2) and the chain orientation lock (Corollary 3a) are mostly anticipated by prior work. The nonlinear parallel arm is, mathematically, a wide two-layer network, which both anchors it to existing theory and narrows what counts as new.

## Skips move the origin; composition carries the topology

### Five strands of residual theory leave unregularized implicit bias unsolved

Residual-network theory falls into five strands. None of them gives what the program most needs: a characterization of where unregularized gradient flow ends up in a general linear residual graph.

The first strand covers landscape and convergence. Linear residual networks of any depth have no spurious local optima, a proof the authors call "strikingly simple" next to the plain-chain case ([Hardt & Ma, ICLR 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.04231)). Gradient descent from zero-initialized residual blocks converges globally, with width requirements independent of depth ([Zou, Long & Gu, ICLR 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.01094)). Identity-initialized residual chains efficiently learn positive-definite targets and can fail on targets with negative eigenvalues ([Bartlett, Helmbold & Long, ICML 2018](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/bartlett18a.html)). The research notes recall that last detail from memory and did not re-check it, but if accurate it bears directly on Phase 0's Corollary 3a. Deep linear regression is implicitly regularized toward flat minima: the sharpness lower bound grows linearly with depth, gradient flow lands within a depth- and width-independent constant of that bound, and convergence is also proved for a residual Gaussian initialization ([Marion & Chizat 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13456)). These results say that residual topology trains; they do not say which solution it selects.

The second strand covers infinite-depth limits. With 1/L branch scaling and smooth initialization, trained residual networks converge to neural ODEs, and gradient flow keeps weights smooth across depth. The authors explicitly leave open whether gradient flow selects a *minimum-complexity* ODE, and they suggest the i.i.d.-initialization limit is an SDE ([Marion et al., ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.01213)). The branch scale α_L and initialization regularity determine which limiting object you get (ODE, SDE, or degenerate) ([Marion et al., JMLR 2025](https://www.jmlr.org/beta/papers/v26/22-0664.html)). With 1/√L scaling, the width and depth limits commute ([Hayou & Yang, ICML 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/hayou23a.html)), and adding μP gives a feature-learning joint limit with hyperparameter transfer across depth ([Bordelon et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16620)). The "feature speed formula" makes the mechanism concrete. The size of a feature update depends on the angle between the feature update and the backward pass. For a ReLU MLP with i.i.d. initialization, **cos θ_ℓ = Θ(1/√ℓ) degenerates with depth, while a ResNet with branch scale O(1/√depth) keeps cos θ_ℓ = Θ(1)** ([Chizat & Netrapalli, NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18718)). For this program the lesson is methodological: the branch multiplier is not a neutral knob. It selects the asymptotic theory that applies, so it can masquerade as a topology effect unless it is swept.

The third strand covers representation cost, and it gives the cleanest closed-form contrast in the literature. For a linear ResNet W_u(I+W_1)…(I+W_L)W_e with weight decay, the minimum cost splits over singular values. With depth-2 blocks at infinite depth it is Σ_{σ≤λ} σ + Σ_{σ>λ} λ(1+log(σ/λ)). That is nuclear-norm-like for small singular values and only **logarithmic in large ones**, so the bias lies "between nuclear norm and rank" ([Boix-Adsera 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19149)). Plain deep chains instead carry the standard Schatten-2/L quasi-norm penalty, and their large-depth nonlinear cost tends to bottleneck rank ([Jacot, ICLR 2023](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/oral/12530)). Leaky ResNets interpolate between the two through an "effective depth" knob, with a Hamiltonian picture in which a "cost of identity" favors low-dimensional representations and produces a bottleneck at large effective depth ([Jacot & Kaiser, CPAL 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17573)). Boix-Adsera warns that dynamics and the absence of weight decay can change the true bias, so these are statements about regularized optima, not about gradient-flow trajectories.

The fourth strand covers signal propagation and kernels. Removing skips makes the deep feedforward NTK function class degenerate, while the ResNet NTK does not ([Huang et al., NeurIPS 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06262)). The NNGP spectrum can be propagated through an arbitrary connectivity DAG to bound convergence rates ([Chen et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05662)). Without skips or MLPs, pure self-attention collapses to rank one doubly exponentially with depth ([Dong, Cordonnier & Loukas, ICML 2021](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/dong21a.html)). The fifth strand is path-based. Residual networks behave like ensembles of relatively shallow paths, with gradient carried by short paths ([Veit, Wilber & Belongie, NeurIPS 2016](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2016/hash/37bc2f75bf1bcfe8450a1a41c200364c-Abstract.html)). Rescaling-invariant path norms are defined for general DAG ReLU networks with skips, although the resulting bounds are loose on ImageNet ResNets ([Gonon et al., ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01225)). For classification with exponential loss, the late phase of gradient descent on near-homogeneous networks, residual ones included, converges in direction to a KKT point of a margin-maximization problem ([Cai et al., ICML 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16075)). Topology therefore enters only through *which* margin problem the network's own parameter-to-function map defines.

### Conservation laws close one door and open another

The single most important boundary result for this program is that **"residual blocks have the same conservation laws as the same block without a skip connection,"** and that laws depending on a block's parameters can be found by analyzing that block alone ([Marcotte, Peyré & Gribonval, ICML 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06194)). Any claim that skips create new local invariants is therefore false, and the SPEC's causal chain (topology → symmetries → conserved quantities → preconditioner) has to locate the topology effect in *cross-block coupling through the path structure*. Phase 0 already finds exactly this: first-order universality at zero branch weights, then second-order effects carried by the global product structure.

The constructive tools sit next door. The same group gives conditions under which gradient flow on θ induces an autonomous ("intrinsic") flow on a lift z = φ(θ). For ReLU networks of any depth, with φ the path-lifting, the flow is intrinsic for a dense set of initializations. For linear networks it is intrinsic for "relaxed balanced" initializations. DAGs are not treated ([Marcotte, Peyré & Gribonval, ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07370)). Gradient flow under any *commuting* reparameterization equals mirror descent with a computable Legendre potential ([Li, Wang, Lee & Arora, NeurIPS 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.04036)). The *shape* of initialization, meaning relative scales across layers or branches, also changes the implicit bias, and some models are not mirror descent under any potential ([Azulay et al., ICML 2021](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/azulay21a.html)). Together these turn "parent-selection family" into a concrete mathematical object: a family of path-lifting maps φ_k, each of which can be tested for intrinsic dynamics and for commutation. The notes found no paper that characterizes which DAG liftings commute.

Discrete symmetries give the sharpest qualitative predictions. Every mirror-reflection symmetry becomes an attracting constrained solution when weight decay or gradient noise is large. Rescaling symmetry gives sparsity, rotation symmetry gives low rank, and **permutation symmetry gives homogeneous ensembling (collapse)** ([Ziyin, ICML 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16932)). Applied to this program, identical parallel branches carry a permutation symmetry and are predicted to homogenize, while composition creates GL(n) symmetries between consecutive layers and is predicted to lower rank. Skips are also argued, but not proved, to break permutation and elimination singularities ([Orhan & Pitkow, ICLR 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.09175)).

### Tensor networks and singular learning theory reach the edge of DAGs and stop

The tensor-network line is the strongest existing example of "connectivity graph → specific emergent structure." Gradient flow on hierarchical tensor factorization is implicitly biased toward low hierarchical tensor rank. Through an equivalence with polynomial CNNs, that becomes a bias toward *locality*, and an explicit regularizer that counteracts locality improves modern CNNs on non-local tasks ([Razin, Maman & Cohen, ICML 2022](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/razin22a.html)). A unified tensor formulation covers fully connected, diagonal, and convolutional linear networks, but **explicitly does not address branched or parallel networks** ([Yun, Krishnan & Mobahi, ICLR 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02501)). Group-structured linear networks converge to low-rank Fourier coefficients regularized by a 2/L-Schatten norm in group-Fourier space ([Lawrence et al., ICML 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06084)). That template ("fixed linear structure → diagonalizing basis → Schatten penalty in that basis") could transfer to routing graphs that commute with a group. The missing object, in the notes' framing, is the implicit bias of a *sum of tensor networks that share factors*. That is what a linear residual DAG computes: each W_i appears in many paths. Single products are covered (Yun), and sums of two independent products are covered (diagonal networks), but shared factors across paths are not.

Singular learning theory has exact real log canonical thresholds (RLCTs) for deep linear networks. The fiber codimension depends only on sorted widths, and for constant width d with N > d it saturates at d(d+1)/2, which the authors summarize as "not very sensitive to depth" ([Pepin Lehalleur & Rimányi 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.19920)). The notes infer from that formula, though the paper does not state it, that a constant-width deep chain has a *lower* RLCT (d(d+1)/4) than a shallow d×d factorization of the same function class (d²/2). **No RLCT or local learning coefficient result exists for residual, parallel, or DAG parameterizations.** The SGLD-based LLC estimator exists ([Lau et al., AISTATS 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v258/lau25a.html)) and has been validated against exact deep-linear RLCTs ([Furman & Lau 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03698v1)), so this gap can be attacked analytically and checked empirically in the same linear setting.

### Composition theory proves matching for approximation and layerwise training, not end-to-end routing

Approximation theory shows that deep networks whose graph *matches* a compositional (tree or DAG) target avoid the curse of dimensionality ([Poggio et al. 2017](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/1611.00740)). Learning theory has moved toward gradient-based guarantees. Three-layer networks learn hierarchical targets with better sample complexity than two-layer networks ([Nichani, Damian & Lee, NeurIPS 2023](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/236b6a814a1d2c0ff504ca7bf380f7ff-Abstract-Conference.html)). SGD on a three-layer ResNet learns a class no kernel method can match ([Allen-Zhu & Li, NeurIPS 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10337)). End-to-end training of a deep architecture with every-layer-to-output connections performs "backward feature correction," which layerwise training cannot ([Allen-Zhu & Li, COLT 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04413)). On the task side, the merged-staircase property is a necessary and nearly sufficient condition for SGD on two-layer mean-field networks to learn sparse functions ([Abbe, Boix-Adsera & Misiakiewicz, COLT 2022](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v178/abbe22a.html)). Deep CNNs provably learn random hierarchy models with *layerwise* training when intermediate layers receive a clean label signal ([Ren, Dandi, Krzakala & Lee 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19756)). Deep networks are learnable on fractal distributions only when shallow approximations are already good ([Malach & Shalev-Shwartz, NeurIPS 2019](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/8871-is-deeper-better-only-when-shallow-is-good)). Parallel topology also changes global optimization geometry. Stacking deep linear networks in parallel gives zero duality gap and closed-form low-rank solutions under weight decay ([Wang, Ergen & Pilanci, ICLR 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06482)). The duality gap of multi-branch networks shrinks to zero as the number of branches grows ([Zhang, Shao & Salakhutdinov, AISTATS 2019](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v89/zhang19d.html)).

The notes found no paper that compares end-to-end gradient-descent learning of a compositional target under *different routing graphs at fixed operators and parameters*. That is the program's central question. It is open in the form stated, but it lies close to the occupied two-versus-three-layer separations, as the recommendations section explains.

## Single-edge ablations read out through representations are the strongest evidence

The most convincing empirical claims that parameter-free connectivity changes emergent structure share one design: remove or rescale a connection inside one architecture family and read the change through representations, not only through loss. Removing skip connections switches off **Residual Alignment**. Well-generalizing ResNets have intermediate representations equally spaced along a line, top Jacobian singular vectors aligned across depth, Jacobian rank at most the number of classes, and top singular values scaling as about 1/depth. All of this disappears without skips. The no-skip comparison had to use 8 blocks because the 16-block plain network did not converge, a depth confound the authors disclose ([Li, Papyan et al., NeurIPS 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.09018v1)). In ViTs, removing the skip from a single block **splits the CKA map into two dissimilar groups**, before and after that block, and costs about 4% accuracy in middle blocks ([Raghu et al., NeurIPS 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.08810)). Residual blocks push features along the negative gradient. The cosine between F(h) and ∂L/∂h is negative in every block and most negative in higher blocks, early blocks re-represent while late blocks refine, and unrolling the last block for 20 extra steps raised accuracy on borderline examples from 43% to 53% ([Jastrzębski et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1710.04773)). Decaying the identity shortcut with depth, a fixed schedule with no learned parameters, raised MAE ViT-B/16 k-NN accuracy from **27.4 to 63.9** and linear-probe accuracy from 67.8 to 72.7, lowered feature effective rank, and slightly hurt fine-tuning (83.6 → 82.9). A learnable decay did worse than the fixed schedule ([Zhang et al., CVPR 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.10947v4)). At random initialization, residual connections push the preferred function complexity "to some of the lowest levels for all activations," regardless of depth ([Teney et al., CVPR 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.02241v3)).

These results do not all point the same way, and the tension is informative. Neural Redshift says residuals lower *functional* complexity at initialization. Huh et al. find that ResNets, unlike linear networks, "do not exhibit a monotonic rank contracting behavior" ([Huh et al., TMLR 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.10427v4)). Zhang et al. argue that identity shortcuts inject "an echo of shallower representations into deeper layers," which keeps *representation* rank high. Input–output complexity and embedding rank are different quantities, and a topology study should measure both. Kernel analyses add that skips leave the asymptotic frequency-decay rate largely unchanged and mainly change locality and conditioning ([Barzilai et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14810); [Belfer et al., JMLR 2024](https://jmlr.org/papers/v25/22-0597.html)). So spectral-bias exponents are a weak dependent variable in the lazy regime.

Learned-routing papers show a convergent but confounded pattern. Hyper-Connections, MUDDFormer, and DeepCrossAttention each let the model learn its cross-layer routing, and each finds weight on embeddings or early layers plus the most recent layers. HC calls this a "Λ-shaped" pattern, with local jagged structure that suggests adjacent layers learned to run in parallel ([Zhu et al., ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.19606v3); [Heddes et al., ICML 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06785v2)). Pythia's later layers exceed **0.97 adjacent-layer cosine similarity**, MUDDPythia has about 2.4× more active attention heads, and removing dense connections from the value stream hurts most ([Xiao et al., ICML 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12170v2)). The confounds are serious. HC adds about 26% activation memory despite unchanged FLOPs. MUDDFormer bundles an FFN width reallocation that helps it and hurts the baseline. Unconstrained HC composite mappings reach an Amax gain of about **3000** and destabilize a 27B model, while doubly stochastic mHC stays at about 1.6 ([Xie et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.24880v2)). Giving parallel streams room does not make them specialize. In mHC-lite, one stream comes to dominate routing and holds most crosscoder features from layer 4 on, through rich-get-richer amplification of a small early asymmetry ([Alimaskina et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03483)). In a separate HC model, representational similarity between streams did not imply functional interchangeability ([Peng et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14833)).

Frozen-model interventions supply a reusable battery and one sharp regularity. Skipping, reversing, randomizing, or parallelizing the middle layers of Llama-2 degrades performance gracefully. Sharing weights among them is catastrophic, and **math and reasoning (GSM8K, ARC) are the most order-sensitive** ([Sun et al., AAAI 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.09298v1)). Deleting or swapping layers retains 72–95% of prediction accuracy ([Lad, Gurnee & Tegmark 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19384v1)). Second-half LLM layers change the residual stream little, and harder problems do not use more depth. The authors found "no evidence of compositional processing across depth," though their main analysis rests on 10 GSM8K examples ([Csordás, Manning & Potts, NeurIPS 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13898v1)). Normalization placement is part of the topology. **Pre-LN makes deep layers near-identity and prunable, while Post-LN inverts the pattern so that early layers become redundant** ([Sun et al., NeurIPS 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05795v3)). Initialization can stand in for skips: "looks-linear" initialization trains deep plain networks ([Balduzzi et al., ICML 2017](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/balduzzi17b.html)), and with signal-propagation-guided choices, skips, normalization, and sequential sub-blocks can all be removed at matched per-update training speed ([He & Hofmann 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01906)).

Two nulls bound expectations for the program's Phase 3. In the one controlled skip ablation for continual learning, skips had **no measurable effect on forgetting** (CNN×4: 68.1 vs 68.2 average accuracy, 8.7 vs 8.9 forgetting, within one standard deviation), while width helped and depth hurt ([Mirzadeh et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.00275)). In pruning at initialization, shuffling the kept weights within each layer does not hurt accuracy, so per-layer density, not specific wiring, drove performance ([Frankle et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08576)). The proper null for any wiring claim is therefore a density-matched rewiring, not "no wiring." Skip effects also grow with depth. Without shortcuts, CIFAR-10 error goes from 7.37% vs 8.18% at depth 20 to 5.79% vs 16.44% at depth 110, and loss landscapes turn chaotic ([Li et al., NeurIPS 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1712.09913)). Shallow experiments can produce nulls that do not generalize.

## Valid measurement starts in function space and climbs toward mechanism

The measurement problem is a correspondence problem. Residual, parallel, and composed networks at matched parameter count do not share units, layers, or weight-space coordinates that mean the same thing. A measure is valid across topologies only if it is computed on objects both networks share: the input–output function, the training data, a reference algorithm, or activations at a cut point whose symmetry is matched. The notes rank measurement families by how few correspondence assumptions they need. The table below summarizes that ranking, with the specific failure mode each family has to guard against.

| Tier | Measure family | Why it is valid across topologies | Main failure mode and guard |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Output agreement (JSD, disagreement), Walsh–Fourier degree profile, purified functional ANOVA, algorithm-discriminating behavioral probes | Never touches the parameterization | Depends on the input measure and on metric discontinuity. Fix the measure, use continuous metrics, purify ANOVA ([Lengerich et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04974v2); [Schaeffer et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.15004)) |
| 1 (synthetic) | Causal abstraction / DAS against a shared high-level algorithm | Each network is aligned to the same reference, not to the other network | Unrestricted maps make interchange-intervention accuracy (IIA) vacuous: random Pythia reaches 100% ([Sutter et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.08802)). Dormant pathways can fake a found subspace ([Makelov et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17030)) |
| 2 | LLC trajectories, susceptibilities | Invariant to smooth reparameterization | SGLD hyperparameters must be tuned per architecture. Susceptibility magnitudes are comparable only within one probe setup ([Baker et al., ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.18274)) |
| 2–3 | Representational similarity at symmetry-matched sites (Procrustes, debiased CKA, mutual k-NN, direct-matching stitching) | Comparable if sites share invariance class | CKA is dominated by top PCs and manipulable; biased at high width/low samples; layer-max inflates scores ([Ding et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.01661); [Murphy et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01012v1); [Gröger et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14486)) |
| 3 | Activation/gradient structure: bias-corrected participation ratio, functional modularity, NTK change from init, TRE compositionality | Single-network descriptors avoid correspondence | Upstream vs downstream modularity disagree ([Lange et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11815v1)); naive participation ratio is biased ([Chun et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.26560v1)) |
| 4 | SAE/crosscoder feature counts, superposition ψ, training-data attribution | Shared data, corroborative only | Instrument-dependent: ψ is "best suited for comparisons under identical settings" ([Bereska et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.13568v1)); L1 crosscoders make shared concepts look model-specific ([Minder et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02922v2)) |
| Within-topology only | Raw Hessian sharpness, linear mode connectivity, weight-graph clusterability without masked nulls | Live in a parameter space the topology defines | Sharpness is not reparameterization-invariant ([Dinh et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04933)); even adaptive sharpness predicts generalization poorly ([Andriushchenko et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07011)) |

Several concrete pitfalls matter specifically for topology comparisons. Equal accuracy does not mean equal function. Wide and deep networks with similar accuracy "make distinctive errors," so per-example disagreement measured against a within-topology seed baseline is the minimum function-space readout ([Nguyen, Raghu & Kornblith, ICLR 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.15327)). Global CKA heatmaps partly measure one dominant principal component. Block structure is driven by a few "dominant datapoints," varies by seed, and disappears when those points are excluded ([Nguyen et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07184)). In one test, about 97% of BERT's last-layer principal components had to be deleted before CKA moved past the seed baseline, while probe accuracy fell from 80% to 63%. Orthogonal Procrustes "attained consistently good performance" ([Ding, Denain & Steinhardt, NeurIPS 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.01661)). No measure dominates across a benchmark of 24 measures and 14 architectures ([ReSi, ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00531v2)). When the generating architecture was known, similarity metrics did not reliably recover it ([Han, Poggio & Cheung, ICML 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06677v1)). After permutation calibration, claims that models converge on a shared global representation "largely disappear," while local neighbourhood agreement survives ([Gröger et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14486)). Task-loss-matching stitching produces out-of-distribution activations, with OOD detectors reaching AUROC near 1, and picks the corresponding layer only 63.75% of the time on ResNet-18 ([Balogh & Jelasity, AAAI 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.11299v1)).

Ablations need topology-aware baselines. Zero-ablating a residual branch leaves the identity path intact, while zero-ablating a plain layer cuts all flow, so raw ablation effect sizes are not comparable across graphs. The notes recommend resample ablation on the natural distribution and normalizing to a matched path-set ablation. Residual networks are path ensembles in which single lesions have small effects that do not imply the absence of function ([Veit et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06431v2)). Because residual and dense graphs have more parallel paths, the dormant-pathway illusion is plausibly larger there. Subspace findings should therefore be confirmed with projection ablation on the natural distribution, not only with interchange interventions. For weight-graph modularity, parallel topologies impose block-diagonal structure by design. "Topology X is more modular" is tautological unless the null shuffles weights while preserving the architecture's connectivity mask, extending the shuffle control of [Filan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03386). The notes found no paper that tests modularity metrics against topology-preserving nulls.

A small set of readouts has documented, large responses to connectivity and is cheap to compute. Lesion, swap, and parallelization curves ([Sun et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.09298v1); [Lad et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19384v1)) show how computation is distributed over depth. The gradient-alignment cosine cos(F_i(h_i), −∇_h L) operationalizes iterative refinement ([Jastrzębski et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1710.04773)). Jacobian singular-vector alignment across depth ([Residual Alignment](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.09018v1)) and per-layer effective rank ([Huh et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10427)) track geometry. Progressive NC1 = Tr(Σ_W)/Tr(Σ_B) by layer ([Rangamani et al., ICML 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/rangamani23a.html)) tracks class structure. Developmental readouts add *when*. The NTK changes rapidly during a chaotic transient of about 2–3 epochs that fixes the final basin ([Fort et al., NeurIPS 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.15110v1)), kernels can align silently while loss is flat ([Atanasov et al., ICLR 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00034v1)), and sparse-parity learning shows hidden progress invisible to the loss ([Barak et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08799)). Measuring kernel *change* relative to initialization factors out each topology's prior kernel, which differs even at init.

## Valid inference means eliminating the boring explanations first

Every well-known re-evaluation of architecture claims found that most of the claimed gain disappeared under equal tuning, shared implementations, and counted seed variance. Properly regularized LSTMs beat NAS cells and RHNs, and tuning alone was worth about 20 perplexity points on PTB ([Melis, Dyer & Blunsom 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.05589)). "Most models can reach similar scores with enough hyperparameter optimization and random restarts" ([Lucic et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.10337v4)). Most Transformer modifications did not transfer across implementations, and ReZero, Fixup, and parameter sharing hurt ([Narang et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11972)). Claimed metric-learning gains of over 100% shrank to a roughly flat trend ([Musgrave et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.08505v3)). Efficient-training gains vanished against a baseline with a fully decayed learning rate ([Kaddour et al., NeurIPS 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06440v1)). These are accuracy claims. Structure claims are probably more fragile, because the metrics are less standardized, and the notes found no published estimate of the seed variance of structural metrics.

The cleanest way to organize a topology study is Platt's strong inference: list the competing hypotheses, then design crucial experiments whose outcomes eliminate some of them ([Platt 1964](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/philosophy/epistemology/1964-platt.pdf)). For "topology T causes structure S," the notes identify six rivals to the mechanistic hypothesis. **Effective learning rate:** T changes the per-layer update size. This is removed by per-arm LR tuning and μP-style multipliers. **Lazy–rich regime:** T shifts how far features move. This is removed by an output- or init-scale sweep and by matching kernel movement. **Effective depth:** T changes the path-length distribution. This is removed by depth-matched controls. **Capacity:** T changes expressivity despite equal parameter count. This is removed by showing that both arms fit the same targets. **Metric artifact:** removed by multi-family metrics with nulls. **Seed noise:** removed by paired seeds and power analysis. The regime confound is the most dangerous. Lazy training comes from "a choice of scaling, often implicit" ([Chizat, Oyallon & Bach 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.07956)). Initialization scale controls the kernel-to-rich transition ([Woodworth et al., COLT 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09277)). The feature-learning rate is set by parameters that "scale the network output" ([Kumar et al., ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06110)). Weight norm can induce grokking on non-algorithmic tasks or eliminate it on algorithmic ones ([Omnigrok](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01117)). Composed and parallel graphs differ in effective output scale at the same nominal initialization, so a topology effect must be shown at a matched regime, or mapped as a two-dimensional phase diagram of topology × scale.

Fair tuning treats topology as the scientific variable and optimizer settings as nuisances, optimized separately for each arm. "We can only fairly compare models with different numbers of hidden layers if the learning rate is tuned separately" ([Google Tuning Playbook](https://github.com/google-research/tuning_playbook)). The playbook distinguishes trial variance (seeds) from study variance (the search itself) and calls study variance the main concern. Expected best performance as a function of the number of trials can be computed from trials already run. Rankings reverse with budget: on SST-5, logistic regression beats a CNN below about 10 trials ([Dodge et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.03004v1)). In a multiverse analysis of "SGD beats Adam," learning rate explained almost all variance. "A claim is robust if it holds across a region of the search space, not just at a single tuned configuration" ([Bell et al., NeurIPS 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.05985)). μP keeps optimal hyperparameters stable across width ([Tensor Programs V](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.03466)). Depth-μP does the same across depth for one-layer residual blocks, but the authors report "fundamental limitations in all possible infinite-depth limits" for multi-layer blocks ([Tensor Programs VI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02244)). The notes found no μP result for parallel-branch or mixed serial/parallel graphs.

The statistics follow from published variance studies. Data sampling is "the most important source of variance in all case studies." Weight initialization is generally under half of bootstrap variance, and the HPO procedure adds variance comparable to initialization ([Bouthillier et al., MLSys 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.03098v1)). The same study recommends randomizing every variance source, pairing arms on the same seeds and splits, and concluding A>B only when the bootstrap interval for P(A>B) has a lower bound above 0.5 and an upper bound above 0.75. It derives **about 29 runs per arm** to detect P(A>B) ≥ 0.75 at α = β = 0.05, and more than 500 runs below 0.6. Its simulations show that comparing averages at k = 50 gives about 90% false negatives, against about 30% for the probability-of-improvement test. Two groups of five seeds with identical hyperparameters produced "significantly" different curves at p = 0.0016 ([Henderson et al., AAAI 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.06560v3)). Over 10,000 seeds, a ResNet9 on CIFAR-10 spans 1.82 points best to worst ([Picard 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.08203v2)). With few runs, interquartile means and interval estimates change conclusions ([Agarwal et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13264)). The unit of analysis for an architecture-to-structure claim is the training run. Within-run confidence intervals over neurons or examples say nothing about run-to-run variability.

Alignment points matter as much as seeds. Structure emerges in phases (memorization, then circuit formation, then cleanup), so the checkpoint changes the answer ([Nanda et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05217)), and "deciding when training is complete" is a core unsolved benchmarking problem ([Dahl et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07179)). Reporting structure at matched steps, at several matched training-loss thresholds, and at the end of a fully decayed schedule separates "topology changes speed" from "topology changes the solution." If structure-versus-loss trajectories overlap across topologies, only speed differs. Finally, the best ML exemplars test mechanisms in both directions. Omnigrok induced and eliminated grokking, Nanda validated the mechanism with Fourier-basis ablations, and Toy Models of Superposition checked empirical phase boundaries against closed-form losses ([Elhage et al. 2022](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/toy_model/index.html)). Measures should be judged on worst-case robustness across environments, not on averages ([Dziugaite et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11924)).

## Recommendations for the topology-design plan

### The parallel arm is a two-layer network, which anchors it and narrows the novelty

One implication of the plan's own definitions changes how Phase 2 should be framed. The research notes do not state it; it follows from the SPEC's definitions. In the parent-selection family with ReLU MLP blocks, the parallel graph computes h_L = x + Σ_i W₂⁽ⁱ⁾ ReLU(W₁⁽ⁱ⁾ x), which is exactly **one two-layer ReLU network of width L·m plus an identity skip**. The sequential graph is a depth-L residual network. At matched parameters, the flagship contrast is therefore a matched-parameter shallow-wide versus deep-residual comparison. That cuts both ways. It anchors the parallel arm to mature theory: mean-field two-layer dynamics, the merged-staircase condition, and leap complexity ([Abbe et al. 2022](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v178/abbe22a.html)), with max-margin limits in the variation-norm space ([Chizat & Bach 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.04486)). Phase 2 can then *predict* the parallel arm's plateau structure on each Boolean target before training, which is exactly the SPEC's "derive before you measure" rule. It also means the existing two- versus three-layer separations ([Nichani et al.](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/236b6a814a1d2c0ff504ca7bf380f7ff-Abstract-Conference.html); [Allen-Zhu & Li](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10337)) already occupy the sample-complexity version of H2. What stays new is the *dynamical* endpoint (the lag between learning constituents and their composite), its scaling with richness, and especially the one-edge mutants. Mutants that interpolate between the two arms turn a depth-versus-width comparison into a genuine topology dose–response. The write-up should state this reduction openly, or a reviewer will.

This also refines the meaning of "parameter-free" across phases. In the linear setting Prop. 2 shows the parallel graph has d² effective parameters and is convex. The nonlinear parallel arm is not degenerate in the same way, because each branch has its own hidden units, but its function class equals that of a single wide shallow network. Matched parameter counts therefore hide a structural capacity difference in both regimes. The SPEC's rule to over-provision width until every arm fits is the right guard. Fit tolerance should be reported per arm and per target, and the lag endpoint should be computed only for arms that fit.

### Prioritize four emergent-structure targets that share one experimental grid

The notes rank emergent-structure targets by three criteria: a cheap, reproducible operationalization; existing evidence that connectivity, not just size, moves the target; and theory that predicts direction. Four targets fit the plan's MLP and linear phases, and one fits the tiny-transformer extension.

The first priority is the **order and timing of interaction acquisition**, measured on the Walsh–Fourier atlas. This is already the SPEC's primary instrument. It is Tier 1 valid, and the parallel arm's leap-complexity theory supplies a quantitative baseline. The atlas should be estimated under the stated uniform hypercube measure on centered logits, which the SPEC already specifies. Emergence times should be defined on continuous coefficient trajectories, or reported across a range of thresholds, because thresholded "emergence" is what manufactures mirages ([Schaeffer et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.15004)).

The second priority is the **layerwise computation profile**: lesion, swap, and parallelization curves, the gradient-alignment cosine, and Jacobian singular-vector alignment across depth. These are the readouts with the largest documented connectivity effects ([Residual Alignment](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.09018v1); [Jastrzębski](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1710.04773); [Raghu](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.08810)), they cost almost nothing, and they directly test whether the sequential arm re-represents (constructs) while the parallel arm only sums.

The third priority is **effective rank and singular-value structure**. Here Phase 0 can derive the answer before measuring it. Boix-Adsera's model W_u(I+W_1)…(I+W_L)W_e with weight decay on the W_i is, apart from its learned embedding and unembedding, the sequential parent-selection chain with weight decay toward identity, the case Phase 0 §8.3 shows breaks balancedness. Prop. 2 shows the parallel graph under the same decay is ridge regression toward I with strength λ/L. The weight-decay endpoint contrast is therefore a log-type penalty on large singular values for the chain versus a Frobenius penalty toward identity for the parallel graph. That is closed-form at infinite depth ([Boix-Adsera](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19149)) and checkable at finite L. Rank measurements should use the bias-corrected participation ratio at matched sample counts ([Chun et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.26560v1)).

The fourth priority is **branch symmetry and specialization**, measured as per-branch output share, ablate-and-rescue, and functional (not weight-graph) modularity. It is riskier because the evidence says parallel routes collapse rather than specialize ([Alimaskina 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03483); [Béna & Goodman 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/natcom/v16y2025i1d10.1038_s41467-024-55188-9.html)). But that same evidence makes a sharp, falsifiable prediction (see the predictions table).

For the tiny-transformer extension in Phase 4, **induction-head emergence** is the best single target. Formation requires cross-layer K-composition. A 1-layer solution needs h·m·p = Ω(n) while 2 layers need O(1) heads and dimension ([Sanford, Hsu & Telgarsky](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.14332v1)), so putting two attention blocks in parallel, both reading the embedding, should block or sharply delay formation. Phase-change step and prefix-matching score are well-defined readouts ([Singh et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07129)), and the notes found no published sequential-versus-parallel experiment. Progressive neural collapse and grokking delay are cheap secondary targets with open niches. Superposition counts should stay deferred, consistent with the SPEC's exclusion of SAE pipelines, because they are instrument-dependent.

### Build the measurement stack in tiers, with topology-aware nulls at every tier

The SPEC's instrument table is close to what the notes recommend, and a few additions would make each tier defensible. At Tier 1, add per-example soft and hard output agreement against a within-arm seed baseline, so a topology effect is expressed as between-arm distance relative to within-arm spread. Add purified functional ANOVA alongside the Walsh atlas for any non-Boolean inputs.

For Phase 4 causal localization, the SPEC already requires validation on Tracr or InterpBench. It should also fix a single restricted map class (linear or orthogonal DAS at fixed rank) for all arms, report IIA on held-out pairs against random-init versions of each topology, report map complexity, and confirm found subspaces with natural-distribution projection ablation. Without the restricted maps and random-init baselines, IIA can be vacuous ([Sutter et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.08802)).

For the LLC, tune the SGLD step size and localization per topology and report diagnostics. Before using the LLC as a stage detector in nonlinear arms, calibrate it in Phase 0 against the exact plain-chain RLCT from [Pepin Lehalleur & Rimányi](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.19920), following [Furman & Lau](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03698v1). For representational comparisons, compare only symmetry-matched sites: residual stream with residual stream, post-ReLU with post-ReLU. Report Procrustes, debiased CKA, and mutual k-NN with permutation nulls corrected for layer search, and pair every score with linear decodability of the known Boolean intermediates g₁, g₂.

For Phase 3 coherence, log NTK change relative to initialization. In the linear chain, Prop. 4 makes the kernel a function of W, so only deviations from that closed form are informative. Weight-graph modularity, if used at all, needs mask-preserving shuffles. Raw sharpness and linear mode connectivity should be confined to within-arm "solution uniqueness" statistics.

Finally, ablation effects in the parent-selection family need care even though every graph has the identity path. Zeroing a branch in the parallel arm removes one additive term, while zeroing an early branch in the sequential arm also changes the inputs to every later branch. Effects should be normalized to a matched ablation of all branches at the same depth, using resample ablation on the natural distribution.

### Tighten the inference protocol where the SPEC is thinnest

The SPEC already requires per-arm LR tuning, richness sweeps, width over-provisioning, two clocks, an identical-graph null, and an operator-label permutation. These map directly onto the notes' checklist. Five changes would bring it to the standard the replication literature implies.

First, raise seed counts. The SPEC's floor of five paired seeds is the weakest level the notes support. For toy-scale experiments, run a pilot of 50–100 seeds of one arm to measure the null distribution of the primary endpoint (the composite lag). Then use about 10 paired seeds for reported results and about 29 per arm for the flagship headline, analyzed with the P(A>B) criterion and bootstrap over runs ([Bouthillier et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.03098v1)). Randomize data seeds independently of init seeds, since data sampling dominates variance.

Second, make the richness sweep a phase diagram with a built-in control. Prop. 1 predicts the topology effect vanishes as branch weights stay small, so the paired effect must go to zero as α → 0 or initialization scale → 0, and grow monotonically with richness. A topology effect that persists at vanishing richness indicates a bug, an effective-LR artifact, or a confound. Log kernel movement and weight norm alongside, so arms can be compared at matched feature-learning regime, not just matched nominal α ([Kumar et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06110); [Woodworth et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09277)). One nuance needs explicit handling. Prop. 1 holds at zero branch weights. At small *random* initialization the arms' kernels already differ at O(ε), and with standard random initialization connectivity changes the kernel outright ([Huang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06262)). The richness axis should therefore be reported separately for zero-branch and random-branch initialization.

Third, use dose–response and reversal designs. The one-edge mutants should be ordered by a graph statistic, such as the length of the longest chain feeding the output or the path-length distribution ([Veit et al.](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2016/hash/37bc2f75bf1bcfe8450a1a41c200364c-Abstract.html)), so that H2 predicts a monotone trend rather than an endpoint difference. Add a random-routing control with the same operators and parameters, to separate "this routing" from "any routing change." Following the Omnigrok pattern of inducing and eliminating an effect, the program should show it can *restore* the composite advantage in a parallel arm by adding the single edge that creates the relevant composition, and *remove* it from a sequential arm by cutting that edge.

Fourth, report tuning-budget robustness. Give every arm the same search algorithm and budget. Report isolation plots and Dodge-style expected-best-versus-trials curves for both loss and the structural endpoint, confirm each LR optimum is interior to the sweep, and report results at each arm's optimum *and* at a shared LR ([Dodge et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.03004v1); [Bell et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.05985)).

Fifth, preregister kill criteria in the form the notes suggest. For example: "If the topology effect on the lag is below 2 seed standard deviations, flips sign across the top three LR points, or disappears at matched training loss, H2 is rejected for this target." Name in advance which outcome would favor the regime hypothesis over the mechanism, such as the effect vanishing once kernel movement is matched. Confine claims to one or two preregistered primary endpoints and treat other metrics as exploratory with Holm or Benjamini–Hochberg correction, which is general statistical practice rather than something the ML sources prescribe. Use a fully decayed schedule in every arm ([Kaddour et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06440v1)). If Phase 4 introduces tiny transformers, normalization placement must be fixed or crossed as a factor, because Pre-LN versus Post-LN flips which layers become redundant ([Sun et al., NeurIPS 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05795v3)).

One Phase 4 point deserves emphasis. The SPEC predicts that composed stacks erase more slowly than shortcut-rich graphs, based on Corollary 3a. The empirical "skips preserve information" literature concerns the *identity* coefficient, which the parent-selection family fixes at 1, not learned shortcut branches. Adding a fixed, decaying identity coefficient α_l as a second parameter-free knob, as Zhang et al. do, would let Phase 4 separate erasure through the identity path from erasure through shortcut branches. These are distinct mechanisms that the current design conflates.

### Spend novelty claims on the linear-DAG problem, RLCT, and the dynamical flagship

Some of the plan's results should be presented as clean restatements, not discoveries. Prop. 1's first-order universality is new in its parent-selection phrasing, but its message, that topology cannot act through local invariants, is the content of [Marcotte et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06194), which should be cited as corroboration. Prop. 2's parallel collapse is an exact linear instance of Ziyin's permutation-symmetry collapse and of multi-branch convexification results. Prop. 3 and Prop. 4 are Arora-style balanced-chain results. Corollary 3a's orientation lock needs checking against [Bartlett, Helmbold & Long 2018](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/bartlett18a.html) before it is claimed. The softmax-gauge escape through null(R) and the cross-entropy setting may still be new.

Three contributions look genuinely open and fit the program's tools.

The first is a **gradient-flow implicit-bias theory for linear parent-selection DAGs**, starting with Prop. 7's coupled (P, W₃) flow. Available machinery includes intrinsic path-space dynamics ([Marcotte 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07370)), the commuting test for mirror-flow equivalence ([Li et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.04036)), and initialization-shape effects ([Azulay et al. 2021](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/azulay21a.html)). The weight-decay counterpart would extend Boix-Adsera's singular-value decomposition argument to DAGs whose path sums are simultaneously diagonalizable. Classifying which parent vectors give commuting liftings would be a crisp, self-contained result even if the general case resists.

The second is the **RLCT of residual and parallel parameterizations** of the same function class. The fiber-codimension method transfers directly. The notes' conjecture is that residual maps near identity targets are *less* singular than plain chains, so their RLCT there is larger. The LLC estimator then gives an empirical check. This connects Phase 0 to the SPEC's use of the LLC as a stage detector and would be the first singular-learning-theory result for non-chain topologies.

The third is the **flagship dynamical study**, reframed honestly as described above: lag, richness scaling, one-edge mutants, and reversal experiments, with the parallel arm anchored to two-layer theory. A matched-parameter sequential-versus-parallel transformer study with internal-representation readouts and induction-head timing is a strong secondary niche. The notes found no study that trains the two arrangements at matched parameters and FLOPs and compares internal representations.

## Conclusion

The literature changes what "topology changes emergent structure" can mean. Because residual blocks share conservation laws with their skip-free versions and every parent-selection graph agrees to first order at zero, a topology effect cannot be a static property of the graph. It is a *dynamical* property of how far learning travels through a composed parameter map. That makes richness, not topology alone, the independent variable, and it makes the strongest possible result a moving phase boundary: an effect that is zero in the lazy limit and grows in a predicted direction as features move. It also explains why so many empirical routing claims are fragile. They report one richness setting, one tuning point, and one alignment clock, where theory says topology effects are second-order and regime-gated.

For this program, the most consequential reframing is that matched-parameter parallel-versus-sequential comparisons are partly depth-versus-width comparisons in disguise. That is a liability for novelty claims and an asset for prediction: the parallel arm comes with an exact two-layer theory, and every deviation of the sequential arm from it is attributable to composition. The program's comparative advantage lies in the combination the literature lacks: exact linear theory for general residual DAGs, a singular-learning-theory account of how routing changes parameter degeneracy, and dose–response experiments over one-edge mutants that are powered, per-arm tuned, and preregistered. If H2 fails cleanly under that protocol, the field learns that composition helps through expressivity and sample complexity but not through learning order. That would itself be a sharper statement than anything currently published.
