# Theory of how network connectivity/topology shapes learning dynamics, implicit bias, and converged solutions

Scope note: this covers theory connecting architecture graph structure (skip/residual connections, parallel vs composed branches, dense or other fixed routing, tensor-network/DAG views) to gradient-descent dynamics, implicit bias and learned structure. Items the project already knows (Arora/Cohen/Hazan 2018; Arora et al. 2019; Du/Hu/Lee 2018; Saxe 2014; Saxe et al. 2022; Jarvis; Kunin 2021/2024; Dominé 2025; Marcotte 2023; Woodworth 2020; Gunasekar 2018; Soudry 2018; Ji & Telgarsky 2019; Lippl et al.; Abbe 2023; Bordelon 2024; Tensor Programs VI) appear only where newer work links to them. ## Q1. Which works analyze implicit bias or learning dynamics of networks with skip/residual connections specifically (linear and nonlinear)?

### Takeaway
Residual theory falls into five strands: (i) landscape and convergence results for linear ResNets (identity initialization, no spurious minima); (ii) infinite-depth/neural-ODE limits, where gradient flow provably keeps weights smooth across depth, with the residual scaling (1/L vs 1/√L) deciding the regime; (iii) representation-cost (weight-decay) characterizations, where a linear ResNet's bias lies *between nuclear norm and rank* and nonlinear ResNets favor low *bottleneck rank*; (iv) signal-propagation/NTK results, where skips prevent kernel degeneracy and keep the backward-to-feature angle non-degenerate; (v) path-based analyses (path norms for DAGs, Veit-style path ensembles). No paper I found gives an implicit-bias characterization of *unregularized gradient flow* for a general linear residual network with small or identity initialization comparable to Arora et al. 2019 for chains. That is the clearest gap.

### Cited Findings

**Linear ResNets: landscape and convergence**
- Hardt & Ma, "Identity Matters in Deep Learning" (ICLR 2017; arXiv 1611.04231). **[PROVED]** Linear residual networks of any depth have no spurious local optima. The authors call the proof "strikingly simple" next to the "substantially more delicate" plain-chain case. For ReLU residual nets they also prove finite-sample expressivity when #params > #samples. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.04231). *Use:* this is the baseline landscape fact that skip topology reparameterizes around the identity. A new topology result has to go beyond "no bad minima."
- Bartlett, Helmbold & Long, "Gradient descent with identity initialization efficiently learns positive definite linear transformations by deep residual networks" (ICML 2018, PMLR v80). **[PROVED, linear, from memory: GD from identity init converges in polynomial time for positive-definite / near-identity targets and can fail for targets with negative eigenvalues.]** — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/bartlett18a.html). *Use:* it bounds which targets an identity-initialized residual chain reaches easily. This predicts topology-dependent failure modes (for example a reflection target) that a parallel or plain topology might not share.
- Zou, Long & Gu, "On the Global Convergence of Training Deep Linear ResNets" (ICLR 2020; arXiv 2003.01094). **[PROVED, linear; detail from memory: GD with zero init of residual blocks plus suitable input/output transformations converges globally with width requirements independent of depth, in contrast to plain deep linear nets.]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.01094); [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2020/poster/1718). *Use:* a convergence-rate comparison between residual and plain chains at equal parameter count is a known result. Novelty has to come from *which solution* is reached, not from whether training converges.
- Marion & Chizat, "Deep linear networks for regression are implicitly regularized towards flat minima" (arXiv 2405.13456, 2024; NeurIPS 2024 per my recollection, unverified). **[PROVED, deep linear, univariate regression, gradient flow]** The lower bound on minimizer sharpness grows linearly with depth. Gradient flow finds a minimizer within a constant factor of that bound, and the constant does not depend on width or depth. With small-scale init the weights become rank-one and aligned. Convergence is also proved for a *residual* Gaussian init. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13456). *Use:* this is a direct template for "implicit bias of the residual vs small-init parameterization" in linear regression. One could test whether the sharpness constant changes with topology (parallel vs composed).

**Infinite-depth / neural-ODE limits**
- Marion, Wu, Sander & Biau, "Implicit regularization of deep residual networks towards neural ODEs" (ICLR 2024; arXiv 2309.01213). **[PROVED, nonlinear 2-layer-MLP residual branches, 1/L scaling, (clipped) gradient flow, weight-tied/smooth init]** As L→∞ the trained network converges to a neural ODE uniformly on finite time horizons (Thm 4). Under a PL condition (width linear in n) it converges to an interpolating neural ODE as t, L→∞ (Thm 6). **[EMPIRICAL]** ReLU or i.i.d. init loses smoothness. **[OPEN, stated by authors]** For i.i.d. init the limit should be an SDE, and showing that GF selects a minimum-complexity ODE is called "an interesting next step." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.01213); [ICLR proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/b29a95e7d9f1e1a6dfc567b556733744-Abstract-Conference.html). *Use:* "smoothness across depth" is a topology-specific emergent structure. Prediction: residual chains with weight-tied init keep inter-layer weight differences O(1/L), while plain chains have no such structure.
- Sander, Ablin & Peyré, "Do Residual Neural Networks discretize Neural ODEs?" (NeurIPS 2022; arXiv 2205.14612). **[PROVED for linear residual case, per Marion et al.'s citation of it]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14612). *Use:* the linear precursor to the result above.
- Marion et al., "Scaling ResNets in the Large-depth Regime" (JMLR 2025; arXiv 2206.06929). Relates the residual scaling factor α_L and init regularity to the limiting object (ODE vs SDE vs degenerate). — [JMLR](https://www.jmlr.org/beta/papers/v26/22-0664.html). *Use:* the residual scale is a hidden hyperparameter that can masquerade as a "topology effect." Experiments should control it.
- Hayou & Yang, "Width and Depth Limits Commute in Residual Networks" (ICML 2023, PMLR v202). Extended as "Commutative Scaling of Width and Depth in Deep Neural Networks" (JMLR v25, 2024). **[PROVED, 1/√depth branch scaling, at init/NNGP level]** — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/hayou23a.html); [JMLR](https://www.jmlr.org/beta/papers/v25/23-1163.html). *Use:* with 1/√L scaling, residual networks have a well-defined joint limit that plain nets lack, so skip topology changes which asymptotic theory applies.
- Bordelon, Noci, Li, Hanin & Pehlevan, "Depthwise Hyperparameter Transfer in Residual Networks: Dynamics and Scaling Limit" (arXiv 2309.16620; ICLR 2024 per my recollection). **[DERIVED via DMFT]** 1/√depth branch scale plus μP gives a feature-learning joint infinite-width-and-depth limit. **[EMPIRICAL]** HP transfer on CIFAR-10/ImageNet ResNets and ViTs. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16620). *Use:* this gives a DMFT toolbox to extend to non-chain DAGs (parallel branches, dense skips). Each DAG has its own kernel recursion.
- Lu, Ma, Lu, Lu & Ying, "A Mean-field Analysis of Deep ResNet and Beyond" (ICML 2020; arXiv 2003.05508). **[PROVED: mean-field over depth+width, global convergence for an idealized ResNet; details from memory]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05508). *Use:* depth-as-measure formulations exist and could be generalized to DAGs.
- Chizat & Netrapalli, "The Feature Speed Formula" (NeurIPS 2024; arXiv 2311.18718). **[PROVED, large width-then-depth, one GD step]** Feature-update size is set by the angle θ_ℓ between feature updates and the backward pass. ReLU MLP with i.i.d. init: cos θ_ℓ = Θ(1/√ℓ), which degenerates. ResNet with branch scale O(1/√depth): cos θ_ℓ = Θ(1). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18718); [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/720e7ebc49c84252abc0754dddf80976-Abstract.html). *Use:* the backward-to-feature angle is a computable per-layer diagnostic for any DAG. Prediction: topologies whose layer-to-layer Jacobians are better conditioned (more short paths) keep feature learning at depth.

**Representation cost / regularized inductive bias**
- Boix-Adsera, "On the inductive bias of infinite-depth ResNets and the bottleneck rank" (arXiv 2501.19149, Jan 2025). **[PROVED]** Linear ResNet f = W_u(I+W_1)…(I+W_L)W_e with weight decay. The min-cost decomposes over singular values. Depth-1 blocks, infinite depth: c = Σ_i min_α [σ_i e^{−α} + λα²]. Depth-2 blocks: Σ_{σ≤λ} σ + Σ_{σ>λ} λ(1+log(σ/λ)). Large λ recovers the nuclear norm. As λ→0 the cost normalizes to rank. So the bias is "between nuclear norm and rank." For ReLU ResNets (infinite depth, λ→0) the normalized cost is sandwiched between Jacobian rank and bottleneck rank. **[Caveat stated by author]** The optimization dynamics and the training regime (e.g., no weight decay) can change the true bias. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19149); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19149v1). *Use:* this is the cleanest closed-form contrast available: plain deep linear chain (Schatten-2/L quasi-norm, standard result) vs residual chain (log-like penalty on singular values above λ). Prediction for the project: residual topology penalizes *large* singular values only logarithmically, so it should be less low-rank-biased than a plain chain at matched depth and weight decay. The formula can be extended to parallel and dense DAGs.
- Jacot & Kaiser, "Hamiltonian Mechanics of Feature Learning: Bottleneck Structure in Leaky ResNets" (CPAL 2025, PMLR v280; arXiv 2405.17573, v3 Mar 2026). Leaky ResNets interpolate ResNet ↔ fully connected through an "effective depth" L̃. Minimum-norm "representation geodesics" have a Lagrangian/Hamiltonian form: kinetic energy (small layer derivatives) vs potential energy ("Cost of Identity", which favors low-dimensional representations). Large L̃ gives a bottleneck structure. **[Mostly DERIVED/HEURISTIC per the abstract's framing]** — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v280/jacot25a.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17573). *Use:* skip strength enters as one continuous knob (L̃). A sweep over skip strength should show a transition to bottleneck structure. This is a direct testable prediction for "how much identity routing."
- Jacot, "Implicit Bias of Large Depth Networks: a Notion of Rank for Nonlinear Functions" (ICLR 2023 oral; arXiv 2209.15055) and "Bottleneck Structure in Learned Features" (NeurIPS 2023; arXiv 2305.19008). **[PROVED for L2-regularized representation cost, large depth, plain chains]** Cost/L → bottleneck rank. — [NeurIPS PDF](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/4a6695df88f2de0d49f875189ea181ef-Paper-Conference.pdf); [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/oral/12530); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19008v3). *Use:* the plain-chain baseline for the Boix-Adsera residual comparison.

**Signal propagation / NTK with skips**
- Yang & Schoenholz, "Mean Field Residual Networks: On the Edge of Chaos" (NeurIPS 2017). **[DERIVED, mean-field at init]** Residual nets show subexponential/polynomial dynamics instead of exponential, and so sit near the edge of chaos without fine tuning (from memory). — [NeurIPS PDF](https://papers.NeurIPS.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/file/81c650caac28cdefce4de5ddc18befa0-Paper.pdf). 
- Tarnowski et al., "Dynamical Isometry is Achieved in Residual Networks in a Universal Way for any Activation Function" (AISTATS 2019, PMLR v89). **[PROVED via free probability, at init]** — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v89/tarnowski19a.html).
- Huang, Wang, Tao & Zhao, "Why Do Deep Residual Networks Generalize Better than Deep Feedforward Networks? — A NTK Perspective" (NeurIPS 2020; arXiv 2002.06262). **[PROVED, infinite width, NTK]** The deep feedforward NTK function class degenerates, becoming asymptotically unlearnable as depth→∞, while the ResNet NTK does not. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06262); [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/1c336b8080f82bcc2cd2499b4c57261d-Abstract.html). *Use:* in the lazy regime, topology changes the kernel spectrum. This is a lazy-regime null hypothesis to separate from rich-regime topology effects.
- Chen, Huang, Gong, Hanin & Wang, "Deep Architecture Connectivity Matters for Its Convergence: A Fine-Grained Analysis" (arXiv 2205.05662, 2022; NeurIPS 2022 per my recollection). **[PROVED at wide-network NNGP level]** Tracks how the NNGP kernel spectrum propagates through a *given connectivity pattern (DAG)* and bounds convergence rates. Used to prune NAS candidates. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05662). *Use:* this is prior art for "DAG → kernel spectrum → convergence speed." The project's novelty has to sit in the rich/feature-learning regime or in implicit bias, not in the lazy convergence rate.
- Dong, Cordonnier & Loukas, "Attention is Not All You Need: Pure Attention Loses Rank Doubly Exponentially with Depth" (ICML 2021). **[PROVED]** Without skip connections (and MLPs), self-attention outputs converge doubly exponentially to rank 1. Skip connections prevent this (path decomposition). — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/dong21a.html). *Use:* a clean path-decomposition argument showing skip topology changes the rank structure of forward representations. Expect this to transfer to other token-mixing operators.

**Path-based analyses**
- Veit, Wilber & Belongie, "Residual Networks Behave Like Ensembles of Relatively Shallow Networks" (NeurIPS 2016). **[EMPIRICAL + combinatorial path counting]** Paths in a residual net follow a binomial length distribution, and gradient mainly flows through short paths. — [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2016/hash/37bc2f75bf1bcfe8450a1a41c200364c-Abstract.html). *Use:* the path-length distribution is a topology statistic you can compute directly from any DAG. It is a candidate order parameter for "effective depth."
- Neyshabur, Salakhutdinov & Srebro, "Path-SGD" (NeurIPS 2015). An optimizer invariant to node-wise rescaling, built from the path-regularizer geometry. — [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2015/hash/eaa32c96f620053cf442ad32258076b9-Abstract.html).
- Gonon, Brisebarre, Riccietti & Gribonval, "A path-norm toolkit for modern networks" (ICLR 2024; arXiv 2310.01225). **[PROVED]** Path-norm generalization bounds that "fully encompass general DAG ReLU networks with biases, skip connections," max-pooling and GroupSort. The path-norms are rescaling-invariant and computable. **[EMPIRICAL challenge]** The bounds evaluated on ImageNet ResNets are far from tight. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01225); [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2024/poster/18089). *Use:* the right formal object for DAG topologies, since the *path-lifting* φ(θ) is defined for any DAG. Path-norm at convergence can be used as a measured "emergent structure" that compares across topologies at fixed parameter count.
- Cai, Zhou, Wu, Mei, Lindsey & Bartlett, "Implicit Bias of Gradient Descent for Non-Homogeneous Deep Networks" (ICML 2025, PMLR v267; arXiv 2502.16075). **[PROVED, exponential loss, GD, once empirical risk is small enough, "mild near-homogeneity"]** Normalized margin increases almost monotonically, the iterates converge in direction, and the limit satisfies the KKT conditions of a margin-maximization problem. The abstract says this covers *residual connections* and non-homogeneous activations, which resolves an open problem from Ji & Telgarsky 2020. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16075); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/cai25m.html). *Use:* the late-phase classification bias of residual nets is "KKT of max-margin" in the network's own parametrization. So the topology enters only through *which margin problem* (the geometry of the parameter→function map). That map is what to characterize.
- Shamir, "Are ResNets Provably Better than Linear Predictors?" (NeurIPS 2018; arXiv 1804.06739). **[PROVED, from memory: for a residual block with skip to output, every local minimum is no worse than the best linear predictor, under mild assumptions]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06739).
- Allen-Zhu & Li, "What Can ResNet Learn Efficiently, Going Beyond Kernels?" (NeurIPS 2019; arXiv 1905.10337). **[PROVED, distribution-free, 3-layer ResNet, smooth activation]** SGD on ResNets efficiently learns a concept class (three-layer residual functions) with sample complexity that no kernel method, including the NTK, can match. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10337); [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/hash/5857d68cd9280bc98d079fa912fd6740-Abstract.html). *Use:* the skip structure ("simple base + small composed correction") is what the proof exploits. A target with that structure predicts residual > plain at matched params.

**Diagnostics on DAGs**
- Bolshim & Kugaevskikh, "Inter-Layer Hessian Analysis of Neural Networks with DAG Architectures" (arXiv 2604.11639, Apr 2026, preprint). Block decomposition of the Hessian indexed by the architecture DAG (H = H^GN + H^T). For ReLU, the input-level tensor term vanishes a.e. The authors claim to explain exponential decay of inter-layer "resonance" in plain nets and its preservation under skips. They propose O(P) metrics (resonance, coupling, stable rank, GN-gap). **[Abstract-level only; theorem assumptions unverified]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11639). *Use:* very recent, close to the project's framing (DAG-indexed curvature). Check it for overlap and possibly use its metrics.

### Inferences
- Across these results, skip connections act mainly by (a) moving the origin of the parameterization to the identity, so init sits at a non-degenerate point instead of the saddle at 0, and (b) changing the path-length distribution and Jacobian conditioning. Both are graph-level quantities, and (b) can be computed for any DAG.
- Prediction combining Boix-Adsera with the plain-chain Schatten-2/L result: at matched depth and weight decay, residual linear chains should end with *less* low-rank structure (log vs power-law penalty on large singular values) than plain chains. Small-init residual nets that start near zero instead of identity should act more like plain chains. That separates "topology" from "init location."
- The 1/L vs 1/√L branch scale changes the limiting object (ODE vs SDE vs NTK-like). Any experiment claiming a topology effect has to report and control branch scaling.

### Gaps
- No paper found that characterizes the implicit bias of *unregularized gradient flow* for linear residual nets from small or identity init (analogous to Arora et al. 2019 for chains or Woodworth et al. for diagonal nets). Marion et al. 2024 explicitly leave the "minimum-complexity ODE" selection open.
- Venues for Zou 2020, Bordelon 2024, Marion & Chizat 2024, and Chen 2022 come from memory or partial confirmation.
- I could not fetch the full Bolshim & Kugaevskikh text, so its theorems are unverified.

## Q2. Which works treat architecture as a general DAG/graph or tensor network and derive implicit bias from graph structure? Has anyone characterized implicit bias for general linear residual DAGs or "parent-selection" families?

### Takeaway
There is a solid tensor-network line: Cohen/Shashua expressivity, where connectivity, overlap and dilation-mixing give provable expressive-efficiency gaps; Razin/Cohen implicit regularization, where GD → low (hierarchical) tensor rank → locality in CNNs; and Yun/Krishnan/Mobahi, a unified tensor formulation of linear nets. Group-structured linear nets have closed-form biases (Fourier-space Schatten-2/L). But Yun et al. explicitly do *not* treat branched/parallel networks. I found **no** paper that characterizes implicit bias for a general linear residual DAG or for "parent-selection" families. That niche looks open.

### Cited Findings
- Yun, Krishnan & Mobahi, "A Unifying View on Implicit Bias in Training Linear Neural Networks" (ICLR 2021; arXiv 2010.02501). **[PROVED, gradient flow]** Linear "tensor networks" f(x) = M(x)∘(v_1,…,v_L) cover fully connected, diagonal and (circular) convolutional nets. Separable classification: under directional-convergence and 100%-accuracy assumptions, parameters align with singular vectors of M(−u∞). For orthogonally decomposable networks the limit is a stationary point of an ℓ_{2/L} max-margin problem in a transformed space. Regression: orthogonally decomposable nets converge to the minimizer of a norm-like function that interpolates weighted ℓ1/ℓ2 and depends on init. Fully connected nets go to min-ℓ2 as α→0. **Branched/parallel networks are not addressed.** Woodworth's two-branch diagonal model appears only as related work. **[Correction noted by the authors]** An earlier claim that an assumption was unnecessary in Thm 2 was wrong. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02501); [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2021/poster/2826). *Use:* the natural formalism to extend. A linear DAG is a *sum over paths* of such tensor products, i.e. a sum of tensor networks sharing factors. Characterizing that sum is the open step.
- Razin, Maman & Cohen, "Implicit Regularization in Tensor Factorization" (ICML 2021, PMLR v139; arXiv 2102.09972). **[PROVED dynamical characterization + EMPIRICAL]** Gradient flow on CP tensor factorization from small init learns components incrementally, giving bias toward low tensor rank. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/razin21a.html).
- Razin, Maman & Cohen, "Implicit Regularization in Hierarchical Tensor Factorization and Deep Convolutional Neural Networks" (ICML 2022, PMLR v162; arXiv 2201.11729). **[PROVED via dynamical-systems analysis]** Implicit regularization toward low *hierarchical* tensor rank. Through the equivalence of hierarchical tensor factorization with certain deep (polynomial) CNNs, this gives implicit regularization toward *locality*. **[EMPIRICAL]** An explicit regularizer that counteracts locality improves modern CNNs on non-local tasks. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/razin22a.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.11729). *Use:* the strongest existing example of "connectivity graph (tree of the hierarchical factorization) → specific emergent structure (locality / low rank across certain partitions)." Prediction for DAGs: the bias is toward low rank *across the partitions that the routing graph separates*. Changing routing changes which input partitions are favored.
- Razin, Verbin & Cohen, "On the Ability of Graph Neural Networks to Model Interactions Between Vertices" (NeurIPS 2023; arXiv 2211.16494). **[PROVED, details from memory]** The interaction a GNN can model between a vertex subset and its complement (separation rank) is bounded by the input graph's "walk index." This motivates edge-sparsification by walk index. — [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/543ec10715d964122ab7cb15f648772b-Abstract.html). *Use:* a graph-theoretic quantity (walk counts) that controls a model's interaction capacity. A similar walk/path-count index over the *architecture* DAG is a plausible order parameter.
- Cohen, Sharir & Shashua, "On the Expressive Power of Deep Learning: A Tensor Analysis" (COLT 2016, PMLR v49). **[PROVED]** Deep convolutional arithmetic circuits (hierarchical Tucker) vs shallow (CP): depth efficiency holds for almost all functions (from memory). — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v49/cohen16.html).
- Cohen, Tamari & Shashua, "Boosting Dilated Convolutional Networks with Mixed Tensor Decompositions" (arXiv 1703.06846; ICLR 2018 per my recollection). **[PROVED]** Expressive efficiency from *connectivity*, not depth: interconnecting dilated conv nets of different dilations via mixed tensor decompositions. "Even a single connection between intermediate layers can already lead to an almost quadratic gap." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06846). *Use:* direct prior art that *adding a parameter-free cross-connection* provably changes the representable function class's efficiency. The project's novelty should be dynamics/implicit bias, not expressivity.
- Sharir & Shashua, "On the Expressive Power of Overlapping Architectures of Deep Learning" (arXiv 1703.02065; ICLR 2018). **[PROVED]** Overlapping receptive fields give exponential expressive efficiency (from memory). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.02065v4).
- Lawrence, Georgiev, Dienes & Kiani, "Implicit Bias of Linear Equivariant Networks" (ICML 2022; arXiv 2110.06084). **[PROVED, L-layer full-width linear G-CNNs, GD, binary classification]** Converges to low-rank *Fourier matrix coefficients*, regularized by the 2/L-Schatten norm in group-Fourier space. Holds for all finite groups, including non-commutative ones, and band-limited infinite groups. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06084); [ICML](https://icml.cc/virtual/2022/spotlight/16544). *Use:* the template "fixed linear structure → diagonalizing basis → Schatten-2/L in that basis." For DAGs whose fixed routing commutes with a group, one might get a closed form the same way.
- Gunasekar et al., "Implicit Bias of Gradient Descent on Linear Convolutional Networks" (NeurIPS 2018) — known to the project. Linked here because Yun et al. and Lawrence et al. generalize it. — [alphaxiv](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/1806.00468).
- Kohn, Merkh, Montúfar & Trager, "Geometry of Linear Convolutional Networks" (arXiv 2108.01538, 2021; SIAM J. Appl. Algebra Geom. per my recollection). **[PROVED, algebraic geometry]** The function space ("neuromanifold") of linear conv nets is a semialgebraic set of polynomials with sparse factorizations; critical points of the squared loss are characterized. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.01538?context=cs). *Use:* the algebraic-geometry toolkit (fiber geometry of the multiplication map) applies to any fixed linear DAG.
- Ongie & Willett, "The Role of Linear Layers in Nonlinear Interpolating Networks" (arXiv 2202.00856, 2022), and Parkinson, Ongie & Willett, "Linear Neural Network Layers Promote Learning Single- and Multi-Index Models" (arXiv 2305.15598). **[PROVED representation-cost results, from memory]** Adding linear layers before a ReLU layer changes the minimum-norm representation cost toward low-rank / multi-index functions. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.00856); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.15598). *Use:* a "parameter-free in function class, but changes bias" result. Adding linear factors doesn't change expressivity but does change the inductive bias. This is the closest analogue to the project's "fixed function class, different routing" question.
- Kolb, Weber, Bischl & Rügamer, "Deep Weight Factorization: Sparse Learning Through the Lens of Artificial Symmetries" (ICLR 2025; arXiv 2502.02496). **[PROVED equivalence]** Depth-D weight factorization with L2 penalty is equivalent to a non-convex sparse regularizer (the standard result being ℓ_{2/D}; exponent not confirmed from the abstract). **[EMPIRICAL]** Deep beats shallow factorization. Needs tailored init. — [ICLR proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/f37347375d8b54e3203e5d24aeb6c58c-Abstract-Conference.html). *Use:* a concrete "same function class, different parameterization → different penalty" result. A parallel vs composed variant (sum of factorizations vs product) is a natural extension.
- Marcotte, Peyré & Gribonval, "Intrinsic training dynamics of deep neural networks" (ICLR 2026; arXiv 2508.07370). **[PROVED]** Gives conditions under which gradient flow on θ induces an autonomous ("intrinsic") flow on a lift z = φ(θ), tied to conservation laws via a kernel-inclusion necessary condition. For ReLU networks of any depth with φ = path-lifting, the flow is intrinsic for a dense set of inits. For linear nets this holds for "relaxed balanced" inits, which in some configurations are the only ones that work. Linear neural ODEs are made explicit. DAGs are not mentioned. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07370); [ICLR 2026](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10010269). *Use:* **probably the most directly usable tool.** Path-lifting is defined for any DAG, so for a given routing graph one can ask whether the path-space dynamics are intrinsic and what mirror-like geometry they carry. The DAG case is not worked out.

### Inferences
- The missing object is the implicit bias of a *sum of shared-factor tensor networks*, which is what a linear DAG with skips or parallel branches computes as a sum over paths. Yun et al. cover single products. Woodworth/diagonal nets cover sums of two independent products. Shared factors across paths (as in residual DAGs, where each W_i appears in many paths) are uncovered.
- Equivalent framing: in path space, the model is linear in path coordinates z = φ(θ). Topology defines φ. The implicit bias is whatever geometry gradient flow on θ induces on z (Marcotte 2026 + Li et al. 2022, see Q3). The project could make "parent-selection" families concrete as families of φ.

### Gaps
- No paper found on implicit bias for general linear residual DAGs, DenseNet-style dense connectivity, or "parent-selection" families. A targeted search for DenseNet theory returned nothing relevant.
- The tensor-network results (Razin) cover polynomial/arithmetic-circuit CNNs. Transfer to ReLU nets is argued empirically, not proved.

## Q3. Symmetry → conserved quantity → dynamics: results beyond those listed, including mirror/reparameterization views.

### Takeaway
Three complementary theories exist. (1) Continuous symmetries give conserved quantities that pin GF to an orbit; these are now fully characterized for standard blocks, and **skip connections do not change a block's conservation laws**. (2) Discrete "mirror" symmetries plus weight decay/noise give sparsity, low-rank and ensemble-collapse structure. (3) Commuting reparameterizations make GF equivalent to mirror flow. A topology changes the parameter→function map φ and hence the symmetry group and lifting. The 2025 conservation-law result says the topology effect is *not* visible in local per-block conservation laws. It has to act through the global map.

### Cited Findings
- Zhao, Ganev, Walters, Yu & Dehmamy, "Symmetries, Flat Minima, and the Conserved Quantities of Gradient Flow" (ICLR 2023; arXiv 2210.17216). **[PROVED]** Builds conserved quantities from continuous (including nonlinear, data-dependent) symmetries, relates them to flat directions of minima, and uses them to parameterize minima. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.17216); [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11070). *Use:* the method for deriving conserved quantities of any new topology's symmetry group.
- Marcotte, Peyré & Gribonval, "Transformative or Conservative? Conservation laws for ResNets and Transformers" (ICML 2025 oral, PMLR v267; arXiv 2506.06194). **[PROVED, gradient flow; plus an analysis of discrete SGD]** "Residual blocks have the same conservation laws as the same block without a skip connection." Shallow ReLU/linear blocks (with or without convolution) have no conservation laws beyond the known ones. Single attention layers are completely characterized. Laws depending on a subset of parameters (consecutive layers, a residual block, an attention layer) can be found by analyzing that block alone. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06194); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/marcotte25a.html). *Use:* **a boundary on novelty**. "Skips change the conserved quantities" is false at block level. A topology effect has to come from how blocks are composed (cross-block coupling and the path structure), not from new local invariants.
- Ziyin, "Symmetry Induces Structure and Constraint of Learning" (ICML 2024, PMLR v235; arXiv 2309.16932). **[PROVED]** Every mirror-reflection symmetry with surface O makes O^T θ = 0 an attracting constrained solution when weight decay or gradient noise is large. Corollaries: rescaling symmetry gives sparsity, rotation symmetry gives low rank, permutation symmetry gives homogeneous ensembling (collapse). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16932); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/ziyin24a.html). *Use:* topology determines which mirror symmetries exist. Parallel identical branches have a permutation symmetry and so a predicted collapse ("homogeneous ensembling") under weight decay or noise. Composition creates rotation symmetries between consecutive layers and so predicts low rank. This is a sharp prediction for parallel-vs-sequential at matched params.
- Ziyin, Xu, Poggio & Chuang, "Parameter Symmetry Breaking and Restoration Determines the Hierarchical Learning in AI Systems" (arXiv 2502.05300, 2025). **[POSITION]** Argues that symmetry breaking/restoration unifies hierarchies in learning dynamics, model complexity and representation formation. No new theorems. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05300).
- Zhao, Walters & Yu, "Symmetry in Neural Network Parameter Spaces" (survey; TMLR 2026 per mlanthology; arXiv 2506.13018). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13018); [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/tmlr/2026/zhao2026tmlr-symmetry). *Use:* survey for coverage. The abstract mentions open gaps but I did not see them enumerated.
- Li, Wang, Lee & Arora, "Implicit Bias of Gradient Descent on Reparametrized Models: On Equivalence to Mirror Descent" (NeurIPS 2022; arXiv 2207.04036). **[PROVED]** GF with any *commuting* parametrization equals continuous mirror descent with a related Legendre function. Conversely (via Nash embedding), any mirror descent is GF under some commuting parametrization. The framework subsumes earlier results in this setting. (Precise "commuting" definition, a Lie-bracket condition, recalled rather than verified.) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.04036); [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/dfa1106ea7065899b13f2be9da04efb4-Abstract.html). *Use:* a test for each topology. If the path-lifting of a routing graph is commuting, its implicit bias is a mirror flow with computable potential. Matrix-product (chain) parametrizations are generally not commuting, which is why chains need separate analysis. Determining which DAGs are commuting is a concrete, likely novel question.
- Azulay, Moroshko, Nacson, Woodworth, Srebro, Globerson & Soudry, "On the Implicit Bias of Initialization Shape: Beyond Infinitesimal Mirror Descent" (ICML 2021, PMLR v139; arXiv 2102.09769). **[PROVED for specific models, from memory]** The *shape* of initialization (relative scales across layers/branches, not only overall scale) changes the implicit bias. Some models are shown not to be mirror descent with any potential. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/azulay21a.html). *Use:* in a DAG, the relative init scale of branches (skip gain vs branch gain) is an "init shape." This predicts that topology and per-branch init scale interact.
- Le & Jegelka, "Training invariances and the low-rank phenomenon: beyond linear networks" (ICLR 2022; arXiv 2201.11968). **[PROVED, from memory]** Extends low-rank alignment results from linear nets to ReLU nets with linear-layer structure, using gradient-flow invariances. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.11968v2).
- Orhan & Pitkow, "Skip Connections Eliminate Singularities" (ICLR 2018; arXiv 1701.09175). **[ARGUED + EMPIRICAL, not proved]** Skips break permutation (overlap) singularities, reduce elimination singularities, and reduce linear dependence of units, at typical inits. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.09175). *Use:* a symmetry-breaking story for skips. It ties directly to Ziyin's mirror symmetries and to singular learning theory (Q4). Prediction: skips should *raise* the local learning coefficient at init (fewer degenerate directions). This has not been tested in theory.

### Inferences
- Combining Marcotte 2025 (no new block-level laws) with Ziyin 2024 (discrete symmetries → structure): the topology signature should show up in **discrete symmetries of the whole graph** (e.g., automorphisms that permute parallel branches) and in the **global lifting φ**, not in continuous per-block invariants.
- Parallel identical branches → permutation symmetry → predicted collapse / homogeneous ensembling under weight decay or SGD noise. Sequential composition → GL(n) symmetries between layers → predicted low rank. This gives a falsifiable parallel-vs-sequential prediction at equal parameter count.

### Gaps
- No paper characterizes which DAG lifting maps are "commuting" in the Li et al. sense.
- I could not access the survey's explicit open-problem list.

## Q4. Singular learning theory: how parameterization degeneracy (RLCT) depends on architecture; results comparing architectures with the same function class but different parameterizations.

### Takeaway
Exact RLCTs are known for deep linear networks (Aoyagi; Pepin Lehalleur & Rimányi 2024), and bounds exist for three-layer nets. The deep-linear formula depends on depth and widths only through sorted widths, and for constant width it saturates with depth. Comparing the formulas suggests a deep chain has a *lower* RLCT than a shallow factorization of the same function class, though the papers don't frame it that way. **I found no RLCT result for residual, parallel or DAG parameterizations.** This is a clean open gap.

### Cited Findings
- Pepin Lehalleur & Rimányi, "Geometry of fibers of the multiplication map of deep linear neural networks" (arXiv 2411.19920, 2024). **[PROVED]** For deep linear nets with true map B, rlct(K_B) = codim(mult⁻¹(B))/2, combined with Aoyagi's 2024 computation. There is an explicit codimension formula in the sorted widths d'_0 ≤ … ≤ d'_N (Thm 7.10). Constant width d with N > d: codim = d(d+1)/2, independent of N. The authors conclude the RLCT is "not very sensitive to depth" and that deep linear nets are "mildly singular." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.19920); [notes PDF](https://rimanyi.web.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/9870/2024/11/Dimension_and_RLCT_for_deep_linear_networks-1.pdf). *Use:* a closed-form baseline. Since a residual chain f = Π(I+W_i) and a plain chain have different fibers over the same function, the analogous fiber codimension for the residual map at B (≈ identity vs ≈ 0) is a directly computable novel quantity.
- Aoyagi, "Singular learning coefficients and efficiency in learning theory" (arXiv 2501.12747, 2025). Learning coefficients for deep linear models and three-layer ReLU networks, extended to softmax. Abstract-level only. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12747).
- Kurumadani, "Upper Bounds for Local Learning Coefficients of Three-Layer Neural Networks" (arXiv 2603.12785, Mar 2026). **[PROVED]** Upper-bound formula for local learning coefficients at singular points via "budget, demand, supply" counting. Applies to non-polynomial real-analytic activations (e.g., swish) and matches a known exact value for 1-D input. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12785).
- Lau, Furman, Wang, Murfet & Wei, "The Local Learning Coefficient: A Singularity-Aware Complexity Measure" (AISTATS 2025, PMLR v258; arXiv 2308.12108). Defines and estimates the LLC (SGLD-based). — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v258/lau25a.html). *Use:* the practical estimator for measuring LLC across topologies at fixed params. Furman & Lau (arXiv 2402.03698) validate it on deep linear nets against the exact RLCT. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03698v1).
- Orhan & Pitkow 2018 (see Q3): skips remove singularities (argued). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.09175).

### Inferences
- From the Pepin Lehalleur–Rimányi formula (my inference, not stated in the paper): a shallow N=1, d×d factorization has codim d², so RLCT d²/2. A constant-width deep chain with N > d has RLCT d(d+1)/4. So depth *lowers* the RLCT for the same function class, which in Watanabe's theory means higher Bayesian posterior concentration on simpler fibers.
- Prediction: residual parameterizations around identity are locally *less* singular at near-identity targets than plain chains at the same targets (the fiber of Π(I+W_i) near I is smooth for small W). Their RLCT near target B ≈ I should therefore be larger (closer to d·#params/2) than the plain chain's. At low-rank targets far from I this may reverse. This is a computable, apparently novel result.

### Gaps
- No RLCT/LLC results found for residual, multi-branch, dense or general DAG parameterizations, analytically or empirically. (One targeted search returned only Orhan & Pitkow.)
- No paper found that compares RLCT across parameterizations of exactly the same function class beyond deep-vs-shallow linear.

## Q5. Incremental / saddle-to-saddle learning: how architecture changes the order of feature acquisition; feature-learning theory in two-layer vs deeper/residual nets (mean-field, DMFT).

### Takeaway
Saddle-to-saddle dynamics now have a cross-architecture theory (Zhang, Saxe & Latham, ICLR 2026). Architecture sets *the unit of incremental complexity*: rank for linear nets, kinks for ReLU, kernels for CNNs, heads for attention. Data-induced saddles produce low-rank weights and init-induced saddles produce sparse weights. Residual architectures are not covered. Since identity-init residual nets do not start at the origin saddle, they are predicted to lose or reshape the plateau sequence. That prediction has not been tested in theory. Depth-limit DMFT for ResNets exists, but nothing comparable exists for parallel or dense DAGs.

### Cited Findings
- Zhang, Saxe & Latham, "Saddle-to-Saddle Dynamics Explains A Simplicity Bias Across Neural Network Architectures" (ICLR 2026; arXiv 2512.20607). Training evolves near invariant manifolds, approaches a saddle and switches manifolds. Linear nets learn increasing rank, ReLU nets increasing kinks, CNNs increasing kernel count, self-attention increasing heads. Data-induced saddles give low-rank weights and init-induced saddles give sparse weights. The theory predicts how data and init set plateau number and duration. **[Proof scope and assumptions not verified beyond the abstract; residual architectures not mentioned]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.20607); [ICLR 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/5e96d63b20f335f57999a1aca7fa0474-Abstract-Conference.html). *Use:* the direct framework to extend to residual, parallel and dense routing. The prediction to test is that the "unit of simplicity" for a DAG is set by its invariant manifolds, e.g. one path or one branch at a time for parallel topologies.
- Jacot, Ged, Şimşek, Hongler & Gabriel, "Saddle-to-Saddle Dynamics in Deep Linear Networks: Small Initialization Training, Symmetry, and Sparsity" (arXiv 2106.15933, 2021). **[PROVED partly / conjecture for general depth, from memory]** — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.15933).
- Pesme & Flammarion, "Saddle-to-Saddle Dynamics in Diagonal Linear Networks" (NeurIPS 2023; arXiv 2304.00488). **[PROVED]** In the vanishing-init limit, GF visits a sequence of saddles, with the jump sequence given by an explicit algorithm related to LARS / homotopy. — [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/17a9ab4190289f0e1504bbb98d1d111a-Abstract.html).
- Gissin, Shalev-Shwartz & Daniely, "The Implicit Bias of Depth: How Incremental Learning Drives Generalization" (ICLR 2020; arXiv 1909.12051). **[PROVED for toy models, from memory]** Deeper parameterizations make incremental learning sharper (more sequential), even at larger init. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.12051).
- Garrod, Keating & Thrampoulidis, "The Implicit Bias of Depth: From Neural Collapse to Softmax Codes" (arXiv 2605.23087, May 2026). Deep unconstrained-feature model ≈ deep linear net with orthogonal inputs, CE loss, unregularized GD. Depth induces a low-rank bias, argued to give "softmax codes" instead of neural collapse, and shrinks the basin of collapse. Spectral init gives early singular-value repulsion. Width has the opposite effect. **[Mix of proved and argued]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23087). *Use:* depth vs width have opposite effects on collapse. Topology (skips or parallel branches) is the natural third axis to add.
- Huh et al., "The Low-Rank Simplicity Bias in Deep Networks" (TMLR 2023; arXiv 2103.10427). **[EMPIRICAL + CONJECTURE]** Deeper nets give lower effective-rank embeddings at init and after training. The authors conjecture that the volume of low-rank functions grows with depth. Linear over-parameterization of nonlinear nets helps generalization. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10427). *Use:* an empirical baseline. Residual nets are predicted to reduce this effect (Boix-Adsera, Q1).
- Bordelon et al. 2023/24 (Q1), DMFT for residual depth limits. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16620).
- Chizat & Bach, "Implicit Bias of Gradient Descent for Wide Two-layer Neural Networks Trained with the Logistic Loss" (COLT 2020; arXiv 2002.04486). **[PROVED, mean-field infinite width, two-layer]** The limit is a max-margin classifier in the variation-norm (F1) space. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.04486). *Use:* the two-layer baseline any deeper or residual mean-field theory has to reduce to.
- Lyu & Li, "Gradient Descent Maximizes the Margin of Homogeneous Neural Networks" (ICLR 2020; arXiv 1906.05890). **[PROVED, homogeneous nets]** Directional convergence to KKT of max-margin. Extended to residual/non-homogeneous nets by Cai et al. 2025 (Q1). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.05890v4).

### Inferences
- Identity-init residual nets begin away from the origin saddle, so the saddle-to-saddle sequence that small-init chains show should be suppressed or reorganized. Prediction: residual topologies show fewer or shorter plateaus but a weaker "one rank at a time" simplicity bias. Zero-init-branch ResNets (common in practice: branch output initialized to 0) are a hybrid that should keep some plateau structure. This makes a clean three-way experiment: plain small-init, residual identity-init with small branches, residual large-init.
- In parallel topologies with shared input and summed outputs, invariant manifolds likely correspond to "one branch active." Saddle-to-saddle theory then predicts sequential branch recruitment, the same mechanism as Saxe et al. 2022's neural race. That links the project's parallel-branch question to known gated-linear results.

### Gaps
- No saddle-to-saddle analysis found for residual, parallel/multi-branch or dense topologies specifically.
- No DMFT or mean-field theory found for general DAG topologies beyond residual chains (and the NNGP-level connectivity analysis of Chen et al. 2022).

## Q6. Theory of compositional/hierarchical function learning where architecture structure matches task structure.

### Takeaway
Approximation theory (Poggio/Mhaskar) shows deep nets matching a compositional (DAG/tree) structure avoid the curse of dimensionality. Learning theory now provides *gradient-based* guarantees: Allen-Zhu & Li (backward feature correction, ResNet-style), Nichani et al. (3-layer), Abbe et al. (staircase), and Ren/Dandi/Krzakala/Lee 2026 (provable RHM learning by deep CNNs with layerwise training). Statistical-physics work (Cagnetta/Wyart RHM) and U-Net-as-belief-propagation (Mei 2024) connect architecture *with skip connections* to the generative hierarchy. The "architecture graph = task DAG" match is mostly established for approximation and for layerwise or idealized training, not for end-to-end GD across arbitrary topologies.

### Cited Findings
- Poggio, Mhaskar, Rosasco, Miranda & Liao, "Why and When Can Deep — but Not Shallow — Networks Avoid the Curse of Dimensionality: a Review" (2017; arXiv 1611.00740). **[PROVED, approximation]** For functions compositional along a binary tree (more generally a DAG) with low-dimensional constituents, deep nets that *match the graph* need far fewer units than shallow nets. — [alphaxiv](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/1611.00740). Also Mhaskar & Poggio, "Function approximation by deep networks" (arXiv 1905.12882). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.12882). *Use:* a formal basis for "routing graph should match the task's compositional DAG." It predicts that mismatched routing (e.g., parallel when the target is sequentially composed) costs units or sample complexity.
- Mossel, "Deep Learning and Hierarchal Generative Models" (arXiv 1612.09057, 2016–2018). **[PROVED: semi-supervised separation; ARGUED: deep algorithms necessary]** Defines "deep" algorithms as those using feature correlations. For a family of hierarchical generative models, low-order moments plus unlabeled data cannot classify information-theoretically, yet an efficient algorithm labels perfectly. The proof uses the fact that belief propagation is not well approximated by linear functions. — [arXiv](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/1612.09057).
- Malach & Shalev-Shwartz, "A Provably Correct Algorithm for Deep Learning that Actually Works" (arXiv 1803.09522, 2018; layerwise, for a hierarchical generative model, from memory) and "Is Deeper Better only when Shallow is Good?" (NeurIPS 2019). **[PROVED]** For fractal-like distributions, deep nets are learnable by GD only when shallow approximations are already good (from memory). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09522v2); [NeurIPS](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/8871-is-deeper-better-only-when-shallow-is-good). *Use:* a boundary result. Hierarchical *learnability* needs the target's coarse levels to carry signal. This is a staircase-like condition the project should state when claiming topology–task matching.
- Allen-Zhu & Li, "Backward Feature Correction: How Deep Learning Performs Deep (Hierarchical) Learning" (COLT 2023, PMLR v195; arXiv 2001.04413). **[PROVED, specific deep architecture with skip-like structure, SGD]** Deep nets learn hierarchical targets efficiently by training all layers together. Higher layers *correct* lower-layer features ("backward feature correction"), which layerwise training and kernels cannot do (from memory of the paper). — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v195/allen-zhu23a); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04413). *Use:* the architecture analyzed uses connections from every layer to the output (DenseNet/ResNet-like). Prediction: topologies that give each layer a direct output path enable backward correction. Strictly sequential chains should learn hierarchies less efficiently.
- Abbe, Boix-Adsera & Misiakiewicz, "The merged-staircase property: a necessary and nearly sufficient condition for SGD learning of sparse functions on two-layer neural networks" (COLT 2022, PMLR v178; arXiv 2202.08658). **[PROVED, mean-field two-layer]** — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v178/abbe22a.html). Leap complexity (Abbe et al. 2023) is known to the project. *Use:* defines the task-side hierarchy that architecture structure must match.
- Nichani, Damian & Lee, "Provable Guarantees for Nonlinear Feature Learning in Three-Layer Neural Networks" (NeurIPS 2023; arXiv 2305.06986). **[PROVED]** Three-layer nets learn hierarchical functions (e.g., nonlinear function of a quadratic feature) with better sample complexity than two-layer nets, by learning nonlinear features in the middle layer (from memory). — [NeurIPS](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/236b6a814a1d2c0ff504ca7bf380f7ff-Abstract-Conference.html).
- Cagnetta, Petrini, Tomasini, Favero & Wyart, "How Deep Neural Networks Learn Compositional Data: The Random Hierarchy Model" (arXiv 2307.02129; Phys. Rev. X 2024 per my recollection). **[DERIVED + EMPIRICAL]** Sample complexity of deep CNNs on a random context-free-grammar-like hierarchy scales polynomially, via synonym-invariant representations. Conjectured to separate deep from shallow. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.02129). Tomasini & Wyart, "How Deep Networks Learn Sparse and Hierarchical Data: The Sparse Random Hierarchy Model" (ICML 2024). — [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/icml/2024/tomasini2024icml-deep).
- Ren, Dandi, Krzakala & Lee, "Provable Learning of Random Hierarchy Models and Hierarchical Shallow-to-Deep Chaining" (arXiv 2601.19756, Jan 2026; v2 Jun 2026). **[PROVED]** Under mild conditions, a deep CNN can be efficiently trained on the RHM with gradient methods. General principle: if intermediate layers get a clean label signal and features are weakly identifiable, **layerwise** training learns the hierarchy. No residual connections. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19756). *Use:* the state of the art for "architecture tree = task tree" learnability. Extending it to end-to-end training and to mismatched topologies is open.
- Mei, "U-Nets as Belief Propagation: Efficient Classification, Denoising, and Diffusion in Generative Hierarchical Models" (ICLR 2025; arXiv 2404.18444). **[PROVED approximation/efficiency, from memory]** U-Nets (with their long skip connections) can implement belief propagation on tree-structured generative hierarchical models, which gives efficient denoising/diffusion sample complexity. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18444v2); [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/poster/28085). *Use:* a rare result where a *specific skip topology* (encoder-decoder long skips) is matched to the message-passing graph of the task. Prediction: removing U-Net skips breaks the BP correspondence.
- Wang, Ergen & Pilanci, "Parallel Deep Neural Networks Have Zero Duality Gap" (ICLR 2023; arXiv 2110.06482). **[PROVED]** Standard deep linear nets with vector outputs have a non-zero duality gap. Stacking deep nets in *parallel* with modified regularization gives zero gap and equivalent convex programs. Weight decay gives low-rank solutions in closed form. Three-layer ReLU: strong duality for rank-1 data. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06482); [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11942). Companion: Ergen & Pilanci, "Path Regularization: A Convexity and Sparsity Inducing Regularization for Parallel ReLU Networks" (arXiv 2110.09548). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.09548v4). *Use:* **directly relevant to parallel vs sequential**. Parallel topology changes the global optimization geometry (convexifiable), and weight decay on parallel branches gives a group-sparsity/path penalty that selects few branches. Prediction: parallel branches + weight decay → branch-sparsity (few active branches).
- Zhang, Shao & Salakhutdinov, "Deep Neural Networks with Multi-Branch Architectures Are Intrinsically Less Non-Convex" (AISTATS 2019, PMLR v89; arXiv 1806.01845). **[PROVED]** With a hinge-loss variant and arbitrary activations, the duality gap shrinks to zero as the number of branches grows. For linear activations with ℓ2 loss the empirical duality gap is zero. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v89/zhang19d.html). *Use:* prior art that branch count (topology) quantitatively reduces non-convexity at fixed total width.

### Inferences
- Task–architecture matching is proved for approximation (Poggio) and for idealized or layerwise learning (Malach, Ren et al., Mossel). For end-to-end GD it is shown with skip-like architectures (Allen-Zhu & Li). That suggests skip/dense connectivity is what makes end-to-end hierarchical learning efficient: each layer gets a direct label signal, which is the "clean signal to intermediate layers" condition in Ren et al.
- Testable prediction: on a staircase/RHM task, dense or residual routing (direct output paths per layer) should learn the levels in order with fewer samples than a strict chain at matched params. Parallel-only routing should fail on the higher levels.

### Gaps
- No paper found that compares end-to-end GD learning of a compositional target under *different routing graphs at fixed operators/params*. This is the project's central question and appears open.
- Venue for Cagnetta et al. (PRX) and several detail claims (Malach, Nichani, Allen-Zhu BFC architecture specifics) come from memory.

## Q7. What is explicitly open?

### Takeaway
Authors state several open problems: GF selecting a minimum-complexity neural ODE (Marion et al.); the SDE limit under i.i.d. init (Marion et al.); dependence of the true residual bias on dynamics rather than the weight-decay cost (Boix-Adsera); general-architecture implicit bias (Vardi survey; Kubjas et al.). Beyond those, the review turned up structural gaps nobody claims: general linear DAG / parent-selection implicit bias, RLCT for non-chain parameterizations, saddle-to-saddle for residual/parallel topologies, DMFT for general DAGs, and commuting-ness of DAG lifting maps.

### Cited Findings
- Marion et al. 2024: the limit for i.i.d. init is suggested to be an SDE, not proved. Showing GF selects a neural ODE that minimizes some complexity measure is "an interesting next step." Momentum/Adam is not covered. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.01213).
- Boix-Adsera 2025: the optimization dynamics and the training regime (e.g., no weight decay) may change the inductive bias, and the cost is only an approximation to the true bias. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19149v1).
- Vardi, "On the Implicit Bias in Deep-Learning Algorithms" (survey, arXiv 2208.12591, 2022; CACM 2023 per my recollection). Surveys implicit bias in linear, homogeneous and two-layer settings, with open questions on nonlinear/deep architectures (open-question list not re-verified in this session). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.12591).
- An open-problem aggregator attributes "characterize the implicit bias … on general neural network architectures" to Kubjas et al. 2024, "Geometry of Polynomial Neural Networks" (arXiv 2402.00949, §7). It states that optimization-induced biases on general architectures are "still largely unknown." — [EmergentMind open problems](https://www.emergentmind.com/open-problems/implicit-bias-optimization-general-neural-networks). (Secondary aggregator. The quote was not checked against the paper.)
- Yun et al. 2021: branched/parallel networks are outside the tensor framework, and directional-convergence assumptions remain for general tensor nets. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02501).
- Marcotte et al. 2025: conservation laws for residual blocks match non-residual ones. So whether *composition* creates global invariants or intrinsic dynamics is a separate question, which Marcotte et al. 2026 address for chains, ReLU path-lifting and linear neural ODEs but not general DAGs. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06194); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07370).
- Gonon et al. 2024: path-norm bounds on ResNets remain far from tight on ImageNet, so whether path geometry *explains* generalization is open. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01225).

### Inferences
Open directions ranked by tractability and fit to the project:
1. **Implicit bias of linear residual / parent-selection DAGs under GF from small or identity init.** Tools: path-lifting + intrinsic dynamics (Marcotte 2026), commuting test (Li et al. 2022), the tensor formalism (Yun 2021), representation-cost formulas (Boix-Adsera 2025) as the λ>0 counterpart. None of the reviewed papers solves it.
2. **Representation cost of general linear DAGs under weight decay.** Boix-Adsera's singular-value decomposition argument may extend to DAGs whose path sums are simultaneously diagonalizable (e.g., when all branches share singular vectors at the optimum). Gives closed-form predictions for parallel-vs-sequential.
3. **RLCT/LLC of residual and parallel parameterizations** of the same function class. The Pepin Lehalleur–Rimányi fiber-codimension approach applies directly. The LLC estimator (Lau et al.) gives empirical checks.
4. **Saddle-to-saddle in residual/parallel topologies.** Extend Zhang–Saxe–Latham's invariant-manifold analysis. Predicts what the "unit of simplicity" is for each DAG.
5. **Discrete-symmetry predictions (Ziyin 2024) for parallel branches** (permutation → collapse) vs composition (rotation → low rank) under weight decay or noise. Directly falsifiable at matched params.
6. **DMFT for general DAGs** (extend Bordelon et al. kernel recursions) and the backward-to-feature angle (Chizat & Netrapalli) as a per-node diagnostic.
