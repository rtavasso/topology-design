# Independent code review of commit 5db9a18 (rung-0 revision 3)

Scope: `git diff 450b883 5db9a18` for `src/`, `experiments/ptr/{run_ptr,analyze,make_grids}.py`, `tests/`, read against DESIGN.md revision 3 §§2–3, 6–7. All checks were run on CPU; scripts are in the reviewer's scratchpad (`review3code/check_{klexit,halting,memory}.py`), not in the repository. `tests/test_ptr.py`: 22 passed, also under `torch.set_default_dtype(torch.float64)`.

## Findings

1. **should-fix — LH-x beyond the training horizon uses untrained logits, not "the last logit repeats"** (`model.py:239–240`, `stacked.py:174`; DESIGN.md:73). `halt_bias[min(t-1, 63)]` repeats only beyond 64. With T = 10, index 9 is overridden by λ_T = 1 and indices ≥ 9 never receive gradient, so at T_eval = 40 loops 10–40 run at σ(−2) = 0.12. Verified on the pilot records: `lam_by_step` for every LHx run is 0.23, 0.24 at t = 8, 9 and then 0.12 from t = 10 on. Not introduced by this commit, but the revision-3 text claims otherwise and LH-x is a preregistered null. Fix: `halt_bias[min(t-1, mc.T-2)]` (repeat the last *trained* logit), or correct the design text. The pilot LHx still halted before t = 10, so the pilot numbers stand.

2. **should-fix — `ppool_time`'s ratio ignores censoring** (`analyze.py:239–242`). `med = np.median([x for x in t if x is not None])` is the median of the *uncensored* runs. Example (run): t95 = [20000, None, None] at budget 25000 gives median 20000 and ratio 20000/base, where a censoring-aware median is "> 25000". P6 (S8 at factor 10⁻¹ "≥ 3×, censoring counts") can flip on this. Fix: treat censored as +∞, take the median over all runs, print "≥ budget/base×" when the median is censored. `firsts` is fine because `fmt_t95` prints the censored count next to the median.

3. **should-fix — the preregistered paired-bootstrap P(A > B) is not implemented** (`analyze.py`, `headline`/`calibration`). §8 fixes thresholds as "run-level paired bootstrap P(A > B)" (P3: P(LH-Δ cheaper than LR) ≥ 0.9 at k = 8, 16; P5: P(LO t95(8) < S8 t95(3)) ≥ 0.9). analyze.py prints only group means ± s.e. and signed gap-of-means. Seeds are paired across arms (LR inherits LF's seed), so this is cheap: add one function that, per prediction, resamples seeds with replacement and reports P(A > B). Otherwise the verdict will be computed ad hoc after the data are seen.

4. **should-fix — the "10 fits a T4 at T = 24 with room" claim is wrong as stated; harmless for rev3** (`launch.py:75`, DESIGN.md:108, `run_ptr.py:48` default 32). Counting the tensors autograd saves (saved-tensors hooks, CPU autocast in bf16 = fp16 byte widths, scaled to R = 10, B = 64, 61 tokens): LHs24 ≈ **15.1 GB**, LHd 6.7, LHs 6.3, LF 5.3, S8 4.7 GB (fp32 would be 20.7 / 8.9 / 8.7 / 7.5 / 6.9). Per loop ≈ 16 KB per token, two thirds of it fp32 copies of h (the `torch.where` of an fp16 `h_new` with an fp32 `h` promotes to fp32, and every LayerNorm and its affine save fp32 inputs). A 10-stack at T = 24 would not fit a 15 GB T4. rev3 never forms one: the T = 24 cell has 5 runs (≈ 7.6 GB) and every T = 10 stack is ≤ 10 (≤ 6.7 GB), S8 is an 8-stack (≈ 3.8 GB); the eval pass is ≈ 2.1 GB transient plus 0.3 GB of query-only `hs` (full states would be 6.0 GB). The exception path retries in fp32 (`run_ptr.py:97–102`), which needs *more* memory; on an OOM it should split the stack in two instead. Fix the help text/design line, set run_ptr's default to 10, and prefer halving over fp32 on OOM.

5. **minor — the stacked-vs-single test barely exercises the delta features** (`tests/test_ptr.py:236`). At w ~ N(0, 0.02²) the per-query spread of λ at t = 1..3 is 0.001 (λ ≈ 0.117, 0.113, 0.109: the bias alone). Run with w = (1, 1) and w = (−2, −2), b = −1 (λ reaches 0.98 by loop 2) the stacked and single models still agree: max |Δλ| 1.4e-6, |Δlogits| 1.6e-6, |Δhalted_logits| 1.6e-6, `steps`/`halt_step` equal, in both `ponder_train` and `eval`; gradients reach `halt.weight` (norm 0.10) and the block (28). Suggest setting the head weights to (1, 1) in the delta parametrization of the test.

6. **minor — trace-based residual triggers differ from `kl_exit` by the 4-decimal rounding of traces** (`train_stacked.py:138`, `analyze.py:294`). On random settling readouts `kl_exit` agrees with the trace rule exactly (steps and correctness 100 % at 10⁻¹…10⁻⁴); against traces rounded to 4 decimals agreement is 100 % at 10⁻² and 10⁻¹, 98.7 % at 10⁻³, 85 % at 10⁻⁴. The primary threshold is unaffected; the trace section is labeled "for continuity". Either round `kl` to 6 decimals or note it.

7. **minor — `calibration` prints LF's `max_steps` (40) for the LR rows and averages the excess over all k for every arm** (`analyze.py:80–81, 142–143`). The LR twin copies LF's `max_steps`/`ce`; P4 defines the excess up to k ≤ 24 for LH-s24. Print the per-k excess (or the max) and drop `max_steps` from the LR row, or compute it from `kl_exit`.

8. **minor — `halted_logits` in `ponder_train` is "the halted readout on the all-T trajectory"** (`model.py:275–276`). Query tokens attend to earlier query tokens, which at test freeze at their own halting step but in training run all T loops, so `acc_halt_by_k` is the policy's prediction up to that pre-existing train/test difference. In `eval` it equals `logits` exactly (verified; `halt_step` equals `steps` at the query positions). The curve label should say so.

9. **minor — fp16 noise floor of the change features in training vs fp32 at eval.** Under autocast the readout matmul is fp16, so the settled-regime step-KL has a quantization floor (≲ 1e-5 for a peaked readout) that is absent at eval (features clamped at log 1e-6 = −13.8). The effect is confined to settled states and makes eval halting no later than training for a head with w < 0 (halt on small change). No action; worth one line in the report if LH-Δ halts earlier at test than its training `halt_step` suggests.

10. **minor — steps-to-criterion is noisy at factor 10⁻²** (`analyze.py:168–179`). k = 3 has ≈ 54 queries per 100-step window at 10⁻²; "≥ 0.95 confirmed by the next window" is passed by a 0.97-accurate model with probability ≈ 0.6 per window pair, which delays t95 by a few windows in the rare condition only. The P6 effects asked for are ≥ 2×, so this will not manufacture a pass; pooling windows to ≥ 200 examples would remove it.

Verified correct (no finding): delta features and off-by-one (h₁ vs e₀, r₁ vs readout(e₀); t = 1 compares the right pair in both models); frozen context tokens give exactly (−13.8, −13.8), no NaN, their λ never enters the loss (sliced to `qpos`) and gradients are finite in the planted and random models; `halted_readout`'s cumsum/argmax agrees with the in-loop `stack().sum()` rule in 58,560/58,560 token decisions across random heads and both modes; `halt_step` is `no_grad`; `acc_by_k` is the T-loop readout in `ponder_train`; `kl_exit` edge cases (settles at loop 2 → 2 loops; never → T; fp16 input → same steps as fp32) and `t_halt + 1` arithmetic; eval runs outside autocast in both trainers; `kl_exit` on the fixed arm only is right (LO/LH freeze states at their own halting, so their per-step readouts are constant afterwards; a KL exit on them would measure min(own halt, LR)); stacked `_traces` with query-slot indexing equals single `residual_traces` on identical weights and inputs (max diff 1e-4 = the trace rounding; probe states equal to 9e-7); `n_by_k`/`acc_halt_by_k` keyed by int in memory and str after JSON, `t95_of` handles both; the pilot records (old format) and the smoke output both analyze end to end; LR/LF@T rows derive correctly; grouping stacks LO f0.01+f0.1 (10), LO f1 (10), LHs likewise, S8 f1+f0.1 (8), LHs24 (5), balanced to 6.50 / 6.39 h; `assign_shards` is deterministic given the same `todo`, which both Kaggle shards compute at the same instant; a failed stack prints a traceback and leaves no file, so a later session picks it up; pilot.json and pilot2.json are byte-identical after regeneration; rev3.json has 83 runs with k_deep = 3 everywhere, β = 0.003, λ_p = 0.1 on every learned arm, S8 at lr 1e-4 / 25k steps, LHs24 at T = 24, steps per arm as §6.
