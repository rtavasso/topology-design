**1. Principled?** Yes. The rule (d2−d1 ≥ 0.1, d3−d2 ≥ 0.1, lexicographic rungs, eval5, training worlds, k ≥ 4) is unchanged and applied to the design's own finest preregistered stratification (§1, §5 already name (world, k, binding, GP1) as the ladder's table; n ≥ 10 is derive.py's standing threshold, used for the §6 table too, though §6 never stated it — say so). Q3's quantity (largest symbolic rung the no-search router matches, on the same instances) is preserved. What is lost: the failure is no longer pinned to one identified decision, and it is no longer a *strict* garden path. §16's claim that "the greedy failure is a later decision in 0.9 of the instances" is **wrong** — 0.9 is just 1 − GP1 share. Recomputed by running the d=1 policy on the 170: greedy fails on 121 (0.71); 43 of those (0.36 of failures, 0.25 of all instances) fail at the *first* decision — through σ-ties broken lexicographically against the correct move (tie1 holds on 54/170), not strict GP1 — and 78 (0.46 of all) fail later. A strict GP exists somewhere on the canonical proof (`secondary.canon_GP1_any`) in only 71/170. Also note the cells are 0.90 binding, so P4 now mostly reads in-world detour lookahead (P2's phenomenon, in-distribution). Fix the sentence.

**3. Statistics.** Selection bias: router success is not used, so its rate is unbiased; the rungs carry a winner's-curse inflation of the gaps (cell SE 0.07–0.12 at n 17–45; five cells sit at gap12 0.087–0.097, so the 6-cell draw is fragile). But the pass rule uses only d2/d3, and that edge is robust: random tie-break gives pooled 0.38/0.47/0.72 vs lex 0.29/0.45/0.72; other partitions ((world,k): 0.33/0.45/0.77; (world,binding,k): 0.31/0.45/0.74) land in the same place. What is *not* robust is the lower separation: under the random tie-break 5 of the 6 cells fail d2−d1 ≥ 0.1, so the cells were chosen partly by a lexicographic artefact and the "≤ d1" falsifier is weak there. Power: strictly between = 77–122 successes of 170; with binomial noise only, ≥7/10 seeds is reached with prob ≥ 0.85 for true p ∈ [0.48, 0.70] and "≥ d3 in ≥7/10" for p ≥ 0.76; within ±0.03 of an edge neither count reaches 7/10 (at p = 0.72: 0.17/0.17/0.66 undecided) — name that outcome. Caveat: the 170 instances are fixed across seeds, so 7/10 tests training variance only; instance-sampling SE ≈ 0.035 is uncovered — print a bootstrap-over-instances CI (analyze.py has `bootstrap_ci`). Forking paths: the partition and threshold were fixed before any router was trained and are the design's own; the only unexplored alternative worth printing (unscored) is the GP-anywhere stratum already in the data: same rule on (world, binding, canon_GP1_any, k) restricted to GP-anywhere cells gives 4/21 cells, n 138, rungs 0.06/0.17/0.49 — the amendment that would have kept "garden path" semantics.
