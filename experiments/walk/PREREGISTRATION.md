# Diagnosing the walk — preregistration (22 Oct 2026)

**Status:** written and committed before any main run. Revised after an independent pre-run review, also before any main run; see *Pre-run revision* at the end. Analysis code: [`analyze.py`](analyze.py), also committed before any data. Grids: [`grids/`](grids/), generated by [`make_grids.py`](make_grids.py). How to run: [`RUNNING.md`](RUNNING.md).

**Why these experiments.** [docs/LEDGER.md](../../docs/LEDGER.md) lists the open questions behind the researcher's goal: a learner that "sees the minimum on the horizon" instead of walking the loss landscape. These four experiments are the first, diagnostic rung:
- **E1** (ledger I10): how far is gradient descent from a learner that reasons over structure? This is the scoreboard.
- **E2** (H5): is the answer visible inside a training run before the network produces it, and can a learner act on that?
- **E3** (H1): under one shared error, does credit for learning go to whichever component is fastest, moving memorization and generalization together?
- **E4** (H8): can a parameterization make different wirings comparable, by lining up where each trains stably? This is the prerequisite for any search over structures.

**Common setting.** Modular addition, p = 59, train fraction 0.4 (1,392 training pairs), AdamW (β = 0.9, 0.98), weight decay 1 unless varied. Six-block graphs:
- asym = `pk012000` (L = blocks 1–3 as a chain, S = blocks 4–6 reading the embedding);
- sym = `pk000000`.

E4 uses the four-block parent-selection graphs. Trainer: the fleet trainer, validated on a T4 against the single-run trainer.

**Prior art checked first** (lesson L2):
- [Two Speeds of Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27078) refits a linear readout on frozen features during grokking and reports that representations improve before the test-accuracy jump.
- [Canalization Before Generalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25813) detects the coming generalization about 4,000 epochs early, from responses to weight-decay perturbations. It reports that static progress measures lag.
- [The Complexity Dynamics of Grokking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09810) tracks weight compressibility, with no ideal-learner reference.
- [Omnigrok](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01117) removes grokking by constraining weight norm.
- Depth-μP (Bordelon et al., [arXiv 2309.16620](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16620); Yang et al., Tensor Programs VI) shows hyperparameter transfer across *depth* with 1/√L branch scaling.

**What we add:**
- E1: a prequential comparison against an explicit program-prior learner.
- E2: the timing of a lead, read from cheap static operators, and an *intervention* that acts on it.
- E3: a quantitative test of credit by speed, by direct manipulation.
- E4: whether depth scaling lines up stability across *wirings* at fixed depth.

**Pilot information** (small local runs, used only to check the instruments; not evidence):
- **Refit operator.** The least-squares refit was too weak a classifier: 0.88–0.91 test accuracy at points where the network had 0.99–1.0. It was replaced by a logistic refit before any main run. The logistic refit matches the network at every checkpoint, but on that pilot (p = 23, 2 seeds) it showed no lead. The ridge refit is still recorded, as a secondary measure.
- **Drift jumps.** At p = 23, they saved 100–400 steps in sym when made mid-plateau, and nothing in asym (3 seeds).
- **Extrapolation operators,** checked against the actual future on held-out seed 699 at p = 59:
  - The straight-line operator predicted future test accuracy in sym: at 0.54 now, it predicted 0.83 at +250 steps (actual 0.93) and 0.98 at +1,000 (actual 1.00). But it overshoots in parameter space early on and in asym, with R² of displacement down to −11 at H = 1,000.
  - A decay-aware operator (exact decay plus a frozen gradient-driven push) relaxed toward the recent past and predicted nothing.
  - Decision: record both; the straight-line operator, with a guard that stops coordinates at zero, is primary. Jump horizons are capped at 1,000 steps.

## E1 — information gap (a measurement, not a hypothesis test)

The pre-run review showed that the original primary prediction could not fail. Every run at the shortest budget would be censored, simply because grokking takes thousands of steps. E1 is therefore a **measurement**: the scoreboard for every later learner. It has calibration checks and no confirmatory hypothesis.

**Streams**
- Training pairs arrive in a seed-fixed order: 140 at step 0 (about 10% of the training set), then 32 more every *b* steps, for 40 increments.
- b ∈ {400, 1600}. There is no training after the last arrival, because only predictions at arrival are scored.
- sym and asym, lr ∈ {1e-3, 2e-3}, seeds 600–604. That's 40 runs.

**Data floor.** Fixed datasets with training fraction ∈ {0.15, 0.2, 0.25, 0.3, 0.4}, up to 60,000 steps, stopping 3,000 steps after grokking.
- sym at 2e-3, asym at 1e-3; seeds 600–602. That's 30 runs.
- A cell "groks" if ≥ 2 of 3 seeds reach 0.95 test accuracy and end at ≥ 0.9.
- Prior art: Power et al. (2022) report that generalization time rises steeply as the training fraction falls. Our measurement is for these networks and both wirings.

**Measures**
- Prequential code length (each pair's cross-entropy at its arrival step, before any training on it), against the program prior and uniform.
- The production point, against *b*.
- The data floor, in examples, against k*.

**References**, computed in the analysis on the identical streams:
- **Program prior:** a Bayes mixture over every function of (a, b) mod p expressible with leaves {a, b, 1, 2}, operations {+, −, ×} and at most 7 nodes (1,015 distinct functions).
  - Prior: 2^−size per expression, summed over equivalent expressions.
  - Half the prior mass goes to a lookup table that predicts uniformly on unseen inputs.
  - Label-noise ε = 10⁻³.
  - k* is the number of examples, presented one at a time, until the posterior on the true rule reaches 0.99. It is 3 on these streams (the review checked).
- **Uniform:** n log p.

**Calibration check:** k* ≤ 16.

**Exploratory comparisons:**
- **Walk versus data:** the production point at b = 400 versus b = 1600, and both against the data floor. A drop with *b* means more walking per example helps. A floor far above k* means gradient descent is sample-inefficient, not only slow.
- sym versus asym.

**Caveats**
- Code length also charges for overconfidence. The production point is the calibration-free companion.
- The program prior is one prior (no free lunch).

## E2 — visibility and actionability

**Design**
- **Base runs:** sym at lr 2e-3 (15,000 steps) and asym at lr 1e-3 (40,000 steps), seeds 610–619, fixed horizons, no early stop. That's 20 runs.
- **Jump runs:** at step *s*, θ ← θ + H · v (guarded straight line), then training continues. Paired with the base run of the same seed.
  - sym: s ∈ {1000, 2000}, H ∈ {250, 1000}.
  - asym: s ∈ {4000, 8000}, H ∈ {250, 1000}.
  - 10 seeds each, 80 runs in total.
- **Noise-floor runs:** an H = 0 jump (a no-op) in the jump batches: sym at 1,000, asym at 4,000, 10 seeds each. That's 20 runs. Base and jump runs sit in different GPU batches, so they are not bit-identical; these runs measure how far apart they drift by chance.

**Measures**, using train labels only (test labels only score):
- **Logistic refit** at every base-run eval: the network's readout re-solved on frozen final-stream features, with the L2 strength chosen on a fixed 20% holdout of the training set. The ridge refit is secondary.
- **Extrapolated test accuracy** for H ∈ {250, 1000, 4000}, under both operators (straight line primary, decay-aware secondary).
- **Sustained grokking:** the first step from which test accuracy stays ≥ 0.95 for 2,000 steps. This replaces "first crossing", following lesson L5.

**Predictions**
- **P1 (readout visibility):** the refit reaches test accuracy 0.9 earlier than the network, with median relative lead (t_model − t_refit)/t_model ≥ 0.2 and a sign test p < 0.05. It is untestable if fewer than 5 runs reach 0.9.
  - H5 predicts support. Our pilot expectation is **not supported**: the readout tracked the network.
  - Reading if not supported: the readout is not where the walk spends its time; the features are.
- **P1b (drift visibility):** the straight-line jump-ahead by H = 1,000 reaches test accuracy 0.5 earlier than the network, with median relative lead ≥ 0.1 and a sign test p < 0.05. Read the lead in units of H: a lead near H mainly says the trajectory is straight over that span.
- **P2 (actionability):** supported if at least one of the 8 jump cells meets all of these:
  - a Holm-adjusted sign test on sustained-grokking steps saved, p < 0.05, in the positive direction;
  - median saving above the median |Δ| of the noise-floor runs;
  - median change in collapse count ≤ 0.
  - Only jumps made before the paired base run's sustained grokking count, and only pairs where at least one of the two runs reaches sustained grokking; otherwise both are censored at the horizon and the pair carries no information. A cell needs ≥ 5 such runs to be testable.
  - *Falsifier:* no cell qualifies. The answer may be visible, but a learner cannot skip ahead to it this way.
- *Exploratory:* the decay-aware operator; longer horizons; sym vs asym.

## E3 — credit by speed

**Design**
- **Main arms:** branch 0 (blocks 1–3) steps ρ× faster than branch 1 (blocks 4–6), with lr·wd held fixed per branch.
  - lr multipliers are (√ρ, 1/√ρ); weight-decay multipliers are (1/√ρ, √ρ).
  - ρ ∈ {¼, ½, 1, 2, 4}.
- **Controls** at ρ ∈ {¼, 4}:
  - **lr-only:** lr multipliers (√ρ, 1/√ρ), weight decay unchanged;
  - **wd-only:** weight-decay multipliers (1/√ρ, √ρ), lr unchanged.
  - The main manipulation changes speed *and*, through 1/wd, each branch's equilibrium weight scale (the review's point; ledger F10). The controls separate the two.
- sym: base lr 2e-3, 30,000 steps. asym: base lr 1e-3, 50,000 steps, which is longer because asym groks slowly at 1e-3.
- Seeds 620–629. That's 180 runs.

**Eligibility** (fixed now). A run counts only if all of these hold:
- it groks by its last 5,000 steps;
- train accuracy stays ≥ 0.9 over the last 2,000 steps;
- it never diverges;
- neither the gen nor the mem sum cancels between branches: |x₀ + x₁| ≥ ½(|x₀| + |x₁|).

A (graph, arm, ρ) cell needs ≥ 6 eligible runs.

**Measures**
- s(mem): branch 0's share of memorization at the run's peak of total memorization.
- s(gen): branch 0's share of generalization over the last 2,000 steps.
- Both are analysed in logit space (shares clipped to [0.02, 0.98]), so the extremes don't saturate.

**Predictions** (H1), per graph:
- **P1:** both logit shares rise with ρ in the main arms. Kendall τ > 0 with permutation p < 0.05 (ρ permuted within seed), using testable cells only. It needs ≥ 3 testable ρ cells.
  - Speed and scale both favour branch 0 here, so P1 alone is weak. It is kept as the precondition for P2 and P3.
- **P2 (co-location persists):** d = logit s(gen) − logit s(mem).
  - *Supported* if d shows no trend with ρ (p ≥ 0.05) **and** every cell's median d lies within 0.5 logit units of ρ = 1's.
  - *Falsified* (speed induces a role split) if the trend is significant **and** some cell shifts by ≥ 0.5.
  - Otherwise *inconclusive*.
- **P3 (step size alone moves credit):** in the lr-only arm, logit shares at ρ = 4 exceed those at ρ = ¼ in paired seeds. Sign test p < 0.05 for both gen and mem; it needs ≥ 6 pairs.
  - *Falsifier:* the lr-only arm does not move credit, while wd-only does. Weight scale, not speed, then decides credit.
- *Diagnostic:* effect sizes (median logit share at ρ 4 minus ρ ¼) for main, lr-only and wd-only.
- *Exploratory:* the logit slope κ.

## E4 — stability regions

**Design**
- Graphs: `pk0000`, `pk0100`, `pk0120`, `pk0123` (longest chain C = 1, 2, 3, 4).
- Grid: lr ∈ {5e-4, 1e-3, …, 6.4e-2} (8 values, doubling) × wd ∈ {0.3, 1, 3}, seeds 630–632.
- Fixed 12,000-step horizon, no early stop. That's 864 runs.
- Three parameterizations, via each block's output multiplier:
  - **std:** 1;
  - **P1:** 1/√L = 0.5. This is a uniform control: all four graphs have L = 4, so it can only shift every graph together.
  - **P2:** 1/√(chain depth of the block). For `pk0000` this is identical to std.

**Stability (Study 2's definition).** A run is stable if it fits (train accuracy 0.99) and then keeps train accuracy ≥ 0.9 for 6,000 steps, without diverging. Grokking within 12,000 steps is reported separately, not required. A cell is stable if at least 2 of 3 seeds are. lr*(graph, wd) is the largest stable lr. The grid now reaches 6.4e-2, above Study 2's lr*(pk0000) of 2.4e-2.

**Predictions** (wd = 1)
- **P0 (replication of Study 2):** under std, lr*(C1) is at least 2 grid steps above lr*(C4). A C4 with no stable cell counts as below the grid.
  - It is untestable if C1 is never stable, or both sit at the top of the grid.
- **H8:** under P1 or P2, |lr* gap| between C1 and C4 is ≤ 1 grid step, *given P0*.
  - It is untestable under that parameterization if C1 is censored at the top of the grid with a small gap.
  - *Falsifier:* C4 stays ≥ 2 steps below C1 under both. Depth scaling then does not make these wirings comparable; structure comparisons and structure search need per-structure settings.
- *Exploratory:*
  - a **silencing check:** test accuracy with pk0123's deepest block removed, in stable runs, by parameterization. If P2 "aligns" stability only by making deep blocks irrelevant, the alignment is hollow.
  - grokking maps; the C1–C4 overlap of stable cells.

## What each outcome changes

- **E1** (measurement):
  - the size of the gap between gradient descent and a program prior;
  - whether more steps per example close it (walk) or the data floor sits far above k* (sample inefficiency).
- **E2:**
  - *P1b and P2 supported:* the destination is visible early and a learner can skip ahead, which motivates learners that jump.
  - *Neither:* the walk is doing irreducible work in the features.
- **E3:**
  - *supported:* credit by speed holds, and any population or two-system learner must keep hypotheses competing, not summed (ledger H9).
  - *falsified:* the shared-error critique weakens.
- **E4:**
  - *H8 supported:* structure search can run under one parameterization;
  - *not:* every structure needs its own settings, and the search must co-evolve them.

All outcomes, including failed predictions, go into the ledger.

## Pre-run revision (22 Oct 2026, after an independent review; before any main run)

The review found six blocking problems. All were fixed as follows:

1. **Drift operator.** The review predicted that the straight line would blow up decaying weights, and proposed a decay-aware operator.
   - Checked against the actual future on a held-out seed: the decay-aware operator predicted nothing. The straight line predicted future accuracy in sym, but overshoots in parameter space early and in asym. The review's simulation used a weight with no gradient, which real training doesn't match.
   - Resolution: both recorded; the straight line (guarded) is primary; jump horizons capped at 1,000 steps.
2. **E1's primary prediction could not fail** (censoring at the short budget). E1 became a measurement with a data-floor sweep.
3. **E4's gaps were biased:**
   - the grid ceiling sat below Study 2's lr*;
   - the stability definition differed from Study 2, counting slow as unstable and watching fast runs for less time;
   - the gap test had a sign bug.
   - Fixed: grid extended to 6.4e-2; Study 2's 6,000-step window; fixed horizon; |gap|; P0 reported on its own.
4. **E3 asym would mostly measure instability and runs that had not grokked.** Added eligibility rules, a minimum number of runs per cell and a longer asym horizon.
5. **E3's P1 could hardly fail, and P2 was partly automatic,** because speed and weight scale were confounded and shares saturate. Added lr-only and wd-only controls (P3), measures in logit space, and a trend test for P2.
6. **E2's P2 repeated lesson L5.** It now uses sustained grokking, a collapse condition, per-cell testing with Holm correction, and an H = 0 noise floor.

Also fixed:
- "untestable" outcomes are reported explicitly instead of "NOT supported";
- the refit is skipped in intervention-only runs;
- `horizon_extrap` is registered as a tuple field.
