# Review 3: rung-3 stage-2 results (RESULTS.md, 2 Nov 2026)

Independent recomputation from the 120 records in `results/kaggle_route-cm_stage2/results/` and the gate report (`results/kaggle_route-cm_stage1/gate.json`), with my own script (no import of `analyze.py`). Every number below is from that recomputation; where it differs from RESULTS the RESULTS value is quoted beside it.

## Verdict

**Not ready for the ledger.** The per-cell counts and the scorecard's count-based verdicts reproduce exactly (my outcome table matches `stage2_report.md` cell for cell under the author's taxonomy), and the shift-B numbers, the stability controls, the from-scratch references and the PF margin table are right. But four things in RESULTS are contradicted by the curves in the records and feed directly into the mechanism sections (§4.3, §4.1) and the synthesis (§1, §10), and one preregistered statistic (PA3's AUROC) was replaced by a qualitative verdict without being computed:

1. EX's halting is a **transit, not an endpoint**: 10 of 11 EX-from-E runs lose the SET at the flag within 0–500 steps of the end of warm-up (in 2 of them during the warm-up), and 4 of the 5 "moved / R-like" runs moved *out of the halting state* (EX_EA30 s1 after 1,900 steps halted; s5 after 300; s6 to R after 1,600; EX_EA15 s2 moved at 1,700, re-collapsed to halting at 2,000–2,400, moved again at 2,500). §4.3's "race between the oracle gradient and the step cost" and §1.4's framing read the end-state histogram as if halting and moving were alternative outcomes; the records say halting comes first in nearly every run and movement is an escape from it.
2. "IW16 never moved" (§1.1) is false: IW16_EA30 s5 reached shortest-correct 0.94–0.97 on P-words with p(PREV) = 1.00 at the relabeled class from step 100 to step 1,000, then collapsed to the all-H route at 1,100. The author's own per-run table prints `max shortest-correct 0.97` and `move 200` for it. The endpoint count (0/5) stands; the mechanism text ("a 16-sample estimator provides [the gradient] only when a sample lands there, which under a committed policy is never", §10) does not.
3. §4.1's "coverage is binary; search is plastic as long as its support touches the alternative at all" is derived from the E-start runs and stated as general, but the L-start EI16 runs show the opposite regime: EI16_LA30 s1's own beam keeps the oracle route on 0.72–0.81 of criterion P-words at every one of the 30 curve points and the router does not move in 3,000 steps (p(SET) at the flag 0.08–0.20, loss 0.49–0.52 flat); s0 and s2 have the same 0.80–0.84 coverage from step 0 and move gradually over 600–700 steps starting at 600 and 1,200. The "zero-coverage" group (1/4) is one router (E s2) under four conditions, and the gate's 0.47 for E s5 was 0.00 on the run's own criterion set from the end of warm-up through step 400 (the §4.1 table prints "–" there).
4. PA3 preregistered an AUROC over ≥ 20 runs (≥ 0.75 holds, ≤ 0.6 falsifies), read within EI16 and pooled without PF/RT (DESIGN §7a). RESULTS §9 says "Not in that form" and gives no number. Computed: within EI16 (17 runs, gate coverage on unsolved words, strict `moved`) **0.705**; pooled over EI16/EX/IW16/PF-IS16 (61 runs; beam coverage / own posterior / 16-sample coverage) **0.821**. By the preregistered rule the pooled form holds and the within-EI16 form is between the bar and the falsifier. The author's caveat (the pooled number is mostly "EI16 has non-zero coverage and the others do not") is fair and should follow the number, not replace it.
